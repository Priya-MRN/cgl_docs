# Syllogism — Solved Question Bank

> Quick links: [Reasoning Index](README.md) · [Reasoning Cheat Sheet](../Cheat-Sheets/Reasoning_Cheat_Sheet.md)

## Key Concepts
- Treat statements as Venn diagrams; a conclusion is valid only if it holds in **every** possible diagram.
- **All A are B**: A lies completely inside B.
- **Some A are B**: A and B overlap (at least one common element).
- **No A are B**: A and B are completely separate circles.
- **Either-or** applies when two conclusions both fail individually but together cover all cases (complementary pair, same subject-predicate).
- Never assume real-world knowledge — use only the given statements.

## EASY

**Q1.** Statements: All cats are animals. All animals are living things. Conclusion: All cats are living things.
(a) Follows  (b) Does not follow  (c) Cannot say  (d) Either-or
**Answer: (a) Follows**
*Solution:* Cats ⊆ animals ⊆ living things, so cats are inside living things. The conclusion follows.

**Q2.** Statements: All pens are blue. Some blue things are bottles. Conclusion: Some pens are bottles.
(a) Follows  (b) Does not follow  (c) Definitely true  (d) Either-or
**Answer: (b) Does not follow**
*Solution:* Pens are inside "blue," but the bottles overlapping "blue" need not include any pens. No guaranteed link, so it does not follow.

**Q3.** Statements: No dog is a cat. All cats are pets. Conclusion: Some pets are not dogs.
(a) Follows  (b) Does not follow  (c) Either-or  (d) Cannot say
**Answer: (a) Follows**
*Solution:* All cats are pets, and no cat is a dog. So those pets that are cats are not dogs — hence some pets are not dogs.

**Q4.** Statements: All roses are flowers. Conclusion: Some flowers are roses.
(a) Follows  (b) Does not follow  (c) Either-or  (d) Cannot say
**Answer: (a) Follows**
*Solution:* If all roses are flowers, then the roses themselves are flowers that are roses — so some flowers are roses (valid conversion of "All A are B" to "Some B are A").

**Q5.** Statements: No book is a pen. Conclusion: No pen is a book.
(a) Follows  (b) Does not follow  (c) Either-or  (d) Cannot say
**Answer: (a) Follows**
*Solution:* "No A is B" is symmetric, so "No pen is a book" must also be true.

## MEDIUM

**Q1.** Statements: All apples are fruits. Some fruits are sweet. Conclusions: I. Some apples are sweet. II. Some fruits are apples.
(a) Only I follows  (b) Only II follows  (c) Both follow  (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (b) Only II follows**
*Solution:* "All apples are fruits" gives "some fruits are apples" (II valid). But the sweet fruits need not be apples, so I is not guaranteed.

**Q2.** Statements: All teachers are educated. No educated person is illiterate. Conclusion: No teacher is illiterate.
(a) Follows  (b) Does not follow  (c) Either-or  (d) Cannot say
**Answer: (a) Follows**
*Solution:* Teachers ⊆ educated, and educated ∩ illiterate = empty. So teachers cannot be illiterate. Follows.

**Q3.** Statements: Some students are boys. All boys are players. Conclusion: Some students are players.
(a) Follows  (b) Does not follow  (c) Either-or  (d) Cannot say
**Answer: (a) Follows**
*Solution:* The students who are boys are all players, so at least those students are players — "some students are players" follows.

**Q4.** Statements: Some pens are red. No red thing is costly. Conclusions: I. Some pens are not costly. II. All pens are costly.
(a) Only I follows  (b) Only II follows  (c) Both follow  (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (a) Only I follows**
*Solution:* The pens that are red cannot be costly, so "some pens are not costly" follows. II contradicts this, so II fails.

**Q5.** Statements: All metals are solids. Some solids are heavy. Conclusion: Some metals are heavy.
(a) Follows  (b) Does not follow  (c) Either-or  (d) Cannot say
**Answer: (b) Does not follow**
*Solution:* Metals are inside solids, but the "heavy solids" may lie outside the metals region. No guaranteed overlap, so it does not follow.

**Q6.** Statements: No fish is a bird. Some birds are pets. Conclusion: Some pets are not fish.
(a) Follows  (b) Does not follow  (c) Either-or  (d) Cannot say
**Answer: (a) Follows**
*Solution:* Some pets are birds, and no bird is a fish; those pets (which are birds) are therefore not fish. So "some pets are not fish" follows.

## HARD

**Q1.** Statements: All A are B. Some B are C. No C is D. Conclusion: Some B are not D.
(a) Follows  (b) Does not follow  (c) Either-or  (d) Cannot say
**Answer: (a) Follows**
*Solution:* Some B are C, and no C is D, so those B that are C are not D. Hence "some B are not D" follows.

**Q2.** Statements: Some books are pens. All pens are pencils. Conclusions: I. Some books are pencils. II. Some pencils are books.
(a) Only I follows  (b) Only II follows  (c) Both follow  (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (c) Both follow**
*Solution:* Books overlapping pens are all pencils, so some books are pencils (I), and equivalently some pencils are books (II). Both follow.

**Q3.** Statements: All cups are plates. No plate is a glass. Conclusions: I. No cup is a glass. II. Some plates are cups.
(a) Only I  (b) Only II  (c) Both follow  (d) Neither
**Answer: (c) Both follow**
*Solution:* Cups ⊆ plates and plates ∩ glass = empty, so no cup is a glass (I). "All cups are plates" gives "some plates are cups" (II). Both follow.

**Q4.** Statements: Some chairs are tables. Conclusions: I. Some chairs are not tables. II. All chairs are tables.
(a) Only I  (b) Only II  (c) Either I or II follows  (d) Neither
**Answer: (c) Either I or II follows**
*Solution:* From "some chairs are tables" we cannot fix whether the remaining chairs are tables or not. The two conclusions form a complementary pair covering both possibilities, so the **either-or** case applies.

## SSC PYQ PATTERN (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q1.** Statements: All keys are locks. All locks are doors. Conclusions: I. All keys are doors. II. Some doors are keys.
(a) Only I  (b) Only II  (c) Both follow  (d) Neither
**Answer: (c) Both follow**
*Solution:* Keys ⊆ locks ⊆ doors, so all keys are doors (I). Therefore some doors are keys (II). Both follow.

**Q2.** Statements: No student is lazy. Ram is a student. Conclusion: Ram is not lazy.
(a) Follows  (b) Does not follow  (c) Either-or  (d) Cannot say
**Answer: (a) Follows**
*Solution:* No student is lazy and Ram is a student, so Ram cannot be lazy. Follows.

**Q3.** Statements: Some mangoes are yellow. Some yellow things are sweet. Conclusion: Some mangoes are sweet.
(a) Follows  (b) Does not follow  (c) Either-or  (d) Cannot say
**Answer: (b) Does not follow**
*Solution:* Two "some" statements give no certain link; the sweet yellow things need not be mangoes. Does not follow.

**Q4.** Statements: All flowers are red. Conclusions: I. Some flowers are red. II. No flower is red.
(a) Only I  (b) Only II  (c) Either-or  (d) Both
**Answer: (a) Only I**
*Solution:* "All flowers are red" directly implies "some flowers are red" (I). II contradicts the statement, so only I follows.

### 🔑 Trick Box
- A conclusion must hold in **all** valid Venn diagrams — one counter-diagram kills it.
- "All A are B" always gives "Some B are A"; "No A is B" always gives "No B is A."
- Two particular (some) or two negative statements rarely yield a definite conclusion.
- Use the **either-or** rule only for a complementary pair (same terms, one positive one negative) when neither alone follows.

> Draw the most "spread-out" diagram that still satisfies the statements — it exposes false conclusions fastest.
