# Chapter 3 — Vocabulary Clusters

# Chapter 4 — Negative Emotion Clusters

> [!CAUTION]
> 🟧 These clusters appear constantly in synonym/antonym + cloze test questions. Learn the **memory story** for each.

### 😠 ANGRY
`Annoyed → Irritated → Angry → Furious → Enraged → Irate → Livid → Incensed → Wrathful → Apoplectic`
🟦 Feeling strong displeasure.
🟩 **Anger thermometer** rising from "annoyed" to "exploding".
🔴 High ↔ Calm, Pleased, Placid.
*Usage:* He was **livid** when his bike was scratched.

### 😢 SAD
`Unhappy → Gloomy → Sad → Sorrowful → Melancholy → Despondent → Forlorn → Disconsolate → Woeful`
🟦 Feeling unhappy.
🟩 Rain cloud over the head, getting darker.
🔴 High ↔ Happy, Cheerful, Joyful.
*Usage:* She felt **forlorn** after her friend left town.

### 😨 FEARFUL
`Nervous → Afraid → Scared → Fearful → Terrified → Petrified → Panic-stricken → Aghast`
🟦 Feeling fear.
🟩 From a small jump to frozen-stiff.
🔴 High ↔ Brave, Fearless, Bold.
*Usage:* The child was **petrified** of the dark.

### 😒 JEALOUS
`Envious → Jealous → Covetous · Resentful · Green-eyed · Begrudging`
🟦 Wanting what others have.
🟩 The "**green-eyed monster**" (Shakespeare).
🟡 Medium ↔ Content, Generous.

### 😬 NERVOUS
`Uneasy → Anxious → Nervous → Tense → Apprehensive → Jittery → Edgy → Fretful`
🟦 Worried and restless.
🟩 Butterflies in the stomach before results.
🔴 High ↔ Calm, Relaxed, Composed.

### 😕 CONFUSED
`Puzzled → Confused → Baffled → Bewildered → Perplexed → Flummoxed → Disoriented`
🟦 Not able to understand clearly.
🟩 Lost in a maze with no map.
🟡 Medium ↔ Clear, Certain, Sure.

### 😳 ASHAMED
`Embarrassed → Ashamed → Humiliated → Mortified · Sheepish · Abashed · Chagrined`
🟦 Feeling bad about something done.
🟩 Wishing the ground would swallow you.
🟡 Medium ↔ Proud, Confident.

### 😤 ARROGANT
`Proud → Conceited → Arrogant → Haughty → Pompous → Supercilious → Disdainful → Overbearing`
🟦 Thinking too highly of oneself.
🟩 Nose stuck in the air, looking down on all.
🔴 High · 🟧 ↔ Humble, Modest, Meek.

### 😞 ANXIOUS / WORRIED
`Concerned → Worried → Troubled → Distressed → Agitated → Anguished`
🟦 Feeling distress about something.
🟡 Medium ↔ Carefree, Untroubled.

> 🟧 SSC's favourite trap area (Spotting Errors + Sentence Improvement + Fill-in-blanks). Learn the **one-line trick** for each.

### 1. Affect ↔ Effect
- **Affect** = to influence (verb). *The weather affects my mood.*
- **Effect** = a result (noun). *The medicine had a good effect.*
🟩 **A**ffect = **A**ction (verb); **E**ffect = **E**nd result.

### 2. Accept ↔ Except
- **Accept** = to receive/agree. *I accept your offer.*
- **Except** = excluding. *Everyone came except Ram.*
🟩 ac**Cept** = **C**onsent; ex**Cept** = ex**C**lude.

### 3. Complement ↔ Compliment
- **Complement** = completes/goes well with. *The tie complements the suit.*
- **Compliment** = words of praise. *She paid me a compliment.*
🟩 Compl**e**ment = compl**e**te; Compl**i**ment = "**I** like it!".

### 4. Advice ↔ Advise
- **Advice** = noun (suggestion). *Take my advice.*
- **Advise** = verb (to suggest). *I advise you to rest.*
🟩 advi**c**e = noun (like "i**c**e"); advi**s**e = verb (the "**s**" hisses an action).

### 5. Principal ↔ Principle
- **Principal** = head of school / main. *The principal spoke.*
- **Principle** = a rule/belief. *A man of principles.*
🟩 Princip**al** = your **pal** (the head); Princip**le** = ru**le**.

### 6. Stationary ↔ Stationery
- **Stationary** = not moving. *The car was stationary.*
- **Stationery** = paper/pens. *Buy stationery.*
🟩 Station**a**ry = st**a**nd still; Station**e**ry = **e**nvelope/p**e**n.

### 7. Loose ↔ Lose
- **Loose** = not tight (adj). *Loose shirt.*
- **Lose** = to misplace/not win (verb). *Don't lose the match.*
🟩 Loose has a double-o → too loose/baggy.

### 8. Their / There / They're
- **Their** = belonging to them.
- **There** = a place.
- **They're** = they are.
🟩 **There** has **here** inside → a place.

### 9. Beside ↔ Besides
- **Beside** = next to. *Sit beside me.*
- **Besides** = in addition to. *Besides English, I know Hindi.*

### 10. Council ↔ Counsel
- **Council** = a group/committee.
- **Counsel** = advice / to advise (also lawyer).
🟩 Coun**c**il = **c**ommittee; Coun**s**el = **s**uggestion.

### 11. Eminent ↔ Imminent
- **Eminent** = famous/distinguished. *An eminent scientist.*
- **Imminent** = about to happen. *Danger is imminent.*
🟩 **I**mminent = **I**ncoming soon.

### 12. Adapt ↔ Adopt ↔ Adept
- **Adapt** = to adjust. *Adapt to change.*
- **Adopt** = to take as one's own. *Adopt a child.*
- **Adept** = skilled. *Adept at chess.*
🟩 ad**A**pt = **A**djust; ad**O**pt = **O**wn it; ad**E**pt = **E**xpert.

### 13. Cite / Site / Sight
- **Cite** = to quote/refer.
- **Site** = a location.
- **Sight** = vision/view.

### 14. Personal ↔ Personnel
- **Personal** = private/individual.
- **Personnel** = staff/employees (stress on *-nel*).

### 15. Elicit ↔ Illicit
- **Elicit** = to draw out (a response).
- **Illicit** = illegal.
🟩 **Il**licit = **Il**legal.

# Chapter 11 — Phrasal Verbs

> [!NOTE]
> 🟦 Same verb + different particle = different meaning. Group by verb to remember faster.

### CARRY
- **Carry on** = continue. *Carry on with your work.*

### LOOK
- **Look after** = to take care of. *Look after the baby.*
- **Look into** = to investigate. *Police looked into the case.*
- **Look up to** = to respect/admire. *I look up to my teacher.*
- **Look down on** = to despise. *Don't look down on the poor.*
- **Look for** = to search. *I'm looking for my keys.*

- **Bring up** = (1) to raise a child (2) to mention a topic. *She brought up two kids. He brought up a new point.*

# Chapter 15 — Exam Hall Elimination Techniques

> [!TIP]
> 🟩 When you don't *know* the word, you can still *reason* the answer. Here's the toolkit.

### 1️⃣ Use the Root
**Q: Synonym of MALADROIT?**
Break it: *mal* (bad) + *adroit* (skilful) → "not skilful" → **Clumsy**. ✅
👉 Roots convert unknown words into solvable ones.

### 2️⃣ Use the Prefix
**Q: Antonym of CONCORD?**
*con* (together) + *cord* (heart) = harmony → opposite is **Discord** (*dis* = apart). ✅

### 3️⃣ Use the Suffix / Word Family
A word ending in **-ous / -ful** is positive-ish; **-less** removes; this hints at meaning when you're stuck on tone.

### 4️⃣ Use Context Clues (in fill-in-the-blanks)
**Q:** *Despite the ___ evidence, the jury acquitted him.*
"Despite" + "acquitted" → evidence was strong but ignored → blank = **damning / incriminating**, not "weak". 👉 Read the *connector* (despite, although, because, therefore).

### 5️⃣ Use Connotation (positive vs negative)
If the question word feels **negative** (e.g., *notorious*), eliminate all **positive** options. *Notorious* = famous for bad reasons → reject "celebrated, admired", keep "infamous".

### 6️⃣ Use Elimination by Word-Pair Logic
For antonyms, two options are often near-synonyms of the question word (traps). Spot and eliminate the synonym(s); the lone "opposite" survives.

### 🎯 Real SSC-Style Worked Examples
1. **Synonym of "Benevolent"** → *bene* = good → **Kind** (reject Malicious, Cruel, Indifferent).
2. **Antonym of "Transient"** → transit = passing → opposite = **Permanent** (reject Temporary, Brief = synonyms/traps).
3. **OWS: "One who cannot be corrected"** → in- + corrigible → **Incorrigible**.
4. **Idiom "to throw in the towel"** → boxing image of surrender → **To give up** (reject literal "to clean up").
5. **Confusing: "The new rule will _____ everyone." (affect/effect)** → need a verb → **affect**. ✅

> [!WARNING]
> 🟥 **Golden exam rules:**
> 1. For antonyms, eliminate synonyms first.
> 2. For idioms, never pick the literal meaning.
> 3. For confusing words, decide noun-vs-verb first.
> 4. When fully stuck, choose the option whose **root/connotation** matches — never leave a no-negative-marking guess blank, but in negative-marking sections, skip only if you can't eliminate even one option.

# Chapter 17 — Error Spotting & Sentence Improvement: Tricks & Tips

> [!IMPORTANT]
> SSC's **Spotting Errors** and **Sentence Improvement** questions test ~10 fixed grammar rules every year. Master these rules and you turn guesswork into a system. Format: **Rule → Trap → Correct example.**

## 🧭 The 6-Step Scanning Method (use on every question)
1. **Find the verb** → check it agrees with the subject (number + person).
2. **Check the tense** → is it consistent with time words (since, ago, yesterday)?
3. **Check prepositions** → SSC's #1 error zone.
4. **Check articles** (a/an/the) → especially "a" vs "an" and missing "the".
5. **Check pronouns** → does each pronoun match its noun?
6. **Read for "sense"** → if a part *sounds* wrong, it usually is. Trust trained instinct.

## 🔑 Rule 1 — Subject–Verb Agreement (highest weight 🟧)

> [!WARNING]
> 🟥 Singular subject → singular verb; plural subject → plural verb.

| Rule | Correct |
|---|---|
| **Each / every / either / neither / one** = singular | *Each of the boys **was** present.* |
| **Either…or / neither…nor** → verb agrees with the **nearer** subject | *Neither he nor his friends **are** coming.* |
| **A number of** = plural; **The number of** = singular | *A number of students **are** absent. The number **is** rising.* |
| **Collective nouns** (team, jury, family) usually singular | *The team **is** winning.* |
| **Mathematics, news, physics, politics** look plural but are singular | *No news **is** good news.* |
| **Two subjects joined by "and"** = plural | *Ram and Shyam **are** friends.* |
| **"and" linking one idea / same person** = singular | *Bread and butter **is** my breakfast.* |
| **Distance, money, time as one unit** = singular | *Ten kilometres **is** a long walk.* |

🟩 **Trap:** *"One of the students who **has** topped..."* → wrong; "who" refers to *students* (plural) → **have**.

## 🔑 Rule 2 — Tenses

| Rule | Correct |
|---|---|
| **Since / for** + present perfect (continuing action) | *I **have lived** here since 2010.* |
| **Past time words** (ago, yesterday, last) → simple past, **never** present perfect | *He **came** yesterday* (NOT has come) |
| **When clause** in past → main clause also past | *When he **came**, I **was** sleeping.* |
| **Universal truths** → always present tense | *The sun **rises** in the east.* |
| **"If" (conditional type 1)** → present + will | *If it **rains**, we **will** stay.* |
| **"If" (type 2 unreal)** → past + would | *If I **were** rich, I **would** travel.* |

🟩 **Trap:** *"He said that he **will** come"* → reported speech → **would** come.

## 🔑 Rule 3 — Prepositions (SSC's favourite trap 🟧)

| Correct usage | Common wrong form |
|---|---|
| **Married to** (not married with) | ✗ married with |
| **Discuss** (no "about") | ✗ discuss about |
| **Comprises / consists of** | ✗ comprises of |
| **Superior/inferior TO** (not "than") | ✗ superior than |
| **Different FROM** | ✗ different than |
| **Composed OF** | ✗ composed from |
| **Insist ON / Depend ON** | ✗ insist about |
| **Capable OF / Afraid OF** | ✗ capable to |
| **Good AT / Bad AT** | ✗ good in |
| **Die OF (disease) / die FROM (injury)** | ✗ die with |
| **Congratulate ON** | ✗ congratulate for |
| **Prevent FROM** | ✗ prevent to |

🟩 **Memory:** "Discuss, order, request, describe, resemble" take **no preposition** after them.

## 🔑 Rule 4 — Articles (a / an / the)

| Rule | Correct |
|---|---|
| **"An"** before vowel **sound** (not letter) | *an hour, an MA, a university, a one-rupee coin* |
| **"The"** before unique things, superlatives, rivers, oceans | *the Sun, the best, the Ganga* |
| **No article** before proper nouns, languages, meals, abstract nouns | *I had breakfast. He teaches English.* |
| **"The"** before adjective used as plural noun | *The poor need help.* |

🟩 **Trap:** *"a honest man"* → "honest" begins with a vowel **sound** (silent h) → **an honest man**.

## 🔑 Rule 5 — Pronouns

| Rule | Correct |
|---|---|
| Pronoun must match noun in number/gender | *Everyone must do **his/their** duty.* |
| **Order of pronouns:** 231 (You, He, I) — second, third, first | *You, he and I **are** ready.* |
| But for **confessing a fault** → 123 (I first) | *I and he are guilty.* |
| **Between** takes object pronoun | *Between you and **me*** (NOT "I") |
| **Than/as** + subject pronoun (with verb implied) | *He is taller than **I (am)**.* |

🟩 **Trap:** *"Let you and **I** go"* → after "let" use object form → **me**.

## 🔑 Rule 6 — Adjectives, Adverbs & Degrees

| Rule | Correct |
|---|---|
| **Double comparative/superlative** = error | ✗ more better → ✓ better |
| **"Than any other"** for comparison within a group | *He is taller than **any other** boy.* |
| **"The most" / "-est"** for three or more | *the tallest of all* |
| **Comparative for two** | *the **better** of the two* |
| **Adverb modifies verb** (not adjective) | *She sang **beautifully** (not beautiful).* |
| Words taking **no comparison:** unique, perfect, complete, round, supreme | ✗ more unique |

## 🔑 Rule 7 — Common Confusable & Usage Errors

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| Less books | **Fewer** books (countable) |
| Much friends | **Many** friends |
| Each of the two boys are | Each of the two boys **is** |
| Cope up with | **Cope with** |
| Return back / repeat again | **Return / repeat** (drop the redundant word) |
| One of my friend | One of my **friends** |
| More superior | **Superior** |
| Hardly… than | Hardly… **when** |
| No sooner… then | No sooner… **than** |
| Scarcely… than | Scarcely… **when** |
| Lest… should not | **Lest… should** (lest already = negative) |
| Both… as well as | **Both… and** |

## 💡 Top 10 Exam-Hall Tips for Error Spotting

> [!TIP]
> 🟩
> 1. **Read the whole sentence first** — don't judge a part in isolation.
> 2. **Subject–verb agreement** is the most-tested error; check it first.
> 3. Watch the **"each / every / one of / neither"** singular traps.
> 4. **Prepositions** after verbs (discuss, marry, comprise) are pure memory — revise the list.
> 5. Spot **redundancy** (return back, free gift, repeat again) — SSC loves it.
> 6. Check **tense shifts** against time-words (since, ago, yesterday).
> 7. Verify **a/an** by **sound**, not spelling.
> 8. In comparisons, look for **double comparatives** and "than any" vs "than any other".
> 9. **"No error"** is a real option (~10–15% of the time) — don't force an error if rules check out.
> 10. **Eliminate** parts that are 100% correct; the error hides in the part you're unsure about.

> [!WARNING]
> 🟥 **Golden rule:** When two parts both look fine, the error is usually a **preposition** or a **subject–verb mismatch** hidden by a phrase in between (e.g., *"The quality of the apples **were** poor"* → subject is *quality* → **was**).
