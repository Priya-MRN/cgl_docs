## A. The Constitution

- **Adopted: 26 November 1949; in force: 26 January 1950.** Drafting Committee Chairman: **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**.
- Borrowed features: Parliamentary system & rule of law (UK), Fundamental Rights & Judicial Review (USA), DPSP (Ireland), Emergency (Germany), Concurrent List (Australia), Fundamental Duties (USSR).
- World's **longest written constitution**.

**Preamble** declares India a **SOVEREIGN, SOCIALIST, SECULAR, DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC** securing Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity. ("Socialist", "Secular", "Integrity" added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**.)

---

## B. Fundamental Rights, Duties & DPSP

**Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35) — 6 rights:**
1. Right to Equality (14–18)
2. Right to Freedom (19–22)
3. Right against Exploitation (23–24)
4. Right to Freedom of Religion (25–28)
5. Cultural & Educational Rights (29–30)
6. Right to Constitutional Remedies (32) — Ambedkar's "**heart and soul**" of the Constitution.

> **Right to Property** was removed as a FR by the **44th Amendment (1978)** → now a legal right (Art. 300A).
> **Writs:** Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto.

**Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Article 51A)** — added by **42nd Amendment (1976)** on the **Swaran Singh Committee** recommendation; originally 10, now **11** (11th added by 86th Amdt, 2002).

**Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Articles 36–51)** — non-justiciable guidelines (borrowed from Ireland); e.g. uniform civil code, village panchayats, free legal aid.

## C. Union, State & Union Territory

**Union Executive**
- **President** (Art. 52) — nominal head; elected by an electoral college (elected MPs + MLAs). Term 5 years.
- **Vice-President** — ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
- **Prime Minister & Council of Ministers** — real executive; collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.

**Union Legislature (Parliament)**
- **Lok Sabha** (House of the People) — max 552; directly elected; term 5 years; presided by the **Speaker**.
- **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States) — max 250; permanent house (1/3 retire every 2 years).
- **Money Bill** (Art. 110) originates only in Lok Sabha.

**State machinery:** Governor (appointed by President), Chief Minister & Council, State Legislature (Vidhan Sabha; some states have a Vidhan Parishad). **High Court** at the state level.

**Union Territories:** administered by the President through an Administrator/LG.

## D. Federalism & Centre–State Relations

- India = a **federation with a unitary bias** ("quasi-federal").
- **Three Lists (7th Schedule):** Union List, State List, Concurrent List (residuary powers with the Union).
- **Centre–State relations:** legislative, administrative, financial; **Finance Commission** (Art. 280) recommends tax-sharing. **NITI Aayog** (2015) replaced the Planning Commission, promoting cooperative federalism.

---

## E. Elections & Judiciary

- **Election Commission of India (Art. 324)** — independent constitutional body; conducts elections; multi-member since 1993 (CEC + 2 ECs).
- **Judiciary:** integrated & independent. **Supreme Court** (Art. 124) → High Courts → subordinate courts. **Judicial review** & **Rule of Law** are basic features. **Basic Structure doctrine** — *Kesavananda Bharati* case (1973).

## F. Accountability — Anti-Corruption, RTI, Rights Bodies

| Body / Law | Note |
|---|---|
| **Lokpal & Lokayuktas Act, 2013** | Lokpal at Centre, Lokayukta in states — ombudsmen against corruption |
| **CVC** (Central Vigilance Commission) | Statutory (2003); top anti-corruption watchdog |
| **CAG** (Art. 148) | Audits government accounts; "guardian of the public purse" |
| **Right to Information Act, 2005** | Citizens can seek information; PIO, Information Commissions |
| **National Human Rights Commission (NHRC, 1993)** | Statutory; protects human rights |
| **National Commission for Women (NCW, 1992)** | Women's empowerment |
| **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** | District/State/National consumer commissions; CCPA |

## G. Political Parties & Party System

- India has a **multi-party system**; parties recognised as **National** or **State** parties by the ECI.
- **Anti-Defection Law** — 10th Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985).

## Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Why is Article 32 the "heart and soul" of the Constitution?**
Because it guarantees the **Right to Constitutional Remedies** — citizens can directly approach the Supreme Court (via writs) to enforce other Fundamental Rights. Without it, the rights would be unenforceable.

**Example 2 — How does the Basic Structure doctrine limit Parliament?**
In *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)*, the SC held that Parliament can amend the Constitution but **cannot alter its "basic structure"** (e.g. secularism, federalism, judicial review, rule of law).
