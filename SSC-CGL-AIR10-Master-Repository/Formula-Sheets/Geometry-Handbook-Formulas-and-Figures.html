3. The Four Centres of a Triangle

CentreWhere lines meetNote
Centroid Gthe 3 medians (vertex → midpoint)divides each median 2:1 (from vertex)
Incentre Ithe 3 angle bisectorscentre of inscribed circle
Circumcentre Othe 3 perp. bisectors of the sidescentre of circumscribed ○; equidistant from vertices
Orthocentre Hthe 3 altitudes (vertex ⟂ opposite side)—
• G, O, H are collinear → the Euler line; and OG : GH = 1 : 2.
• In an equilateral triangle all four centres coincide.
Radii: inradius r = Area / s  ·  circumradius R = (a·b·c) / (4·Area).
In a right triangle, R = hypotenuse / 2 and the circumcentre O is the midpoint of the hypotenuse.

9B. Intersecting Chords, Secants & Tangent–Secant (Power of a Point) ⭐

Theorem (Power of a Point)Relation
Two chords intersect inside at XAX · XB = CX · XD
Two secants from an external point PPA · PB = PC · PD
Tangent–Secant from external point PPT² = PA · PB
Two tangents from external point PPT₁ = PT₂ (equal lengths)

🪄 One idea: for any point P and a line through it cutting the circle at two points, the product of the two distances is constant (the "power" of P). Inside → chord·chord; outside → secant·secant; tangent is the limiting case where the two points merge → PT².
