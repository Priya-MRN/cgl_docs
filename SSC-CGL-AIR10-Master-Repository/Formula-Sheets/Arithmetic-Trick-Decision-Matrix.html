1 · Profit & Loss 🔴 High Repeat · 2–4 Q/paper

Golden rule: fix CP = 100 (or CP = ₹100 per unit) and everything becomes a percentage you can read off directly.

2 · Percentage 🔴 Feeds P&L, SI/CI, DI

Convert every % to a fraction (25%=1/4, 12½%=1/8, 16⅔%=1/6). The fraction method is 3× faster than long multiplication.

Price ↑, cut use Price rises x%% cut in consumption "expenditure unchanged" x/(100+x)×100 Rise 1/4 → cut 1/(4+1)=1/5 +25% → 25/125 = 20% Fraction: rise 1/n → cut 1/(n+1) Reducing by the same 25%
A is x% more than B A vs B relationB is how much less "by what % is B less than A" x/(100+x)×100 +25% ↔ −20% (5:4 pair) A 25% more → B is 20% less Memorize ± pairs (⅓↔¼, 25↔20) Assuming it is also 25%
Election margin Winner w%, wins by VTotal votes "won by … votes" Margin=(2w−100)% ; Total=V×100/margin V ÷ margin-fraction 60%, win 1600 → margin 20% → 8000 V/(margin%) ×100 Dividing V by 60% not by margin

CI−SI, 3 yr Difference, r, t=3Principal "…for 3 years" Diff = P(r/100)²·(3+r/100) — r 10%: Diff = P(0.01)(3.1) Plug once; watch the (3+r/100) Forgetting the (3+r/100) factor

Amount at two times A at t and at 2tPrincipal "becomes X after 3 yr, Y after 6 yr" Ratio Y/X = (1+r)ᵗ ; P=X/(that) Y/X gives the growth factor for t 20070/13380=1.5; P=13380/1.5=8920 Divide the two amounts first Trying to find r explicitly

4 · Time & Work + Pipes & Cisterns 🔴 Every paper

LCM method beats fractions every time. Total Work = LCM(days). Efficiency = Total ÷ days → whole numbers → add/subtract freely.

SituationGivenAskedRecognition cueShortcut / FormulaLCM / Ratio trickWorked exampleFastest routeCommon mistake
Two together A=a days, B=b daysCombined time "working together" ab/(a+b) LCM: add efficiencies, Total÷sum 12 & 18 → 216/30 = 7.2 d Small numbers: ab/(a+b) mentally Averaging the two times
Find one alone (A+B) & BA alone "together …, B alone …" 1/A = 1/T − 1/B LCM: effᴬ = Total − effᴮ T=8,B=12 → 1/8−1/12=1/24 → 24 d Subtract efficiencies on LCM Subtracting the days directly
Pairwise (A+B,B+C,C+A) 3 pair-timesAll-three time "A&B in…, B&C in…, C&A in…" Sum pairs = 2(A+B+C) LCM: pair effs, halve the sum 12,15,20 → sum 1/5 → all = 10 d Add three rates, halve Averaging the three pair-times
% more efficient A is x% more than BOther's time "25% more efficient" eff 125:100=5:4 → time 4:5 Time ratio = 100:(100+x) A=20d (4 parts) → B = 25 d Flip eff-ratio to time-ratio Adding 25% to A's days
Leave / join midway Some work, then changeRemaining days "after k days, X leaves/joins" Units done → Total − done → ÷ new rate Track units on the LCM board B+C 3d=42u of 72 → A does 30/3=10 d Subtract units, divide by new eff Restarting the whole fraction sum
Men–Days–Hours M₁D₁H₁, work scaledNew M/D/H "×larger work, hrs/day" M₁D₁H₁/W₁ = M₂D₂H₂/W₂ Keep man-days constant 10·15·8/1 = 12·D·10/3 → 30 d One chain-rule equation Mixing men & days before final ÷
Pipe + leak Fill x, empty yNet fill time "leak empties in …" Net = 1/x − 1/y (inlet +, leak −) LCM: +eff for fill, −eff for leak 10 & 15 → +3−2=+1 of 30 → 30 h LCM board with signs Adding instead of subtracting leak
Leak from delay Fills in x, took x′ with leakLeak-empty time "due to leak took longer" Leak = x·x′/(x′−x) 1/x − 1/x′ = leak rate 12 & 16 → 192/4 = 48 h x·x′/(x′−x) Using x′−x as the answer

5 · Partnership

Profit share ∝ capital × time. Reduce to a clean ratio, then split. Deduct any salary/commission before ratio-splitting.

SituationGivenAskedRecognition cueShortcut / FormulaRatio / ButterflyWorked exampleFastest routeCommon mistake
Equal time Capitals, same periodProfit split "invested for a year each" Share ∝ capital Ratio of capitals C 3000:5000 = 3:5 of profit Cap ratio = profit ratio Forgetting to reduce ratio
Different times Capital & months differProfit split "A for 12 mo, B for 8 mo" Share ∝ C×T Multiply C×T, take ratio C 4000×12 : 6000×8 = 48:48 = 1:1 Equivalent capital = C×T Using capital only, ignoring time
Work-done share (link) Wages for a jobEach share "earn ₹… for the work" Wages ∝ work = eff×days C does 4/11 → 4/11 of money ₹1540, C does 4/11 → ₹560 Work-fraction × total money Splitting by days agreed, not work
Working partner Salary + capital ratioNet split "gets salary for managing" Deduct salary → split remainder by C×T Remaining ÷ equivalent capital Profit−salary, then ratio Salary out first, ratio after Ratio-splitting the whole profit

6 · Mixture & Alligation

Alligation is the fastest tool whenever a mean/average value sits between two ingredient values — it gives the mix ratio in one cross.

SituationGivenAskedRecognition cueShortcut / FormulaAlligation cross (Butterfly)Worked exampleFastest routeCommon mistake
Two-ingredient mix Cheap c, dear d, mean mMix ratio "mixed to cost/average …" cheap:dear = (d−m):(m−c) Cross-subtract m diagonally Rice ₹20 & ₹30, mean 24 → (30−24):(24−20)=6:4=3:2 Draw the X, cross-subtract Reversing which diff goes with which
Milk–water dilution Add water freeRatio / profit "adds water and sells at CP" Water is 0-cost ingredient in cross Treat water value = 0 Milk ₹40 to mean 32 → water:milk = (40−32):(32−0)=8:32=1:4 Alligation with 0 for water Forgetting water contributes 0 value
Repeated replacement Remove & refill n timesFinal pure qty "drawn off and replaced, n times" Final = V(1−r/V)ⁿ Ratio (1−r/V) per operation 40L, 8L out ×3 → 40(4/5)³ = 20.48 L Power formula once Subtracting r each time linearly
Two mixtures combined Ratios in each vesselFinal ratio "mix vessel A and B" Find each part's fraction, alligate on the fraction Cross on the milk-fraction 3:2 & 7:3 milk → alligate .6 & .7 Alligation on the component fraction Adding the ratios directly

8 · Trains

Decide the distance to cover first: pole = train length only; platform/bridge = length + platform; passing another train = sum of both lengths.

SituationGivenAskedRecognition cueShortcut / FormulaRatio trickWorked exampleFastest routeCommon mistake
Cross a pole/man L, tSpeed "crosses a pole/standing man" S = L/t — 150 m in 15 s → 10 m/s = 36 km/h L/t then ×18/5 Adding a platform that isn't there
Cross platform/bridge L, P, tSpeed / length "crosses a platform/bridge" S = (L+P)/t — 200+300 in 25 s → 20 m/s = 72 km/h (L+P)/t Forgetting to add platform
Pole then platform t₁ (pole), t₂ (platform P)Length "pole in …, platform in …" Extra time (t₂−t₁) covers P → S=P/(t₂−t₁) Platform ÷ extra time = speed 120 m in extra 6 s → 20 m/s, L=240 m P/(t₂−t₁) gives speed instantly Setting up two full equations slowly
Two trains, opposite L₁,L₂, speedsCrossing time "opposite directions" Rel speed = S₁+S₂; dist = L₁+L₂ Add speeds, add lengths 120+80 m at 72 km/h(20 m/s) → 10 s (L₁+L₂)/(S₁+S₂) Subtracting speeds
Two trains, same dir L₁,L₂, speedsOvertake time "same direction / overtakes" Rel speed = S₁−S₂; dist = L₁+L₂ Subtract speeds, add lengths 300 m at 12 km/h(10/3 m/s) → 90 s (L₁+L₂)/(S₁−S₂) Using only one train's length

9 · Boats & Streams

Two back-solve identities crack almost everything: b = (down+up)/2 and s = (down−up)/2.

SituationGivenAskedRecognition cueShortcut / FormulaRatio trickWorked exampleFastest routeCommon mistake
Basic up/down b (still), s (stream)Down/up speed "downstream / upstream" down=b+s, up=b−s — b 8, s 2 → down 10, up 6 Add for down, subtract for up Swapping the ± signs
Back-solve b & s down & up speeds/timesb or s "…km down in …h, back in …h" b=(d+u)/2, s=(d−u)/2 Convert each leg to speed first 15 & 9 → b=12, s=3 Speeds first, then the two identities Averaging times, not speeds
Up = k × down time Time ratio, s knownb "twice/thrice as long upstream" down speed = k × up speed → solve (b+s)=k(b−s) up=2×down time, s=3 → b=9 Set (b+s)=k(b−s), solve b Equating times to speeds directly
There-and-back time b, s, total timeDistance "to a place and back in … h" d/(b+s) + d/(b−s) = T Sum the two leg-times b6,s2,T2h → d/8+d/4=2 → 16/3 km One equation, solve d Using one speed for both legs

Three universal speed-tools that reappear in every topic above:
◆ Butterfly / cross-multiplication — combining ratios, alligation, C×T partnership, proportion (ad=bc).
▣ LCM board — Time&Work, Pipes; turn every rate into a whole number.
⚖ Ratio inversion — efficiency↔time, speed↔time; whatever is "more" of one is "less" of the other in exact inverse.
