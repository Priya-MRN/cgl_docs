# Analogy & Classification (Odd-One-Out) — 500 Question Practice Set (SSC CGL, Reasoning / Tier-I & II Level)

> **Format:** All 500 questions appear first; the full **Answer Key & Explanations** is at the very end.
> **Difficulty skew:** ~20% Medium · ~60% Hard · ~20% Very-Hard — CGL standard.
> **Rule-based items (number/letter analogy & classification) are generated by an exact rule and machine-verified** — the keyed option is provably correct. **Word/GK analogies are hand-authored and fact-checked**, each with one defensible answer.
> Negative marking in CGL is −0.50; distractors are real common-mistake traps (wrong rule, off-by-one, plausible-but-wrong fact).

## How these questions work (quick reference)
- **Number analogy** `a : f(a) :: b : ?` — find the rule (square, cube, ×k, +k, n(n+1), n²+k) and apply it to b.
- **Number classification** — three numbers share a property (all squares / cubes / primes / divisible by k); pick the one that breaks it.
- **Letter analogy** `XY : ?? :: PQ : ?` — letters move by a fixed (or positional) shift; A=1…Z=26, wrap Z→A.
- **Letter/alphanumeric classification** — three clusters share a gap pattern or letter↔number rule; pick the odd one.
- **Word/GK analogy** — semantic relation (worker:workplace, animal:young, country:capital, instrument:player, etc.).

**Q2. (b) 148**  *[H]* — The rule is n -> n^2+4: 19^2+4=365, 15^2+4=229. So the answer is 12^2+4 = 148.
**Q3. (c) 158**  *[VH]* — Three numbers are products of two consecutive integers, n(n+1) (90, 182, 210); 158 cannot be written as n(n+1), so it is the odd one out.
**Q4. (a) 92**  *[M]* — Three of the four numbers are exactly divisible by 7 (35, 63, 91); 92 is not divisible by 7 (92/7 leaves remainder 1), so it is the odd one out.
**Q5. (b) 108**  *[VH]* — Three numbers are products of two consecutive integers, n(n+1) (12, 110, 182); 108 cannot be written as n(n+1), so it is the odd one out.
**Q6. (d) MRU**  *[H]* — Each letter moves +3 places (BEK->EHN). Applying +3 to JOR gives MRU.
**Q7. (c) Paris**  *[H]* — Relationship: river : the city on it. As Thames -> London, so Seine -> Paris.
**Q8. (d) NKO**  *[H]* — Each letter moves +2 places (HNS->JPU). Applying +2 to LIM gives NKO.
**Q9. (b) 67**  *[H]* — Three of the four numbers are perfect cubes (27, 64, 216); 67 is not a perfect cube, so it is the odd one out.
