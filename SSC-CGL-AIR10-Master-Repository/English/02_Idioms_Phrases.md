# Idioms & Phrases — Solved Question Bank

> Navigation: [English Master Bank](README.md) · [English Grammar Cheat Sheet](../Cheat-Sheets/English_Grammar_Cheat_Sheet.md)

## Key Rules
- An **idiom** has a figurative meaning that cannot be guessed from the literal words.
- Memorise idioms with a **vivid mental image** to recall the meaning quickly.
- Watch out for distractor options that match the literal words but not the meaning.
- Context within the sentence often hints at whether the meaning is positive or negative.

## EASY

**Q1.** What does **"A piece of cake"** mean?
(a) A tasty dessert  (b) Something very easy  (c) A small portion  (d) A celebration
**Answer: (b) Something very easy**
*Solution:* "A piece of cake" figuratively means a task that is very easy. The literal dessert meanings (a, c, d) are distractors.

**Q2.** What does **"Break the ice"** mean?
(a) To start a conversation in a social setting  (b) To break something frozen  (c) To end a friendship  (d) To feel cold
**Answer: (a) To start a conversation in a social setting**
*Solution:* "Break the ice" means to ease initial tension and start interacting. Options b and d are literal; c is unrelated.

**Q3.** What does **"Once in a blue moon"** mean?
(a) Very often  (b) During night  (c) Very rarely  (d) Every month
**Answer: (c) Very rarely**
*Solution:* "Once in a blue moon" means something that happens very rarely. The other options misread "moon" literally.

**Q4.** What does **"Hit the books"** mean?
(a) To throw books  (b) To study hard  (c) To buy books  (d) To stop reading
**Answer: (b) To study hard**
*Solution:* "Hit the books" is an idiom for studying seriously. The literal actions (a, c, d) are distractors.

**Q5.** What does **"Under the weather"** mean?
(a) Outside in the rain  (b) Feeling unwell  (c) Very happy  (d) Forecasting weather
**Answer: (b) Feeling unwell**
*Solution:* "Under the weather" means slightly ill or unwell. The weather-related options are literal traps.

**Q6.** What does **"Let the cat out of the bag"** mean?
(a) To free a pet  (b) To reveal a secret  (c) To make a mistake  (d) To buy an animal
**Answer: (b) To reveal a secret**
*Solution:* "Let the cat out of the bag" means to disclose a secret, often accidentally. Option a is literal; c and d are unrelated.

## MEDIUM

**Q1.** What does **"To bite the bullet"** mean?
(a) To act foolishly  (b) To endure a painful situation bravely  (c) To win easily  (d) To eat quickly
**Answer: (b) To endure a painful situation bravely**
*Solution:* "Bite the bullet" means to face something difficult or unpleasant with courage. The other choices do not match this sense.

**Q2.** What does **"To cost an arm and a leg"** mean?
(a) To be very cheap  (b) To cause an injury  (c) To be extremely expensive  (d) To be free
**Answer: (c) To be extremely expensive**
*Solution:* "Cost an arm and a leg" means to be very expensive. Options a and d are opposites; b is literal.

**Q3.** What does **"To turn a blind eye"** mean?
(a) To go blind  (b) To ignore something deliberately  (c) To look closely  (d) To wink
**Answer: (b) To ignore something deliberately**
*Solution:* "Turn a blind eye" means to pretend not to notice something wrong. The literal sight options are distractors.

**Q4.** What does **"To be in hot water"** mean?
(a) To take a bath  (b) To be in trouble  (c) To be relaxed  (d) To feel warm
**Answer: (b) To be in trouble**
*Solution:* "In hot water" means in a difficult situation or trouble. The bathing/temperature options are literal traps.

**Q5.** What does **"To pull someone's leg"** mean?
(a) To injure someone  (b) To help someone walk  (c) To tease or joke with someone  (d) To race someone
**Answer: (c) To tease or joke with someone**
*Solution:* "Pull someone's leg" means to tease them playfully. Options a, b and d misread the phrase literally.

**Q6.** What does **"To beat around the bush"** mean?
(a) To clean a garden  (b) To avoid the main point  (c) To win a fight  (d) To get straight to the point
**Answer: (b) To avoid the main point**
*Solution:* "Beat around the bush" means to talk evasively without addressing the issue. Option d is its opposite.

**Q7.** What does **"To add fuel to the fire"** mean?
(a) To solve a problem  (b) To make a situation worse  (c) To cook food  (d) To stay calm
**Answer: (b) To make a situation worse**
*Solution:* "Add fuel to the fire" means to worsen an already tense situation. Option a is the opposite; c is literal.

## HARD

**Q1.** What does **"To steal someone's thunder"** mean?
(a) To frighten someone  (b) To take credit for another's work or idea  (c) To make noise  (d) To rob a person
**Answer: (b) To take credit for another's work or idea**
*Solution:* "Steal someone's thunder" means to win praise that should have gone to another. The literal noise/theft options are distractors.

**Q2.** What does **"A blessing in disguise"** mean?
(a) A hidden curse  (b) Something good that seemed bad at first  (c) A religious gift  (d) A costume
**Answer: (b) Something good that seemed bad at first**
*Solution:* "A blessing in disguise" is a misfortune that turns out beneficial. Option a is the opposite; c and d are literal.

**Q3.** What does **"To burn the midnight oil"** mean?
(a) To waste resources  (b) To work or study late into the night  (c) To light a lamp  (d) To sleep early
**Answer: (b) To work or study late into the night**
*Solution:* "Burn the midnight oil" means to work late at night. Option d is the opposite; c is a literal reading.

**Q4.** What does **"To throw in the towel"** mean?
(a) To clean up  (b) To give up or surrender  (c) To start a fight  (d) To take a break
**Answer: (b) To give up or surrender**
*Solution:* The boxing idiom "throw in the towel" means to admit defeat. The cleaning/fighting options do not fit.

**Q5.** What does **"To bury the hatchet"** mean?
(a) To start a quarrel  (b) To make peace and end a conflict  (c) To hide a weapon  (d) To dig a grave
**Answer: (b) To make peace and end a conflict**
*Solution:* "Bury the hatchet" means to settle a dispute and become friendly. Option a is the opposite; c is literal.

## SSC PYQ PATTERN
*(SSC CGL pattern)*

**Q1.** Select the most appropriate meaning of the idiom. **"To call it a day"**
(a) To name a day  (b) To stop working for the day  (c) To start work  (d) To take a holiday
**Answer: (b) To stop working for the day**
*Solution:* "Call it a day" means to decide to stop an activity for the day. The other options misinterpret the phrase.

**Q2.** Select the most appropriate meaning of the idiom. **"To make a mountain out of a molehill"**
(a) To climb a hill  (b) To exaggerate a minor problem  (c) To solve a big problem  (d) To work hard
**Answer: (b) To exaggerate a minor problem**
*Solution:* The idiom means to treat a trivial matter as if it were serious. Options a, c and d do not match.

**Q3.** Select the most appropriate meaning of the idiom. **"To smell a rat"**
(a) To find a pet  (b) To suspect that something is wrong  (c) To clean the house  (d) To enjoy a meal
**Answer: (b) To suspect that something is wrong**
*Solution:* "Smell a rat" means to sense that something dishonest is going on. The literal options are distractors.

**Q4.** Select the most appropriate meaning of the idiom. **"To be on cloud nine"**
(a) To be in an aeroplane  (b) To be extremely happy  (c) To be confused  (d) To be very tired
**Answer: (b) To be extremely happy**
*Solution:* "On cloud nine" means to feel extremely happy. Options a, c and d are unrelated to elation.

**Q5.** Select the most appropriate meaning of the idiom. **"To keep one's fingers crossed"**
(a) To make a promise  (b) To hope for good luck  (c) To feel angry  (d) To count something
**Answer: (b) To hope for good luck**
*Solution:* "Keep one's fingers crossed" means to hope that things go well. The other options do not capture this meaning.

### 🔑 Memory Techniques
- Attach a **picture** to each idiom (e.g. someone biting a literal bullet) to lock the meaning.
- Sort idioms by **theme** (difficulty, money, secrets) to recall related expressions together.
- Note whether the idiom is **positive or negative** — it halves the answer choices.
- Practise using each idiom in your **own sentence** to fix usage in memory.
