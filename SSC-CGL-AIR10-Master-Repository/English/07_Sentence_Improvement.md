# Sentence Improvement — Solved Question Bank

> Navigation: [English Master Bank](README.md) · [English Grammar Cheat Sheet](../Cheat-Sheets/English_Grammar_Cheat_Sheet.md)

## Key Rules
- Read the full sentence; the **underlined part** is what may need improvement.
- Check tense, agreement, prepositions, articles and idiomatic usage.
- If the underlined part is already correct, choose **"No improvement"**.
- Prefer the option that is grammatical **and** keeps the original meaning.

## EASY

**Q1.** Improve the underlined part. She **don't like** spicy food.
(a) doesn't like  (b) didn't liked  (c) not like  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) doesn't like**
*Solution:* The singular subject "She" needs "doesn't", not "don't". The other options are ungrammatical.

**Q2.** Improve the underlined part. He is junior **than me** in the office.
(a) than I  (b) to me  (c) from me  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (b) to me**
*Solution:* "Junior" is followed by "to", not "than". So "junior to me" is correct.

**Q3.** Improve the underlined part. The children **was playing** in the park.
(a) were playing  (b) is playing  (c) has played  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) were playing**
*Solution:* The plural subject "children" needs the plural verb "were playing".

**Q4.** Improve the underlined part. I look forward **to meet you** soon.
(a) to meeting you  (b) to met you  (c) for meeting you  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) to meeting you**
*Solution:* In "look forward to", the "to" is a preposition, so it is followed by the gerund "meeting".

**Q5.** Improve the underlined part. He has been working here **since five years**.
(a) for five years  (b) from five years  (c) in five years  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) for five years**
*Solution:* A duration takes "for"; "since" is used for a point in time. So "for five years" is correct.

**Q6.** Improve the underlined part. She sings **good**.
(a) well  (b) better  (c) goodly  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) well**
*Solution:* The verb "sings" needs the adverb "well", not the adjective "good".

## MEDIUM

**Q1.** Improve the underlined part. Hardly had I reached the station **than the train left**.
(a) when the train left  (b) then the train left  (c) that the train left  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) when the train left**
*Solution:* "Hardly" pairs with "when", so "when the train left" is correct.

**Q2.** Improve the underlined part. The teacher asked the students **to not talk** during the exam.
(a) not to talk  (b) to don't talk  (c) to not talking  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) not to talk**
*Solution:* The standard form places "not" before the infinitive: "not to talk".

**Q3.** Improve the underlined part. If I **was** you, I would accept the offer.
(a) were  (b) am  (c) had been  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) were**
*Solution:* A hypothetical condition uses the subjunctive "were" for all subjects, so "If I were you" is correct.

**Q4.** Improve the underlined part. He is one of the best players who **has** ever represented the country.
(a) have  (b) is  (c) was  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) have**
*Solution:* In "one of the best players who...", the verb agrees with the plural "players", so "have" is correct.

**Q5.** Improve the underlined part. The quality of the products **are** improving.
(a) is  (b) were  (c) have been  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) is**
*Solution:* The subject is "The quality" (singular); "of the products" is a modifier. So the verb should be "is".

**Q6.** Improve the underlined part. She prefers coffee **than tea**.
(a) to tea  (b) over tea  (c) from tea  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) to tea**
*Solution:* "Prefer" is followed by "to", so "prefers coffee to tea" is correct.

## HARD

**Q1.** Improve the underlined part. No sooner did the teacher enter **than the students stood up**.
(a) when the students stood up  (b) then the students stood up  (c) that the students stood up  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (d) No improvement**
*Solution:* "No sooner" correctly pairs with "than", so the underlined part is already right.

**Q2.** Improve the underlined part. The committee **were unable** to reach a decision.
(a) was unable  (b) are unable  (c) have been unable  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) was unable**
*Solution:* When the committee acts as a single body, it takes a singular verb, so "was unable" is correct.

**Q3.** Improve the underlined part. Each of the candidates **have** submitted their forms.
(a) has  (b) are  (c) were  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) has**
*Solution:* "Each" is singular and requires "has submitted".

**Q4.** Improve the underlined part. Scarcely had she left **when the phone rang**.
(a) than the phone rang  (b) then the phone rang  (c) that the phone rang  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (d) No improvement**
*Solution:* "Scarcely" pairs with "when", so the underlined part is already correct.

**Q5.** Improve the underlined part. The number of accidents **have** decreased this year.
(a) has  (b) are  (c) were  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) has**
*Solution:* "The number of" takes a singular verb, so "has decreased" is correct.

## SSC PYQ PATTERN
*(SSC CGL pattern)*

**Q1.** Select the most appropriate option to improve the underlined segment. He **succeeded to pass** the examination.
(a) succeeded in passing  (b) succeeded for passing  (c) succeeded to passing  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) succeeded in passing**
*Solution:* "Succeed" is followed by "in" + gerund, so "succeeded in passing" is correct.

**Q2.** Select the most appropriate option to improve the underlined segment. She is **more cleverer** than her sister.
(a) cleverer  (b) most clever  (c) more clever  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) cleverer**
*Solution:* "Cleverer" is already comparative; adding "more" is a double comparative error.

**Q3.** Select the most appropriate option to improve the underlined segment. The thief **ran away** as soon as he saw the police.
(a) had ran away  (b) run away  (c) was ran away  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (d) No improvement**
*Solution:* "Ran away" is the correct simple past form; the sentence needs no change.

**Q4.** Select the most appropriate option to improve the underlined segment. He insisted **on going** there alone.
(a) to go  (b) for going  (c) at going  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (d) No improvement**
*Solution:* "Insist on" + gerund is the correct construction, so "on going" is right.

**Q5.** Select the most appropriate option to improve the underlined segment. Neither of the boys **were** present in the class.
(a) was  (b) are  (c) have been  (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a) was**
*Solution:* "Neither" is singular and takes a singular verb, so "was present" is correct.

### 🔑 Memory Techniques
- Memorise **verb + preposition** patterns: succeed in, insist on, prefer to, look forward to (+ -ing).
- For comparatives, never combine **"more" with -er** (more taller, more cleverer are wrong).
- Use **"No improvement"** confidently when correlatives and agreement already match.
- Strip out **modifier phrases** to test if the verb agrees with the true subject.
