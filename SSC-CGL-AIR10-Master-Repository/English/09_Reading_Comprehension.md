# Reading Comprehension — Solved Question Bank

> Navigation: [English Master Bank](README.md) · [English Grammar Cheat Sheet](../Cheat-Sheets/English_Grammar_Cheat_Sheet.md)

## Key Rules
- Read the passage **once for the main idea**, then return to it for specific details.
- For inference questions, choose what the passage **implies**, not your outside opinion.
- For vocabulary-in-context questions, replace the word and check the sentence still makes sense.
- In para jumbles, find the **opening sentence** first, then link ideas with pronouns and connectors.

## EASY

### Passage 1

> Honey bees are among the most industrious insects in nature. A single hive may contain tens of thousands of bees, each performing a specific task. The worker bees gather nectar from flowers and convert it into honey, while also pollinating the plants they visit. This pollination is vital for agriculture, as a large share of the crops we eat depends on it. The queen bee, in contrast, does not gather food; her main role is to lay eggs and ensure the survival of the colony. Scientists warn that a decline in bee populations could seriously threaten global food supplies.

**Q1.** What is the main role of the queen bee?
(a) To gather nectar  (b) To lay eggs and sustain the colony  (c) To pollinate flowers  (d) To make honey
**Answer: (b) To lay eggs and sustain the colony**
*Solution:* The passage states the queen's main role is to lay eggs and ensure the colony's survival. Gathering nectar and pollinating are the workers' tasks.

**Q2.** Why is pollination by bees important?
(a) It produces honey  (b) It helps bees travel  (c) Many crops depend on it  (d) It protects the queen
**Answer: (c) Many crops depend on it**
*Solution:* The passage says a large share of the crops we eat depends on pollination, making it vital for agriculture.

**Q3.** What do worker bees do with nectar?
(a) Feed it to the queen only  (b) Convert it into honey  (c) Store it without change  (d) Use it to build the hive
**Answer: (b) Convert it into honey**
*Solution:* The passage clearly states that worker bees convert gathered nectar into honey.

**Q4.** What concern do scientists raise?
(a) Bees are too aggressive  (b) Hives are growing too large  (c) A decline in bees could threaten food supplies  (d) Honey is becoming scarce
**Answer: (c) A decline in bees could threaten food supplies**
*Solution:* The last sentence warns that falling bee populations could seriously threaten global food supplies.

**Q5.** The word "industrious" in the passage most nearly means:
(a) Lazy  (b) Hardworking  (c) Dangerous  (d) Colourful
**Answer: (b) Hardworking**
*Solution:* "Industrious" means diligent and hardworking, which fits the description of busy bees. "Lazy" is the opposite.

## MEDIUM

### Passage 2

> For centuries, people believed that the value of education lay only in the jobs it could secure. Today, however, educators increasingly stress that learning should also cultivate curiosity and critical thinking. A student who merely memorises facts may pass examinations but will struggle to adapt in a rapidly changing world. By contrast, a student who learns to question, analyse and connect ideas is better prepared to solve unfamiliar problems. In this sense, the true purpose of education is not simply to fill the mind with information, but to teach the mind how to think.

**Q1.** What did people traditionally believe about education?
(a) That it cultivates curiosity  (b) That its value lay only in securing jobs  (c) That it teaches critical thinking  (d) That it is unnecessary
**Answer: (b) That its value lay only in securing jobs**
*Solution:* The opening sentence states the traditional belief that education's value lay only in the jobs it could secure.

**Q2.** According to the passage, what is the limitation of merely memorising facts?
(a) It improves curiosity  (b) It guarantees success in life  (c) It leaves a student unable to adapt to change  (d) It develops critical thinking
**Answer: (c) It leaves a student unable to adapt to change**
*Solution:* The passage says a student who only memorises may pass exams but will struggle to adapt in a changing world.

**Q3.** What quality does the passage value in a well-educated student?
(a) The ability to memorise quickly  (b) The ability to question, analyse and connect ideas  (c) Obedience to teachers  (d) Speed in passing exams
**Answer: (b) The ability to question, analyse and connect ideas**
*Solution:* The passage praises the student who learns to question, analyse and connect ideas as better prepared for new problems.

**Q4.** What is the author's view of the true purpose of education?
(a) To fill the mind with information  (b) To teach the mind how to think  (c) To guarantee high-paying jobs  (d) To memorise as many facts as possible
**Answer: (b) To teach the mind how to think**
*Solution:* The concluding sentence states the true purpose is not to fill the mind with information but to teach it how to think.

**Q5.** The word "cultivate" in the passage most nearly means:
(a) Destroy  (b) Develop  (c) Ignore  (d) Reduce
**Answer: (b) Develop**
*Solution:* To "cultivate" curiosity means to develop or foster it. "Destroy", "ignore" and "reduce" are opposite in sense.

## HARD

### Inference-based questions on Passage 2

**Q1.** Which statement would the author most likely agree with?
(a) Examinations are the best measure of true learning.  (b) Adaptability matters more than rote memorisation.  (c) Education has no link with employment.  (d) Curiosity is irrelevant to learning.
**Answer: (b) Adaptability matters more than rote memorisation.**
*Solution:* The passage favours adaptable, thinking students over those who merely memorise, so the author would agree adaptability matters more.

**Q2.** It can be inferred that the author considers critical thinking to be:
(a) A modern fashion with little value  (b) Essential for solving unfamiliar problems  (c) Less important than job training  (d) A barrier to passing exams
**Answer: (b) Essential for solving unfamiliar problems**
*Solution:* The passage links questioning and analysing to being "better prepared to solve unfamiliar problems", implying critical thinking is essential.

**Q3.** The tone of the passage can best be described as:
(a) Sarcastic  (b) Reflective and persuasive  (c) Angry  (d) Humorous
**Answer: (b) Reflective and persuasive**
*Solution:* The author reflects on the purpose of education and argues a viewpoint, making the tone reflective and persuasive.

**Q4.** "To teach the mind how to think" suggests that education should be:
(a) Limited to factual recall  (b) Focused on developing reasoning skills  (c) Purely vocational  (d) Discontinued after school
**Answer: (b) Focused on developing reasoning skills**
*Solution:* Teaching the mind "how to think" emphasises reasoning over mere recall.

**Q5.** Which of the following best summarises the passage?
(a) Education is only about getting a job.  (b) Education should develop thinking, not just store facts.  (c) Memorisation is the key to success.  (d) Curiosity hinders academic progress.
**Answer: (b) Education should develop thinking, not just store facts.**
*Solution:* This captures the central contrast of the passage between storing information and developing the ability to think.

## SSC PYQ PATTERN — Para Jumble
*(SSC CGL pattern)*

In each question, the first (S1) and last (S6) sentences are fixed. Arrange the middle sentences (P, Q, R, S) in the correct order.

**Q1.**
S1: A young man once visited a wise old teacher to learn the secret of success.
P: The teacher quietly pushed the man's head under the water and held it there.
Q: The teacher led him to a nearby river and asked him to step in.
R: When the man finally came up gasping, the teacher asked what he had wanted most while under water.
S: "Air," replied the man at once.
S6: "When you want success as much as you wanted air, you will surely get it," said the teacher.
(a) QPRS  (b) PQSR  (c) QRPS  (d) PRQS
**Answer: (a) QPRS**
*Solution:* S1 introduces the visit; Q takes him to the river; P pushes him under; R describes him surfacing and the question; S gives his answer "Air", which leads naturally into S6.

**Q2.**
S1: Plastic pollution has become one of the most serious environmental problems of our time.
P: Once discarded, much of this plastic ends up in rivers and oceans.
Q: Every year, millions of tonnes of plastic are produced across the world.
R: There, it harms marine life and slowly breaks into tiny particles called microplastics.
S: These microplastics eventually enter the food chain and reach human beings.
S6: Tackling this crisis therefore requires urgent action from individuals and governments alike.
(a) QPRS  (b) PQRS  (c) QRPS  (d) PRSQ
**Answer: (a) QPRS**
*Solution:* Q states the scale of production; P says discarded plastic reaches water bodies; R explains the harm there and microplastics; S follows microplastics into the food chain, leading to the call for action in S6.

**Q3.**
S1: Reading is a habit that benefits people of all ages.
P: Books, in particular, allow us to explore distant places and different eras.
Q: It opens the door to new ideas, cultures and experiences.
R: Through them, we can share the thoughts of great minds across centuries.
S: In this way, a single book can become a lifelong companion.
S6: For these reasons, cultivating the habit of reading is truly rewarding.
(a) QPRS  (b) PQRS  (c) QRPS  (d) PRSQ
**Answer: (a) QPRS**
*Solution:* Q explains what reading opens up; P narrows to books exploring places and eras; R continues with "through them" sharing great minds; S concludes the book as a companion, leading to S6.

### 🔑 Memory Techniques
- For detail questions, **scan for keywords** from the question stem inside the passage.
- For inference questions, ask: **"What must be true if the passage is true?"**
- In para jumbles, track **pronouns and connectors** (these, there, therefore) to chain sentences.
- Always identify the **independent opening sentence** before ordering the rest.
