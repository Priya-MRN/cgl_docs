# Newspaper Vocabulary — The "Elite Words" Builder

> Navigation: [English Master Bank](README.md) · [Vocabulary Synonyms & Antonyms](01_Vocabulary_Synonyms_Antonyms.md)

## Why this file exists
Indian English newspapers — **The Hindu**, **The Indian Express**, **Times of India**, **Hindustan Times**, **The Economic Times**, **The Telegraph**, **Business Standard**, **Deccan Herald**, **The New Indian Express**, **Mint** — reuse the same pool of "difficult" words in their editorials and op-eds over and over.

If you learn the words below, you will understand **most editorial English** these papers print. This is the exact vocabulary "elite" readers use — explained in the simplest possible way, because you said you are starting from the basics. **You do not need to know any of these already.**

## How to use this file (read this once)
1. Do **5 words a day**. Read the meaning, the easy synonym, and the sentence out loud.
2. Cover the meaning and try to recall it. If you fail, that's fine — try again tomorrow.
3. When you read any newspaper, **circle words you don't know** and add them at the bottom under "My Own Words".
4. Reading 1 editorial a day + this list = you will reach the top level in a few months. Slow and steady wins.

**Legend for each entry:**
**Word** *(part of speech)* — simple meaning · **easy word for it:** synonym
> *Newspaper-style sentence.*

## TIER 1 — EASY (common in newspapers, but feel "big" at first)

**Allege** *(verb)* — to say someone did something wrong, without proof yet · **easy word:** claim, accuse
> *The report alleges that the company hid its losses.*

**Curb** *(verb)* — to control or reduce something · **easy word:** limit, stop
> *The government announced new rules to curb pollution in the city.*

**Hike** *(noun/verb)* — an increase (usually in price or rate) · **easy word:** rise, raise
> *A sharp hike in fuel prices has angered commuters.*

**Slump** *(noun/verb)* — a big fall or weak period · **easy word:** drop, fall
> *The economy is facing a slump as factories cut production.*

**Boost** *(verb/noun)* — to increase or improve · **easy word:** help, lift
> *The new policy aims to boost exports.*

**Probe** *(noun/verb)* — an investigation · **easy word:** inquiry, check
> *A probe has been ordered into the bridge collapse.*

**Hail** *(verb)* — to praise something publicly · **easy word:** praise, welcome
> *Leaders hailed the agreement as a historic step.*

**Slam** *(verb)* — to criticise strongly · **easy word:** attack, blame
> *The opposition slammed the budget for ignoring farmers.*

**Urge** *(verb)* — to strongly request · **easy word:** push, ask
> *Doctors urge people to get vaccinated before winter.*

**Vow** *(verb/noun)* — to promise firmly · **easy word:** promise
> *The minister vowed to complete the project by next year.*

**Soar** *(verb)* — to rise quickly and high · **easy word:** jump, climb
> *Vegetable prices soared after heavy rains.*

**Plunge** *(verb)* — to fall suddenly and steeply · **easy word:** crash, drop
> *Stock markets plunged amid global tension.*

**Halt** *(verb/noun)* — to stop · **easy word:** stop, pause
> *Train services came to a halt due to the strike.*

**Spark** *(verb)* — to cause or start (usually trouble) · **easy word:** trigger, cause
> *The remark sparked protests across the state.*

**Woo** *(verb)* — to try to attract or win support · **easy word:** attract
> *Both parties are trying to woo young voters.*

**Ease** *(verb)* — to make less severe · **easy word:** reduce, relax
> *The bank moved to ease lending rules for small businesses.*

**Mull** *(verb)* — to think about carefully · **easy word:** consider
> *The cabinet is mulling a new tax on luxury cars.*

**Nab** *(verb)* — to catch or arrest · **easy word:** catch, arrest
> *Police nabbed two suspects near the border.*

**Oust** *(verb)* — to force someone out of power · **easy word:** remove, throw out
> *The party voted to oust its own leader.*

**Reel** *(verb)* — to be badly shaken or struggling · **easy word:** suffer
> *The region is reeling under a severe drought.*

## TIER 2 — MEDIUM (regular editorial words)

**Mitigate** *(verb)* — to make a bad thing less harmful · **easy word:** reduce, soften
> *Tree plantation can help mitigate the effects of heatwaves.*

**Scrutiny** *(noun)* — careful and close examination · **easy word:** checking, inspection
> *The deal is under scrutiny by the tax department.*

**Ambiguous** *(adjective)* — unclear, having more than one meaning · **easy word:** confusing, unclear
> *The law's ambiguous wording has confused officials.*

**Imminent** *(adjective)* — about to happen very soon · **easy word:** near, close
> *Weather officials warned of an imminent cyclone.*

**Pragmatic** *(adjective)* — practical, dealing with real facts not ideas · **easy word:** practical, sensible
> *A pragmatic approach is needed to solve the water crisis.*

**Resilient** *(adjective)* — able to recover quickly from problems · **easy word:** strong, tough
> *The economy proved resilient despite global shocks.*

**Volatile** *(adjective)* — changing suddenly and unpredictably · **easy word:** unstable, shaky
> *Oil prices remain volatile due to the conflict.*

**Stringent** *(adjective)* — very strict · **easy word:** strict, tough
> *Stringent safety norms were introduced after the accident.*

**Lucrative** *(adjective)* — earning a lot of money · **easy word:** profitable
> *The deal opened up a lucrative market for Indian firms.*

**Plummet** *(verb)* — to fall very fast · **easy word:** crash, drop
> *Sales plummeted during the lockdown.*

**Endorse** *(verb)* — to support publicly · **easy word:** support, back
> *Several scientists endorsed the new health guidelines.*

**Hamper** *(verb)* — to slow down or block progress · **easy word:** block, hold back
> *Heavy rain hampered rescue operations.*

**Reiterate** *(verb)* — to say again to make it clear · **easy word:** repeat
> *The minister reiterated that no tax would be raised.*

**Allay** *(verb)* — to reduce someone's fear or worry · **easy word:** calm, ease
> *The government tried to allay public fears about the vaccine.*

**Bolster** *(verb)* — to strengthen or support · **easy word:** strengthen, boost
> *New troops were sent to bolster the border force.*

**Deteriorate** *(verb)* — to become worse · **easy word:** worsen
> *His health deteriorated after the surgery.*

**Unprecedented** *(adjective)* — never happened before · **easy word:** new, first-ever
> *The country faced an unprecedented heatwave this summer.*

**Contentious** *(adjective)* — causing a lot of disagreement · **easy word:** disputed, controversial
> *The contentious bill was sent back for review.*

**Coalition** *(noun)* — a group of parties joining together · **easy word:** alliance, group
> *The state is ruled by a three-party coalition.*

**Mandate** *(noun)* — the power/permission given by people's votes · **easy word:** authority, approval
> *The party won a clear mandate in the election.*

**Austerity** *(noun)* — strict saving / cutting spending · **easy word:** cost-cutting
> *The government announced austerity measures to control the deficit.*

**Deficit** *(noun)* — when spending is more than income · **easy word:** shortage, gap
> *India is working to reduce its fiscal deficit.*

**Subsidy** *(noun)* — money the government gives to lower a cost · **easy word:** financial help
> *Farmers receive a subsidy on fertilisers.*

**Inflation** *(noun)* — the general rise in prices over time · **easy word:** price rise
> *Rising inflation has hit middle-class families hard.*

**Sanction** *(noun/verb)* — (1) official punishment/ban (2) to approve · **easy word:** ban / approve
> *Western countries imposed sanctions on the regime.*

## TIER 3 — HARD (true editorial / op-ed words)

**Ubiquitous** *(adjective)* — found everywhere · **easy word:** everywhere, common
> *Smartphones have become ubiquitous in rural India.*

**Egregious** *(adjective)* — extremely bad, shockingly wrong · **easy word:** terrible
> *The audit revealed egregious misuse of public funds.*

**Quintessential** *(adjective)* — the most perfect example of something · **easy word:** perfect, typical
> *He was the quintessential public servant — honest and hardworking.*

**Tenuous** *(adjective)* — weak, not strong or certain · **easy word:** weak, thin
> *The evidence linking the two events is tenuous.*

**Ostensible** *(adjective)* — appearing true on the surface, but maybe not real · **easy word:** apparent, so-called
> *The ostensible reason for the visit was trade.*

**Inexorable** *(adjective)* — impossible to stop · **easy word:** unstoppable
> *The inexorable rise of automation is changing jobs.*

**Precarious** *(adjective)* — unsafe, likely to fail or fall · **easy word:** risky, shaky
> *Millions live in a precarious financial situation.*

**Inadvertent** *(adjective)* — done by mistake, not on purpose · **easy word:** accidental
> *An inadvertent error in the data delayed the results.*

**Pernicious** *(adjective)* — causing harm in a slow, hidden way · **easy word:** harmful
> *Corruption has a pernicious effect on public trust.*

**Innocuous** *(adjective)* — harmless · **easy word:** safe, harmless
> *What seemed an innocuous remark triggered a big row.*

**Nascent** *(adjective)* — just beginning to develop · **easy word:** new, young
> *India's nascent semiconductor industry needs support.*

**Myriad** *(adjective/noun)* — a very large number · **easy word:** countless, many
> *Farmers face a myriad of problems every season.*

**Exacerbate** *(verb)* — to make a bad thing worse · **easy word:** worsen
> *Poor planning exacerbated the flooding.*

**Undermine** *(verb)* — to weaken slowly · **easy word:** weaken
> *Fake news can undermine democracy.*

**Galvanise** *(verb)* — to shock people into action · **easy word:** stir, push into action
> *The tragedy galvanised the public to demand reforms.*

**Exonerate** *(verb)* — to officially declare not guilty · **easy word:** clear, free of blame
> *The court exonerated him of all charges.*

**Vindicate** *(verb)* — to prove someone was right after doubt · **easy word:** prove right
> *The findings vindicated the scientist's earlier warning.*

**Reconcile** *(verb)* — to make two opposing things agree / to make peace · **easy word:** settle, match
> *It is hard to reconcile fast growth with low pollution.*

**Capitulate** *(verb)* — to give up / surrender · **easy word:** surrender
> *The company refused to capitulate to the demands.*

**Obfuscate** *(verb)* — to deliberately make something unclear · **easy word:** confuse, hide
> *The reply only obfuscated the real cost of the project.*

**Acrimony** *(noun)* — bitter, angry feelings between people · **easy word:** bitterness
> *The talks ended in acrimony with no deal.*

**Impetus** *(noun)* — a force that drives something forward · **easy word:** push, drive
> *The reform gave a fresh impetus to growth.*

**Paradigm** *(noun)* — a typical model or pattern of doing something · **easy word:** model, pattern
> *Remote work has caused a paradigm shift in offices.*

**Predicament** *(noun)* — a difficult situation with no easy way out · **easy word:** trouble, fix
> *Migrant workers were left in a desperate predicament.*

**Quagmire** *(noun)* — a messy situation that is hard to escape · **easy word:** mess, trap
> *The legal quagmire has stalled the project for years.*

**Watershed** *(noun)* — a major turning point · **easy word:** turning point
> *The verdict was a watershed moment for women's rights.*

**Caveat** *(noun)* — a warning or condition to keep in mind · **easy word:** warning, condition
> *The report comes with one caveat: the data is incomplete.*

**Nuance** *(noun)* — a small, fine difference in meaning · **easy word:** subtle point
> *The debate ignored the nuances of the policy.*

**Rhetoric** *(noun)* — fancy speech meant to persuade (often empty) · **easy word:** big talk
> *Voters are tired of rhetoric and want real action.*

**Antithesis** *(noun)* — the exact opposite · **easy word:** opposite
> *His calm style was the antithesis of his rival's.*

## TIER 4 — HARDEST (the words that separate top readers)

**Inimical** *(adjective)* — harmful or hostile to something · **easy word:** harmful, against
> *Such policies are inimical to small traders.*

**Sycophant** *(noun)* — a person who flatters powerful people to gain favour · **easy word:** flatterer, yes-man
> *The leader was surrounded by sycophants, not advisers.*

**Demagogue** *(noun)* — a leader who wins support by stirring emotions, not facts · **easy word:** crowd-pleaser
> *Critics called him a demagogue who exploited fear.*

**Hegemony** *(noun)* — control or dominance of one group over others · **easy word:** dominance, control
> *The treaty challenged the region's old hegemony.*

**Panacea** *(noun)* — a cure for all problems (usually said it is NOT one) · **easy word:** cure-all
> *Technology is not a panacea for poverty.*

**Pyrrhic** *(adjective)* — a win that costs so much it feels like a loss · **easy word:** costly victory
> *It was a Pyrrhic victory — they won the case but lost everything.*

**Recalcitrant** *(adjective)* — refusing to obey, stubborn · **easy word:** stubborn, defiant
> *Recalcitrant states delayed the national reform.*

**Intransigent** *(adjective)* — refusing to change one's view · **easy word:** stubborn, rigid
> *Both sides remained intransigent, and talks failed.*

**Ephemeral** *(adjective)* — lasting a very short time · **easy word:** short-lived
> *Online fame is often ephemeral.*

**Insidious** *(adjective)* — harmful in a slow, hidden way · **easy word:** sneaky, harmful
> *Misinformation spreads in an insidious manner.*

**Specious** *(adjective)* — looks correct but is actually false · **easy word:** misleading
> *The argument was specious and fell apart under questioning.*

**Spurious** *(adjective)* — fake, not genuine · **easy word:** false, fake
> *The market was flooded with spurious medicines.*

**Draconian** *(adjective)* — extremely harsh and severe (laws) · **easy word:** very harsh
> *Activists called the new law draconian.*

**Sanguine** *(adjective)* — hopeful and positive even in difficulty · **easy word:** optimistic, hopeful
> *Economists remain sanguine about next year's growth.*

**Truculent** *(adjective)* — aggressive and eager to argue · **easy word:** hostile, fighting
> *The minister gave a truculent reply to the press.*

**Obsequious** *(adjective)* — too eager to please / overly obedient · **easy word:** servile
> *His obsequious manner annoyed his colleagues.*

**Ostentatious** *(adjective)* — showing off wealth to impress · **easy word:** showy, flashy
> *The ostentatious wedding drew public criticism.*

**Vituperative** *(adjective)* — full of bitter, abusive criticism · **easy word:** harshly critical
> *The campaign turned vituperative in its final week.*

**Pusillanimous** *(adjective)* — cowardly, lacking courage · **easy word:** cowardly
> *Opponents accused the government of a pusillanimous response.*

**Obdurate** *(adjective)* — stubbornly refusing to change · **easy word:** stubborn
> *The management stayed obdurate despite the strike.*

**Mendacious** *(adjective)* — lying, not telling the truth · **easy word:** dishonest, false
> *The report was dismissed as mendacious propaganda.*

**Perfunctory** *(adjective)* — done quickly without real care · **easy word:** careless, half-hearted
> *The inquiry was perfunctory and ignored key facts.*

**Quixotic** *(adjective)* — unrealistic and impractical in a hopeful way · **easy word:** unrealistic, dreamy
> *His quixotic plan to end all poverty won few backers.*

**Esoteric** *(adjective)* — understood by only a few experts · **easy word:** specialised, obscure
> *The debate turned into an esoteric discussion few followed.*

**Ineluctable** *(adjective)* — impossible to avoid · **easy word:** unavoidable
> *Reform became an ineluctable necessity.*

**Vicissitude** *(noun)* — a change of fortune, ups and downs · **easy word:** ups and downs
> *The industry has survived many vicissitudes.*

**Obloquy** *(noun)* — strong public criticism and shame · **easy word:** disgrace, blame
> *The scandal brought obloquy on the whole party.*

**Chicanery** *(noun)* — clever trickery to deceive · **easy word:** trickery, cheating
> *Voters were warned against electoral chicanery.*

**Sangfroid** *(noun)* — calmness under pressure · **easy word:** composure, cool head
> *She handled the crisis with remarkable sangfroid.*

**Schadenfreude** *(noun)* — pleasure at another person's trouble · **easy word:** gloating
> *There was open schadenfreude when the rival firm collapsed.*

**Brinkmanship** *(noun)* — pushing a dangerous situation to the edge to win · **easy word:** risky pressure tactics
> *The nuclear brinkmanship alarmed the world.*

**Détente** *(noun)* — easing of tension between rival nations · **easy word:** peace-thaw
> *The summit marked a rare détente between the two countries.*

**Quagmire** *(noun)* — see Tier 3; appears constantly in foreign-policy editorials.

## A note for you (please read)
You said even common "elite" words are hard right now — that is completely normal, and it is **not** a sign you can't do this. Every strong reader started exactly where you are. The difference is only practice over time.

**Your simple daily plan:**
- ☐ Learn **5 words** from this file
- ☐ Read **1 short editorial** (The Hindu or Indian Express op-ed page)
- ☐ Add **1–2 new words** you found to the "My Own Words" table above

Do this for 90 days and you will read newspapers more easily than most people around you. You can absolutely reach the top level. Keep going.
