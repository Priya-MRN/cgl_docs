# Newspaper Vocabulary — 1 Full Year (≈365 Words)

> Navigation: [English Master Bank](README.md) · [Newspaper Vocabulary (Starter)](10_Newspaper_Vocabulary.md) · [Newspaper Antonyms & Thesaurus](13_Newspaper_Antonyms_Thesaurus.md)

## What this is
A **full year of newspaper words** — 367 words taken from the editorial and op-ed pages of Indian English newspapers (**The Hindu, The Indian Express, Times of India, Hindustan Times, The Economic Times, The Telegraph, Business Standard, Mint, Deccan Herald**).

The words are split into **12 monthly sets**, graded **Easy (Month 1) → Hardest (Month 12)**. Learn about **one word a day** and in a year you will read these papers with ease. None of these repeat the words in [10_Newspaper_Vocabulary.md](10_Newspaper_Vocabulary.md).

## How to study (simple plan)
- **1 word a day.** Read the meaning, the easy synonyms, the sentence, and the memory trick out loud.
- At the **end of each month**, cover the meanings and test yourself on that month's ~30 words.
- Pair this with the **antonyms file** ([13_Newspaper_Antonyms_Thesaurus.md](13_Newspaper_Antonyms_Thesaurus.md)) to learn the opposite of each word too.

**Each entry is laid out as:**

**Word** *(part of speech)* — simple meaning · **easy synonyms:** ... · 🧠 **trick:** memory hook
> *Newspaper-style sentence.*

## MONTH 1 — EASY  *(words 1–31)*

**Abide** *(verb)* — to accept and follow a rule or decision · **easy synonyms:** follow, obey, accept · 🧠 **trick:** 'a-BIDE' sounds like 'a bride' who must abide by wedding vows.
> *The court made it clear that all parties must abide by its order, irrespective of their political differences.*

**Adhere** *(verb)* — to stick to or strictly follow a rule · **easy synonyms:** stick, follow, keep to · 🧠 **trick:** Adhesive tape 'adheres' (sticks); rules you stick to.
> *Banks have been told to adhere to the new lending norms issued by the Reserve Bank of India.*

**Affluent** *(adjective)* — having a lot of money; rich · **easy synonyms:** rich, wealthy, well-off · 🧠 **trick:** 'Afflu-ent' has 'flow' in it — money flows freely to the rich.
> *The new tax mainly affects affluent households living in the metro cities.*

**Alleviate** *(verb)* — to make a problem or pain less severe · **easy synonyms:** reduce, ease, lessen · 🧠 **trick:** 'A-LIEVE-iate' sounds like 'relieve' — both reduce pain.
> *The relief package is meant to alleviate the suffering of farmers hit by the floods.*

**Adverse** *(adjective)* — harmful or unfavourable · **easy synonyms:** bad, harmful, negative · 🧠 **trick:** 'Adverse' = against (ad-verse, turned against you).
> *Experts warned of the adverse effects of rising prices on poor families.*

**Advocate** *(verb)* — to publicly support an idea or cause · **easy synonyms:** support, back, recommend · 🧠 **trick:** A lawyer (advocate) speaks up in support of someone.
> *Several economists advocate greater spending on health and education.*

**Acute** *(adjective)* — very serious or severe · **easy synonyms:** severe, sharp, serious · 🧠 **trick:** An 'acute' angle is sharp; an acute problem is sharply serious.
> *The city is facing an acute shortage of drinking water this summer.*

**Ample** *(adjective)* — more than enough; plenty · **easy synonyms:** enough, plenty, large · 🧠 **trick:** 'Ample' sounds like 'a pile' — a big pile, plenty.
> *The government claims it has ample reserves of food grains to manage any crisis.*

**Avert** *(verb)* — to stop something bad from happening · **easy synonyms:** prevent, stop, avoid · 🧠 **trick:** 'A-VERT' — turn away (a-vert) danger before it hits.
> *Timely talks between the two sides helped avert a major strike.*

**Acclaim** *(noun)* — strong public praise · **easy synonyms:** praise, applause, honour · 🧠 **trick:** 'Ac-CLAIM' — people loudly claim how good it is.
> *The film won wide acclaim for its honest portrayal of rural life.*

**Amend** *(verb)* — to officially change or correct a law or text · **easy synonyms:** change, fix, correct · 🧠 **trick:** To 'a-mend' is to mend (repair) a law.
> *Parliament is expected to amend the law to close the loophole.*

**Abrupt** *(adjective)* — sudden and unexpected · **easy synonyms:** sudden, quick, sharp · 🧠 **trick:** 'Abrupt' sounds like 'a-rupt' (erupt) — sudden burst.
> *The minister's abrupt resignation took even his own party by surprise.*

**Breach** *(noun)* — the breaking of a rule, law, or promise · **easy synonyms:** break, violation, breaking · 🧠 **trick:** A 'breach' in a wall is a break; a breach of law breaks it.
> *The data leak was described as a serious breach of citizens' privacy.*

**Candid** *(adjective)* — honest and direct in speaking · **easy synonyms:** honest, frank, open · 🧠 **trick:** A 'candid' photo is unposed and honest, like candid talk.
> *In a candid interview, the chief minister admitted the policy had failed.*

**Coerce** *(verb)* — to force someone to do something by threat · **easy synonyms:** force, pressure, push · 🧠 **trick:** 'Co-ERCE' sounds like 'force' with pressure.
> *The report alleged that villagers were coerced into selling their land.*

**Deter** *(verb)* — to discourage someone from doing something · **easy synonyms:** discourage, stop, prevent · 🧠 **trick:** A 'deter-rent' deters crime — it scares people off.
> *Stricter penalties are meant to deter people from breaking traffic rules.*

**Diligent** *(adjective)* — hard-working and careful · **easy synonyms:** careful, hard-working, sincere · 🧠 **trick:** 'Diligent' students do their daily work with care.
> *Only diligent follow-up by officials ensured the project was finished on time.*

**Endeavour** *(noun)* — a serious effort to achieve something · **easy synonyms:** effort, attempt, try · 🧠 **trick:** 'En-DEAVOUR' — you pour your energy into the effort.
> *The campaign is a sincere endeavour to make villages free of open drains.*

**Feasible** *(adjective)* — possible and practical to do · **easy synonyms:** possible, doable, practical · 🧠 **trick:** If it can be done (fea-sible like 'do-able'), it's feasible.
> *Critics doubt whether the ambitious plan is financially feasible.*

**Grievance** *(noun)* — a complaint about unfair treatment · **easy synonyms:** complaint, problem, grudge · 🧠 **trick:** A 'grievance' makes you grieve — a sad complaint.
> *The protesters said the government had ignored their long-standing grievances.*

**Impede** *(verb)* — to slow down or block progress · **easy synonyms:** block, slow, stop · 🧠 **trick:** Something at your 'feet' (im-PEDE, pedal) blocks your path.
> *Poor roads continue to impede the growth of trade in the region.*

**Lenient** *(adjective)* — not strict; gentle in punishment · **easy synonyms:** soft, gentle, easy · 🧠 **trick:** 'Lenient' is like 'lean' — bending easily, going soft.
> *The opposition accused the agency of being lenient with powerful offenders.*

**Mediate** *(verb)* — to help two sides settle a dispute · **easy synonyms:** settle, help, intervene · 🧠 **trick:** A 'mediator' sits in the middle (medi-) to fix a fight.
> *The Centre offered to mediate between the two states over the river dispute.*

**Notable** *(adjective)* — important or worth noticing · **easy synonyms:** important, well-known, special · 🧠 **trick:** Something worth 'noting' is notable.
> *There has been a notable improvement in literacy rates over the decade.*

**Persist** *(verb)* — to continue firmly despite difficulty · **easy synonyms:** continue, last, remain · 🧠 **trick:** 'Per-SIST' — keep insisting, keep going.
> *Unemployment is likely to persist unless new industries are set up.*

**Prudent** *(adjective)* — sensible and careful in decisions · **easy synonyms:** wise, careful, sensible · 🧠 **trick:** A 'prudent' person is careful, like being 'pru-dent' (no dents/mistakes).
> *Analysts called it a prudent budget that avoided needless spending.*

**Rampant** *(adjective)* — spreading widely and out of control · **easy synonyms:** widespread, wild, uncontrolled · 🧠 **trick:** A 'ramp' going up fast — rampant spreads fast and wild.
> *Rampant corruption in the department has shaken public trust.*

**Scarce** *(adjective)* — very little available; in short supply · **easy synonyms:** rare, few, limited · 🧠 **trick:** 'Scarce' sounds like 'scare' — it's scary when things run out.
> *Jobs have become scarce for young graduates in small towns.*

**Tackle** *(verb)* — to deal with a difficult problem · **easy synonyms:** handle, face, deal with · 🧠 **trick:** In sports you 'tackle' an opponent; here you tackle a problem.
> *The state has launched a fresh drive to tackle child malnutrition.*

**Viable** *(adjective)* — able to work or succeed practically · **easy synonyms:** workable, possible, practical · 🧠 **trick:** 'Vi-able' has 'able' in it — able to live and work.
> *Solar power is now a viable alternative for many rural households.*

**Backlash** *(noun)* — a strong, angry reaction from many people against something · **easy synonyms:** anger, protest, reaction · 🧠 **trick:** BACK-lash = people lashing BACK at a decision.
> *The sudden hike in toll charges triggered a sharp backlash from commuters across the highway.*

## MONTH 2 — EASY  *(words 32–62)*

**Bleak** *(adjective)* — without hope; looking very bad for the future · **easy synonyms:** hopeless, dark, gloomy · 🧠 **trick:** Sounds like 'black' - a black, hopeless future.
> *With factories shutting down, the job outlook for young graduates looks increasingly bleak.*

**Brunt** *(noun)* — the worst part or main force of something bad · **easy synonyms:** worst part, full force, blow · 🧠 **trick:** BRUNT = you take the 'blunt' hit of the trouble.
> *Farmers in the region bore the brunt of the failed monsoon this year.*

**Backbone** *(noun)* — the most important supporting part of something · **easy synonyms:** support, base, main part · 🧠 **trick:** Your spine (backbone) holds your whole body up.
> *Small farmers remain the backbone of the country's rural economy.*

**Bolster** *(verb)* — to support and make something stronger · **easy synonyms:** support, strengthen, help · 🧠 **trick:** A 'bolster' pillow supports your back - it props you up.
> *New highways are expected to bolster trade between the two states.*

**Bid** *(noun)* — an attempt or effort to do something · **easy synonyms:** attempt, try, effort · 🧠 **trick:** A 'bid' in an auction = an attempt to win.
> *In a bid to reduce pollution, the city banned old diesel vehicles.*

**Blame** *(verb/noun)* — to say someone is responsible for something bad · **easy synonyms:** fault, accuse, hold responsible · 🧠 **trick:** BLAME = put the 'flame' of fault on someone.
> *Opposition leaders were quick to blame the government for the power cuts.*

**Clout** *(noun)* — power or influence to affect decisions · **easy synonyms:** power, influence, weight · 🧠 **trick:** CLOUT sounds like 'clout punch' - a powerful hit, real influence.
> *The trade body still carries enormous clout in shaping export policy.*

**Dent** *(noun/verb)* — to damage or weaken something a little · **easy synonyms:** damage, hurt, harm · 🧠 **trick:** A 'dent' in a car = small visible damage.
> *The corruption charges have made a serious dent in the party's image.*

**Dwindle** *(verb)* — to slowly become smaller in amount or number · **easy synonyms:** shrink, drop, fall · 🧠 **trick:** DWINDLE sounds like 'window' getting smaller and smaller.
> *As water levels dwindle, several villages now depend on tankers.*

**Erode** *(verb)* — to slowly weaken or destroy something over time · **easy synonyms:** weaken, wear away, reduce · 🧠 **trick:** Water 'erodes' a riverbank - slowly eats it away.
> *Repeated scandals have steadily eroded public trust in the institution.*

**Flaw** *(noun)* — a fault or weakness that makes something not perfect · **easy synonyms:** fault, weakness, defect · 🧠 **trick:** FLAW = a 'flaw' is a 'fault' (both start with f).
> *Experts pointed out a basic flaw in the design of the new policy.*

**Grim** *(adjective)* — very serious, worrying, and unpleasant · **easy synonyms:** serious, sad, harsh · 🧠 **trick:** GRIM = the 'Grim Reaper' - dark and serious.
> *The report paints a grim picture of rising unemployment in small towns.*

**Hinge** *(verb)* — to depend completely on something · **easy synonyms:** depend, rely, rest · 🧠 **trick:** A door HINGES on its hinge - it all depends on it.
> *The success of the scheme hinges on how quickly funds reach the districts.*

**Hurdle** *(noun)* — a problem or difficulty that must be solved · **easy synonyms:** problem, barrier, obstacle · 🧠 **trick:** In a race you jump over a HURDLE - an obstacle in your way.
> *Poor road connectivity remains the biggest hurdle to rural growth.*

**Loom** *(verb)* — to appear close and threatening; about to happen · **easy synonyms:** approach, threaten, near · 🧠 **trick:** A dark cloud LOOMS overhead, ready to rain trouble.
> *A severe water crisis looms over several cities this summer.*

**Lag** *(verb)* — to move or develop more slowly than others · **easy synonyms:** trail, fall behind, slow · 🧠 **trick:** A slow internet has 'lag' - it falls behind.
> *Rural schools continue to lag behind urban ones in basic facilities.*

**Plea** *(noun)* — an urgent and emotional request · **easy synonyms:** request, appeal, call · 🧠 **trick:** PLEA = you 'please' beg for something.
> *The court rejected the plea to delay the eviction of street vendors.*

**Pledge** *(verb/noun)* — to make a serious promise · **easy synonyms:** promise, vow, commit · 🧠 **trick:** You PLEDGE allegiance - a serious promise.
> *The minister pledged to double spending on public health this year.*

**Prompt** *(verb)* — to cause something to happen or make someone act · **easy synonyms:** cause, lead to, trigger · 🧠 **trick:** A 'prompt' on screen makes you act right away.
> *The factory fire prompted a fresh review of safety rules in the area.*

**Quell** *(verb)* — to stop or calm something, like protest or fear · **easy synonyms:** stop, calm, control · 🧠 **trick:** QUELL sounds like 'quiet' - you quiet it down.
> *Police were deployed to quell the violence that broke out near the market.*

**Setback** *(noun)* — a problem that delays or stops progress · **easy synonyms:** delay, problem, blow · 🧠 **trick:** SET-BACK = it sets you BACK, pushing you backwards.
> *The project suffered a major setback after the central funds were withheld.*

**Stall** *(verb)* — to stop or delay something on purpose or by problem · **easy synonyms:** delay, stop, hold up · 🧠 **trick:** A car 'stalls' - the engine stops dead.
> *The talks stalled after both sides refused to soften their demands.*

**Stem** *(verb)* — to stop or slow down something bad from spreading · **easy synonyms:** stop, check, control · 🧠 **trick:** You put your thumb to STEM a leak - stop the flow.
> *The new rules aim to stem the flow of black money in the market.*

**Thrive** *(verb)* — to grow well and become very successful · **easy synonyms:** grow, succeed, do well · 🧠 **trick:** THRIVE sounds like 'drive' - driving forward to success.
> *Small businesses began to thrive once the local market reopened.*

**Turmoil** *(noun)* — a state of great confusion and trouble · **easy synonyms:** chaos, trouble, unrest · 🧠 **trick:** TURMOIL = a 'turning, boiling' pot of confusion.
> *The sudden policy change threw the stock market into turmoil.*

**Wane** *(verb)* — to slowly become weaker or less · **easy synonyms:** fade, weaken, drop · 🧠 **trick:** The moon 'wanes' - it slowly shrinks away.
> *Public interest in the campaign began to wane after the elections ended.*

**Breed** *(verb)* — to cause something, usually something bad, to develop · **easy synonyms:** cause, create, produce · 🧠 **trick:** Problems 'breed' more problems, like animals breeding more.
> *Delays in justice only breed anger and distrust among ordinary citizens.*

**Culminate** *(verb)* — to end or reach a final result after a long process · **easy synonyms:** end, finish, result in · 🧠 **trick:** 'Culmin-' sounds like 'climb' to the 'mountain top' — the high final point.
> *Months of farmer protests culminated in a meeting between the union leaders and the agriculture minister.*

**Compel** *(verb)* — to force someone to do something · **easy synonyms:** force, push, make · 🧠 **trick:** 'Com-pel' = to 'propel' someone forward by force.
> *Rising fuel prices have compelled many middle-class families to cut back on travel.*

**Compelling** *(adjective)* — very convincing; making you want to pay attention · **easy synonyms:** strong, convincing, powerful · 🧠 **trick:** Something so strong it 'compels' (forces) you to agree.
> *The economist made a compelling case for raising taxes on the super-rich.*

**Cite** *(verb)* — to mention something as a reason or proof · **easy synonyms:** mention, quote, point to · 🧠 **trick:** 'Cite' sounds like 'sight' — you point your sight at the proof.
> *The court cited the right to privacy while striking down the surveillance order.*

## MONTH 3 — EASY  *(words 63–93)*

**Compliance** *(noun)* — the act of obeying a rule or law · **easy synonyms:** obeying, following, agreement · 🧠 **trick:** To 'comply' is to obey — 'compliance' is the obeying.
> *Many factories were shut down for non-compliance with the new pollution norms.*

**Credible** *(adjective)* — able to be believed or trusted · **easy synonyms:** believable, trusted, reliable · 🧠 **trick:** Shares the root with 'credit' — you give credit to what is believable.
> *Without credible evidence, the police could not file a charge sheet against the accused.*

**Concede** *(verb)* — to admit something is true or to give in · **easy synonyms:** admit, accept, give in · 🧠 **trick:** 'Con-cede' = to 'cede' (hand over) the argument and admit you were wrong.
> *The chief minister finally conceded that the relief work had been delayed.*

**Comprehensive** *(adjective)* — complete and covering everything · **easy synonyms:** complete, full, thorough · 🧠 **trick:** To 'comprehend' fully — a 'comprehensive' plan covers it all.
> *The committee called for a comprehensive review of the country's labour laws.*

**Cohesive** *(adjective)* — well united and working closely together · **easy synonyms:** united, joined, together · 🧠 **trick:** Like glue 'cohering' — a cohesive team sticks together.
> *A cohesive opposition could pose a real challenge to the ruling party in the next election.*

**Convene** *(verb)* — to call people together for a meeting · **easy synonyms:** gather, meet, call together · 🧠 **trick:** 'Con-vene' — people 'come' to a venue when you convene them.
> *The Speaker convened an emergency session of Parliament to discuss the flood crisis.*

**Contend** *(verb)* — to argue or claim that something is true; to compete · **easy synonyms:** argue, claim, compete · 🧠 **trick:** In a 'contest' you contend — you argue or fight for your side.
> *Critics contend that the new policy favours large corporations over small traders.*

**Converge** *(verb)* — to come together from different directions to one point · **easy synonyms:** meet, join, come together · 🧠 **trick:** Opposite of 'diverge' — lines 'con-verge' (come) into one point.
> *Thousands of protesters converged on the state capital to demand higher wages.*

**Diminish** *(verb)* — to become smaller or less · **easy synonyms:** shrink, lessen, reduce · 🧠 **trick:** 'Di-mini-sh' — it becomes 'mini', smaller and smaller.
> *The party's influence in the southern states has steadily diminished over the past decade.*

**Flagrant** *(adjective)* — clearly and openly bad; shocking · **easy synonyms:** obvious, shocking, open · 🧠 **trick:** 'Flag-rant' — so bad it waves a red flag for all to see.
> *The demolition was a flagrant violation of the court's stay order.*

**Deplore** *(verb)* — to strongly disapprove of and feel sad about · **easy synonyms:** condemn, hate, regret · 🧠 **trick:** 'De-plore' sounds like 'deplorable' — you cry over something terrible.
> *Editorials across the country deplored the attack on journalists covering the rally.*

**Hinder** *(verb)* — to make something harder or slow it down · **easy synonyms:** block, slow, delay · 🧠 **trick:** Your 'hind' (back) leg holds you back — to hinder is to hold back.
> *Poor road connectivity continues to hinder the growth of rural industries.*

**Foster** *(verb)* — to help something grow or develop · **easy synonyms:** encourage, support, grow · 🧠 **trick:** A 'foster' parent nurtures a child to grow — to foster is to nurture.
> *The new education policy aims to foster creativity rather than rote learning.*

**Divisive** *(adjective)* — causing people to split into hostile groups · **easy synonyms:** splitting, dividing, separating · 🧠 **trick:** From 'divide' — divisive politics divides people.
> *The leader was criticised for making divisive remarks ahead of the elections.*

**Coherent** *(adjective)* — clear, logical, and easy to understand · **easy synonyms:** clear, logical, sensible · 🧠 **trick:** Cousin of 'cohesive' — ideas that stick together logically are coherent.
> *The government has yet to present a coherent strategy to tackle rising unemployment.*

**Compromise** *(noun/verb)* — a deal where each side gives up something; or to weaken something · **easy synonyms:** deal, middle ground, weaken · 🧠 **trick:** 'Com-promise' — both sides make a promise to give a little.
> *Both parties must compromise if the long-running border talks are to succeed.*

**Deliberate** *(adjective/verb)* — done on purpose; or to think carefully before deciding · **easy synonyms:** planned, intended, discuss · 🧠 **trick:** 'De-liberate' — you 'free' the truth slowly by thinking it over carefully.
> *The committee will deliberate for two weeks before announcing its final decision.*

**Cumbersome** *(adjective)* — slow, heavy, and difficult to manage · **easy synonyms:** clumsy, heavy, difficult · 🧠 **trick:** 'Cumber-some' — a 'cucumber' that is too big to carry, slow and heavy.
> *Businesses complain that the cumbersome approval process discourages new investment.*

**Discord** *(noun)* — disagreement and lack of harmony · **easy synonyms:** conflict, fighting, quarrel · 🧠 **trick:** Opposite of a 'chord' in music — 'dis-cord' is a clashing, ugly sound.
> *Growing discord within the alliance threatens the stability of the state government.*

**Compatible** *(adjective)* — able to exist or work well together · **easy synonyms:** matching, suited, fitting · 🧠 **trick:** Like two phone chargers that fit — compatible things go together.
> *The reforms must be compatible with the country's wider development goals.*

**Culpable** *(adjective)* — deserving blame for a wrong · **easy synonyms:** guilty, responsible, at fault · 🧠 **trick:** 'Culp-' is the Latin root for 'fault' (as in 'mea culpa') — culpable means at fault.
> *The inquiry held three officials culpable for the bridge collapse.*

**Deficient** *(adjective)* — not having enough of something needed · **easy synonyms:** lacking, short, missing · 🧠 **trick:** Linked to 'deficit' — when something is deficient, an amount is missing.
> *Rural health centres remain deficient in both doctors and basic medicines.*

**Deteriorating** *(adjective)* — getting worse over time · **easy synonyms:** worsening, falling, weakening · 🧠 **trick:** DE-deteriorate = 'down' the stairs, things keep getting worse step by step.
> *The deteriorating air quality in Delhi has forced the government to shut schools for several days each winter.*

**Dire** *(adjective)* — very serious or terrible · **easy synonyms:** terrible, urgent, serious · 🧠 **trick:** DIRE rhymes with 'fire' — a dire situation is on fire and very serious.
> *The flood-hit districts face a dire shortage of clean drinking water and medicines.*

**Dissent** *(noun)* — strong disagreement with an official idea or decision · **easy synonyms:** protest, disagreement, objection · 🧠 **trick:** DIS-SENT = you 'sent back' a no; you disagree.
> *In a healthy democracy, the right to dissent must be protected rather than treated as a crime.*

**Deft** *(adjective)* — skilful and quick, done in a clever way · **easy synonyms:** skilled, clever, smart · 🧠 **trick:** DEFT sounds like 'daft' but is the opposite — daft is foolish, deft is clever.
> *The minister's deft handling of the crisis prevented panic from spreading in the markets.*

**Disparity** *(noun)* — a big and unfair difference between two things · **easy synonyms:** gap, difference, unfairness · 🧠 **trick:** DIS-PARITY = 'not parity'; parity means equal, so disparity is unequal.
> *The wide income disparity between urban and rural India remains one of the nation's deepest challenges.*

**Dwarf** *(verb)* — to make something else look small in comparison · **easy synonyms:** outsize, overshadow, beat · 🧠 **trick:** A giant standing next to a DWARF makes the dwarf look tiny — dwarfs it.
> *This year's defence budget dwarfs the amount set aside for public health and education combined.*

**Derail** *(verb)* — to spoil a plan or process so it cannot continue · **easy synonyms:** spoil, stop, ruin · 🧠 **trick:** A train going off the RAIL — DE-RAIL — the whole journey is ruined.
> *Repeated walkouts by the opposition threatened to derail the entire winter session of Parliament.*

**Defy** *(verb)* — to openly refuse to obey something · **easy synonyms:** disobey, resist, ignore · 🧠 **trick:** DEFY = 'de-fight' you; you fight back and refuse to obey.
> *Thousands of farmers continued to defy the ban and marched towards the capital.*

**Dampen** *(verb)* — to make a feeling or activity weaker · **easy synonyms:** reduce, weaken, lower · 🧠 **trick:** DAMPEN = make 'damp'; a damp cloth puts out the fire of enthusiasm.
> *High interest rates are likely to dampen demand in the housing sector this year.*

## MONTH 4 — MEDIUM  *(words 94–124)*

**Daunting** *(adjective)* — seeming very difficult and a bit frightening · **easy synonyms:** scary, hard, difficult · 🧠 **trick:** DAUNTING sounds like 'haunting' — a haunting, scary, huge challenge.
> *Rebuilding the cyclone-ravaged coastline is a daunting task that will take years and crores of rupees.*

**Dismal** *(adjective)* — very poor, gloomy and disappointing · **easy synonyms:** poor, sad, bad · 🧠 **trick:** DIS-MAL = 'mal' means bad (like malfunction); dismal is dreary and bad.
> *The dismal performance of the manufacturing sector dragged down the quarter's growth figures.*

**Dubious** *(adjective)* — doubtful, possibly not honest or true · **easy synonyms:** doubtful, shady, unsure · 🧠 **trick:** DUBIOUS = you 'doubt' it; it sounds fishy and untrustworthy.
> *The company's dubious accounting practices finally drew the attention of investigators.*

**Dwell** *(verb)* — to keep thinking or talking about something for a long time · **easy synonyms:** focus, linger, brood · 🧠 **trick:** DWELL = where you 'live'; to dwell on something is to live inside that thought.
> *Instead of dwelling on past failures, the new policy looks ahead to long-term reform.*

**Detrimental** *(adjective)* — causing harm or damage · **easy synonyms:** harmful, damaging, bad · 🧠 **trick:** DETRIMENTAL contains 'detriment' = harm; it does harm.
> *Experts warn that cutting research funding would be detrimental to the country's future progress.*

**Diffuse** *(verb)* — to spread something widely, or to calm a tense situation · **easy synonyms:** spread, calm, ease · 🧠 **trick:** DIF-FUSE = pull out the 'fuse' so the bomb of tension spreads out and calms.
> *Quick talks between the two states helped diffuse the tension over the river-water dispute.*

**Discern** *(verb)* — to notice or understand something clearly · **easy synonyms:** notice, see, judge · 🧠 **trick:** DIS-CERN sounds like 'this concern' — you clearly make out the concern.
> *It is hard to discern any real change in policy beyond the government's bold announcements.*

**Deplete** *(verb)* — to use up something until very little is left · **easy synonyms:** use up, drain, empty · 🧠 **trick:** DE-PLETE sounds like 'de-plenty' — take away the plenty until it's empty.
> *Years of over-extraction have depleted the city's once-rich groundwater reserves.*

**Disrupt** *(verb)* — to interrupt and stop the normal flow of something · **easy synonyms:** interrupt, break, stop · 🧠 **trick:** DIS-RUPT = 'rupt' means break (like rupture); it breaks up normal order.
> *Heavy rains disrupted rail and road traffic across the western coast for two full days.*
