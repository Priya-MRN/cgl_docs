# Error Detection — Solved Question Bank

> Navigation: [English Master Bank](README.md) · [English Grammar Cheat Sheet](../Cheat-Sheets/English_Grammar_Cheat_Sheet.md)

## Key Rules
- Check **subject–verb agreement** first (singular subject → singular verb).
- Watch **tense consistency**, **prepositions**, **articles** and **pronoun case**.
- A part may be error-free; option **(d) No error** is sometimes the correct answer.
- Read the whole sentence before locating the faulty segment.

## EASY

**Q1.** Spot the error.
(a) One of my friends  (b) are going  (c) to the market today  (d) No error
**Answer: (b) are going**
*Solution:* "One of my friends" is singular (the subject is "One"), so the verb must be "is going", not "are going".

**Q2.** Spot the error.
(a) She do not  (b) like  (c) cold weather  (d) No error
**Answer: (a) She do not**
*Solution:* The third-person singular "She" requires "does not", not "do not".

**Q3.** Spot the error.
(a) The boy  (b) along with his friends  (c) were playing  (d) No error
**Answer: (c) were playing**
*Solution:* The subject is "The boy" (singular); the phrase "along with his friends" does not change the number, so it should be "was playing".

**Q4.** Spot the error.
(a) He is  (b) more taller  (c) than his brother  (d) No error
**Answer: (b) more taller**
*Solution:* "Taller" is already comparative, so "more" is redundant. It should simply be "taller".

**Q5.** Spot the error.
(a) I have  (b) been working here  (c) since five years  (d) No error
**Answer: (c) since five years**
*Solution:* "Since" marks a point in time; for a period we use "for". It should be "for five years".

**Q6.** Spot the error.
(a) Each of the students  (b) have submitted  (c) their assignment  (d) No error
**Answer: (b) have submitted**
*Solution:* "Each" is singular, so the verb must be "has submitted", not "have submitted".

## MEDIUM

**Q1.** Spot the error.
(a) The teacher asked  (b) the students  (c) to not make noise  (d) No error
**Answer: (c) to not make noise**
*Solution:* The natural construction is "not to make noise"; splitting the infinitive as "to not make" is non-standard here.

**Q2.** Spot the error.
(a) Neither the manager  (b) nor the employees  (c) was present  (d) No error
**Answer: (c) was present**
*Solution:* With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject "employees" (plural), so it should be "were present".

**Q3.** Spot the error.
(a) He gave me  (b) an advice  (c) that proved useful  (d) No error
**Answer: (b) an advice**
*Solution:* "Advice" is an uncountable noun and cannot take "an". It should be "a piece of advice" or just "advice".

**Q4.** Spot the error.
(a) The committee  (b) have decided  (c) to postpone the meeting  (d) No error
**Answer: (b) have decided**
*Solution:* "Committee" acting as a single unit is singular, so "has decided" is correct.

**Q5.** Spot the error.
(a) Hardly had he  (b) entered the room  (c) than the lights went off  (d) No error
**Answer: (c) than the lights went off**
*Solution:* "Hardly...when" is the correct pairing, not "hardly...than". It should be "when the lights went off".

**Q6.** Spot the error.
(a) Both the sisters  (b) are good  (c) in mathematics  (d) No error
**Answer: (c) in mathematics**
*Solution:* The correct preposition after "good" for a subject of skill is "at", so it should be "good at mathematics".

**Q7.** Spot the error.
(a) She is senior  (b) than me  (c) by two years  (d) No error
**Answer: (b) than me**
*Solution:* "Senior" is followed by "to", not "than". It should be "senior to me".

## HARD

**Q1.** Spot the error.
(a) No sooner did  (b) the bell ring  (c) when the children rushed out  (d) No error
**Answer: (c) when the children rushed out**
*Solution:* "No sooner" pairs with "than", not "when". It should be "than the children rushed out".

**Q2.** Spot the error.
(a) The number of  (b) applicants  (c) have increased this year  (d) No error
**Answer: (c) have increased this year**
*Solution:* "The number of" takes a singular verb, so it should be "has increased". (Compare "a number of", which is plural.)

**Q3.** Spot the error.
(a) If I were  (b) you, I would  (c) have accepted the offer  (d) No error
**Answer: (d) No error**
*Solution:* "If I were you, I would have accepted" is grammatically correct in the conditional. No part needs correction.

**Q4.** Spot the error.
(a) He is one of  (b) those students who  (c) always works hard  (d) No error
**Answer: (c) always works hard**
*Solution:* In "one of those students who...", the verb agrees with the plural "students", so it should be "work hard".

**Q5.** Spot the error.
(a) Scarcely had I  (b) reached the station  (c) than the train left  (d) No error
**Answer: (c) than the train left**
*Solution:* "Scarcely" pairs with "when", not "than". It should be "when the train left".

## SSC PYQ PATTERN
*(SSC CGL pattern)*

**Q1.** Identify the segment that contains the grammatical error.
(a) Many people  (b) prefers tea  (c) to coffee  (d) No error
**Answer: (b) prefers tea**
*Solution:* "Many people" is plural, so the verb should be "prefer", not "prefers".

**Q2.** Identify the segment that contains the grammatical error.
(a) He has been  (b) ill  (c) since last Monday  (d) No error
**Answer: (d) No error**
*Solution:* "Since last Monday" correctly marks a point in time with the present perfect "has been". The sentence is correct.

**Q3.** Identify the segment that contains the grammatical error.
(a) The jury  (b) was divided  (c) in their opinion  (d) No error
**Answer: (b) was divided**
*Solution:* When members act individually (divided in opinion), "jury" takes a plural verb, so it should be "were divided".

**Q4.** Identify the segment that contains the grammatical error.
(a) She prefers  (b) reading  (c) than watching television  (d) No error
**Answer: (c) than watching television**
*Solution:* "Prefer" is followed by "to", not "than". It should be "to watching television".

**Q5.** Identify the segment that contains the grammatical error.
(a) Neither of  (b) the two answers  (c) are correct  (d) No error
**Answer: (c) are correct**
*Solution:* "Neither" is singular, so the verb should be "is correct", not "are correct".

### 🔑 Memory Techniques
- Memorise the **correlative pairs**: no sooner...than, hardly/scarcely...when, neither...nor.
- For agreement, **ignore the phrase between commas** and match the verb to the real subject.
- Keep a list of **preposition partners**: senior to, prefer to, good at, married to.
- Treat **uncountable nouns** (advice, information, furniture) as never taking "a/an" or plural -s.
