🚀 Newton's Laws & Force ↑ top

Law / conceptStatement / formula
1st Law (Inertia)A body stays at rest / uniform motion unless acted on by an external force. Defines inertia.
2nd LawF = m·a; force = rate of change of momentum. Gives the unit newton.
3rd LawEvery action has an equal & opposite reaction. (Rocket propulsion, gun recoil.)
Conservation of momentumTotal momentum before = after (no external force). Basis of rockets.
Frictionf = μN; opposes relative motion. Static > kinetic.
Centripetal forceF = mv²/r; directed to centre (circular motion).

🌍 Gravitation ↑ top

ConceptFormula / fact
Newton's law of gravitationF = G·m₁m₂/r²; G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²
Acceleration due to gravity (g)≈ 9.8 m/s²; max at poles, min at equator; zero at centre of Earth
WeightW = m·g; mass is constant, weight varies with g
Escape velocity (Earth)≈ 11.2 km/s; v = √(2gR)
Kepler's lawsorbits are ellipses; equal areas in equal time; T² ∝ r³

💧 Properties of Matter & Fluids ↑ top

ConceptStatement / formula
Densityρ = mass/volume
Archimedes' principleUpthrust = weight of fluid displaced. (Floating, ships, hydrometer.)
Pascal's lawPressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally. (Hydraulic lift, brakes.)
Bernoulli's principleFaster fluid → lower pressure. (Aeroplane lift, spin of a ball.)
Surface tensionliquid surface acts like a stretched membrane (droplets, capillary rise)
Viscosityinternal friction of a fluid; honey > water
Hooke's lawstress ∝ strain (within elastic limit)

🔥 Heat & Thermodynamics ↑ top

ConceptFact / formula
Heat (Q)Q = m·c·ΔT; c = specific heat. Unit: joule / calorie.
Temperature scalesCelsius, Fahrenheit, Kelvin; °F = (9/5)°C + 32; K = °C + 273.15
Latent heatheat for phase change at constant temperature (fusion / vaporisation)
Modes of heat transferConduction (solids), Convection (fluids), Radiation (no medium)
1st law of thermodynamicsenergy conservation: ΔU = Q − W
Anomalous expansion of waterwater is densest at 4 °C (lets fish survive under ice)

🔊 Sound & Waves ↑ top

ConceptFact / formula
Wave equationv = f·λ (speed = frequency × wavelength)
Speed of sound (air)≈ 343 m/s (20 °C); faster in solids > liquids > gases; cannot travel in vacuum
Audible range (human)20 Hz – 20,000 Hz. Below = infrasonic, above = ultrasonic.
Echoreflected sound; needs ≥ 17 m distance (0.1 s gap)
Doppler effectchange in pitch due to relative motion (siren passing by)
SONAR / ultrasounduses ultrasonic waves (depth-finding, medical scans)

ConceptFormula / law
