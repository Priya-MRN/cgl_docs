# Geography — Quick Notes & MCQs

## Quick Notes

**India — Location & Extent**
- Latitudinal/longitudinal extent: 8°4'N–37°6'N and 68°7'E–97°25'E.
- Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N) passes through 8 Indian states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram.
- Standard Meridian: 82°30'E (passing through Mirzapur, near Allahabad/Prayagraj), IST = UTC+5:30.
- Southernmost point of mainland: Kanyakumari; of Indian territory: Indira Point (Great Nicobar).
- India is the 7th largest country by area; shares longest border with Bangladesh, then China.

**Physical Divisions**
1. The Northern (Himalayan) Mountains; 2. The Northern Plains (Indo-Gangetic); 3. The Peninsular Plateau; 4. The Indian Desert (Thar); 5. The Coastal Plains; 6. The Islands.

**Mountains**
- The Himalayas (young fold mountains) run in three parallel ranges: Greater Himalayas (Himadri — highest, contains Everest, Kanchenjunga), Lesser Himalayas (Himachal — hill stations like Shimla, Mussoorie), and the Outer Himalayas (Shiwaliks).
- Highest peak in India: Kanchenjunga (8,586 m, Sikkim). World's highest: Mt. Everest (Sagarmatha/Chomolungma, 8,848.86 m, Nepal-China).
- Aravalli Range (Rajasthan) — oldest fold mountains in India; highest point Guru Shikhar (Mt. Abu).
- The Western Ghats (Sahyadri) are continuous and higher (highest peak Anamudi, Kerala — highest in South India); the Eastern Ghats are discontinuous.
- Himalayan passes: Nathu La & Jelep La (Sikkim), Zoji La (J&K/Ladakh), Rohtang & Shipki La (Himachal), Bomdi La (Arunachal).

**Rivers**
- Himalayan (perennial, snow-fed) vs Peninsular (rain-fed/seasonal).
- The Ganga: origin Gangotri glacier (as Bhagirathi); Alaknanda + Bhagirathi meet at Devprayag to form the Ganga; longest river in India; world's largest delta (Sundarbans, with Brahmaputra). Major tributaries: Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, Son.
- The Indus: origin near Mansarovar (Tibet); the Indus Water Treaty (1960) — eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India, western (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan.
- The Brahmaputra: called Tsangpo (Tibet), Dihang (Arunachal); causes floods in Assam.
- Peninsular rivers flowing east into the Bay of Bengal: Godavari ("Dakshin Ganga" — longest peninsular river), Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi.
- Peninsular rivers flowing west into the Arabian Sea (form estuaries, not deltas): Narmada (Amarkantak) and Tapi (Tapti) — both flow through rift valleys.

**Soils of India (ICAR)**
- Alluvial soil — most widespread & fertile; Indo-Gangetic plains; rich in potash, poor in nitrogen.
- Black soil (Regur) — Deccan trap; ideal for cotton; rich in lime, iron, magnesia.
- Red soil — due to iron oxide; eastern & southern Deccan.
- Laterite soil — high rainfall, leaching; supports tea, coffee, cashew.
- Arid/Desert soil — Rajasthan; saline. Mountain/Forest soil — Himalayan slopes.

**Climate**
- India has a tropical monsoon climate. Southwest Monsoon (June–September) brings most rainfall ("burst of monsoon"). Northeast (retreating) Monsoon (Oct–Dec) brings rain to the Tamil Nadu (Coromandel) coast.
- Mawsynram (Meghalaya) — highest rainfall in the world; near Cherrapunji.

**National Parks & Wildlife (key)**
- Jim Corbett (Uttarakhand) — India's first national park (1936); tigers.
- Kaziranga (Assam) — one-horned rhinoceros; UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- Gir (Gujarat) — Asiatic lions.
- Sundarbans (West Bengal) — Royal Bengal tiger, mangroves.
- Periyar (Kerala) — elephants & tigers. Ranthambore (Rajasthan) — tigers. Bandhavgarh & Kanha (MP). Manas (Assam). Keoladeo/Bharatpur (Rajasthan) — birds.
- Project Tiger launched 1973; Project Elephant 1992.

**World Geography Basics**
- Largest continent: Asia; largest ocean: Pacific; deepest point: Mariana Trench (Challenger Deep).
- Highest waterfall: Angel Falls (Venezuela). Longest river: Nile (some say Amazon); largest river by volume: Amazon.
- Largest desert: Antarctica (cold); largest hot desert: Sahara.
- Highest mountain: Everest. Largest mountain range: Andes (longest above water); Himalayas (highest).
- Major lines: Equator (0°), Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N), Tropic of Capricorn (23.5°S), Arctic & Antarctic Circles. Prime Meridian (0°) through Greenwich, UK. International Date Line ~180°.
- Largest country (area): Russia; most populous: India (2023 onwards). Smallest country: Vatican City.

## MCQs

**Q1.** The Standard Meridian of India is —
(a) 68°7'E  (b) 82°30'E  (c) 97°25'E  (d) 23°30'N
**Answer: (b) 82°30'E**

**Q2.** The highest mountain peak located entirely within India is —
(a) Mt. Everest  (b) K2  (c) Kanchenjunga  (d) Nanda Devi
**Answer: (c) Kanchenjunga**
*Note:* Nanda Devi is the highest peak entirely within Indian territory; Kanchenjunga lies on the India–Nepal border. SSC keys most often accept Kanchenjunga as the highest in India.

**Q3.** The oldest fold mountains in India are the —
(a) Himalayas  (b) Aravallis  (c) Western Ghats  (d) Vindhyas
**Answer: (b) Aravallis**

**Q4.** The Ganga is formed at Devprayag by the confluence of the Bhagirathi and —
(a) Yamuna  (b) Alaknanda  (c) Kosi  (d) Gandak
**Answer: (b) Alaknanda**

**Q5.** Which river is known as the "Dakshin Ganga"?
(a) Krishna  (b) Kaveri  (c) Godavari  (d) Narmada
**Answer: (c) Godavari**

**Q6.** The Narmada and Tapi rivers flow through —
(a) deltas  (b) rift valleys  (c) the Himalayas  (d) the Eastern Ghats
**Answer: (b) rift valleys**

**Q7.** Which soil is most suitable for cotton cultivation?
(a) Alluvial  (b) Red  (c) Black (Regur)  (d) Laterite
**Answer: (c) Black (Regur) soil**

**Q8.** Kaziranga National Park is famous for the —
(a) Asiatic lion  (b) one-horned rhinoceros  (c) snow leopard  (d) elephant
**Answer: (b) one-horned rhinoceros**

**Q9.** The Asiatic lion is found in which national park?
(a) Kaziranga  (b) Gir  (c) Corbett  (d) Sundarbans
**Answer: (b) Gir National Park (Gujarat)**

**Q10.** India's first national park is —
(a) Kanha  (b) Gir  (c) Jim Corbett  (d) Ranthambore
**Answer: (c) Jim Corbett (1936)**

**Q11.** The place receiving the highest rainfall in the world is —
(a) Cherrapunji  (b) Mawsynram  (c) Shillong  (d) Agumbe
**Answer: (b) Mawsynram (Meghalaya)**

**Q12.** Which is the longest river in India?
(a) Brahmaputra  (b) Godavari  (c) Ganga  (d) Yamuna
**Answer: (c) Ganga**

**Q13.** The highest peak of the Western Ghats is —
(a) Anamudi  (b) Doddabetta  (c) Guru Shikhar  (d) Mahendragiri
**Answer: (a) Anamudi (Kerala)**

**Q14.** The deepest point in the world's oceans is the —
(a) Sunda Trench  (b) Mariana Trench  (c) Puerto Rico Trench  (d) Java Trench
**Answer: (b) Mariana Trench**

**Q15.** The largest hot desert in the world is the —
(a) Thar  (b) Gobi  (c) Sahara  (d) Kalahari
**Answer: (c) Sahara**

## SSC PYQ Pattern

**Q1.** The Tropic of Cancer does NOT pass through which of the following states? (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) Gujarat  (b) Madhya Pradesh  (c) Bihar  (d) Tripura
**Answer: (c) Bihar**
*Note:* It passes through Jharkhand and West Bengal, but not Bihar.

**Q2.** The Indus Water Treaty (1960) allots which rivers to India? (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) Indus, Jhelum, Chenab  (b) Ravi, Beas, Sutlej  (c) Ganga, Yamuna, Kosi  (d) Narmada, Tapi, Mahi
**Answer: (b) Ravi, Beas, Sutlej (eastern rivers)**

**Q3.** Which soil is most widespread and fertile in India? (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) Black soil  (b) Red soil  (c) Alluvial soil  (d) Laterite soil
**Answer: (c) Alluvial soil**

**Q4.** Zoji La pass connects Srinagar to — (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) Leh (Ladakh)  (b) Shimla  (c) Gangtok  (d) Tawang
**Answer: (a) Leh (Ladakh)**

**Q5.** The southernmost point of India (Indira Point) is located in — (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) Kanyakumari  (b) Lakshadweep  (c) Great Nicobar  (d) Rameswaram
**Answer: (c) Great Nicobar**
