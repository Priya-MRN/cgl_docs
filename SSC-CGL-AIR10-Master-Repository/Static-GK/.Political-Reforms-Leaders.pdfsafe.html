⚡ 200 Units Free Electricity Scheme

FieldDetail
Effective10 May 2026 — order of the Energy (D3) Department
BenefitDomestic consumers using ≤ 500 units bimonthly → first 200 units FREE. Those using > 500 units → 100 units free (existing structure)
CliffExceeding 500 units by even one unit forfeits the 200-unit slab
Beneficiaries~2.23 crore of 2.46 crore domestic consumers (~90%)
ProcessNo application — auto-adjusted in bills by TNPDCL
MottoNone announced
⚠️ Most-likely-wrong fact on the internet. Telangana Today says the 200 units
go to those using more than 500 units; Oneindia says the threshold is 200,
not 500. Both contradict the majority of sources and are internally illogical (a free
slab rewarding higher consumption). Use the ≤500 rule.

5.5 Sheikh Abdullah — "Sher-e-Kashmir" (Lion of Kashmir)

Reform: 🌾 Big Landed Estates Abolition Act, 1950 — the "Land to the Tiller" Act

  • Ownership ceiling 22.75 acres (186 kanals)
  • Surplus redistributed to sharecroppers/landless — without ANY compensation to landlords
  • 1950–52: 700,000 landless peasants became proprietors (incl. 250,000 lower-caste

  • Possible only because the Indian Constitution's property provisions did not yet apply

in J&K (Article 370)

⚠️ Exam trap: J&K uniquely had a "Prime Minister" (not CM) until 1965 — Abdullah
held that title. Zero-compensation expropriation was the most radical land reform in
Indian history.

5.7 Jyoti Basu

FieldDetail
Full nameJyotirindra Basu (Jyoti Bose)
DesignationChief Minister, West Bengal; CPI(M)
State of birthCalcutta (Kolkata), Bengal Presidency
Born – Died8 Jul 1914 – 17 Jan 2010
Age at death95
Years in office21 Jun 1977 – 5 Nov 2000 — 23 YEARS

Reform: 🌾 OPERATION BARGA — launched JUNE 1978

  • Recorded the names of bargadars (sharecroppers) to shield them from

jotedars (landlords)

  • Legal backing: Bengal Land Holding Revenue Act 1979 + Revenue Rules 1980
  • Guaranteed crop share — 75% if the bargadar supplied inputs, 50% if the landlord

did; rights made hereditary; protection from eviction

  • Results: ~1.5 million of 2.2–2.5 million bargadars registered; registered share

rose 23% → 65% (1977–1990); farm output rose, rural poverty fell

Why it mattered: the most successful tenancy-recording reform in India — it
registered rights rather than seizing land. Basu was **India's longest-serving Chief
Minister (23 years) and famously declined the Prime Ministership in 1996** — his
party's refusal was later called a "historic blunder."
