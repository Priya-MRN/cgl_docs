## 1. Overview at a Glance

The **Indus Valley Civilization (IVC)** — also called the **Harappan Civilization** (after **Harappa**, the first site to be discovered) — was India's **first urban civilization** and one of the world's **earliest three civilizations**, alongside **Mesopotamia (Sumeria)** and **Egypt**.

- It is named "Harappan" because **Harappa** was the first site excavated (1921). Archaeologists name a culture after its first-discovered site, hence the official term **Harappan Civilization**.
- It belonged to the **Bronze Age** (people knew copper and bronze but **not iron**).
- It is also called the **Saraswati–Sindhu Civilization** because a large number of sites lie along the now-dry **Ghaggar–Hakra (ancient Saraswati)** river bed.

> **Highlight:** IVC was a **non-Aryan**, **urban**, **Bronze Age** civilization — these three words are the most common one-line description in exams.

## 2. Time Period (Chronology)

Dates differ slightly across sources; use these standard exam figures:

- **Overall span:** c. **2500–1700 BCE** (broad), some texts give c. 3300–1300 BCE for all phases.
- **Three phases:**
  - **Early Harappan:** c. **3300–2600 BCE** (formative/proto-urban).
  - **Mature Harappan:** c. **2600–1900 BCE** (the **urban, full-bloom** phase — Great Bath, town planning, seals).
  - **Late Harappan:** c. **1900–1300 BCE** (decline, de-urbanisation).
- **Dating method:** **Radiocarbon (C-14) dating** fixed the mature phase to **c. 2500–1750 BCE**.

> **Highlight:** If asked for "the period of IVC," the safe answer is **2500–1750 BCE**; for the **Mature/Urban phase**, answer **2600–1900 BCE**.

## 3. Discovery & Key Archaeologists

- **1921 — Harappa** discovered by **Daya Ram Sahni** (on the river **Ravi**, in Montgomery district, now Pakistan).
- **1922 — Mohenjodaro** ("Mound of the Dead") discovered by **R. D. Banerji** (on the river **Indus**, in Larkana district, Sindh, now Pakistan).
- The discoveries were announced by **Sir John Marshall** (then Director-General of the **Archaeological Survey of India, ASI**) in **1924**, formally bringing IVC to world attention.
- Other key excavators: **Madho Sarup Vats**, **Mortimer Wheeler**, **S. R. Rao** (Lothal), **B. B. Lal**, **R. S. Bisht** (Dholavira).

> **Highlight:** Remember the pair — **Harappa → Daya Ram Sahni (1921)** and **Mohenjodaro → R. D. Banerji (1922)**.

## 4. Geographical Extent

- IVC was **larger than ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia combined** — roughly **1.3 million sq. km**, spread in a **triangular shape**.
- **Boundaries (extent):**
  - **North:** **Manda** (Jammu & Kashmir, on the Chenab).
  - **South:** **Daimabad** (Maharashtra, on the Godavari/Pravara).
  - **East:** **Alamgirpur** (Uttar Pradesh, on the Hindon).
  - **West:** **Sutkagendor** (Balochistan, Pakistan, near the Makran coast / Pakistan–Iran border).
- It spread across present-day **Pakistan, and the Indian states of Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, J&K, and Maharashtra**.

> **Highlight (the four corners):** **Manda (N), Daimabad (S), Alamgirpur (E), Sutkagendor (W).**

## 5. Major Sites and What Each Is Famous For

| Site | River / Location | Famous For (Key Findings) |
|------|------------------|---------------------------|
| **Harappa** | Ravi, Punjab (Pakistan) | First site found; **6 granaries in a row**, workmen's quarters, **cemetery R-37**, stone dancing figures, coffin burial. |
| **Mohenjodaro** | Indus, Sindh (Pakistan) | **Great Bath**, **Great Granary** (largest building), **Bronze "Dancing Girl"**, **Pashupati seal**, bearded **Priest-King** statue, "Mound of the Dead". |
| **Lothal** | Bhogava, Gujarat | **Dockyard** (artificial harbour for **maritime trade**), rice husk, fire altars, bead-making, **terracotta ship**. |
| **Kalibangan** | Ghaggar, Rajasthan | **Ploughed agricultural field** (earliest known), **fire altars**, camel bones, "Black Bangles". |
| **Dholavira** | Khadir Bet, Gujarat | **Advanced water management** (reservoirs, dams), unique **three-part city division**, a **signboard** (10 large symbols); **UNESCO World Heritage Site (2021)**. |
| **Rakhigarhi** | Ghaggar, Haryana | **Largest IVC site in India** (largest overall in some recent claims); DNA studies. |
| **Banawali** | Saraswati bed, Haryana | Both pre-Harappan & Harappan; toy plough, good-quality barley. |
| **Chanhudaro** | Sindh (Pakistan) | **Bead-making factory**; **only site without a citadel**; lipstick, dog-paw on brick. |
| **Surkotada** | Gujarat (Kutch) | **First actual bones of a horse**. |
| **Ropar / Rupnagar** | Sutlej, Punjab (India) | First site excavated **after Independence**; dog buried with human. |

> **Highlight (must-memorise pairs):**
> - **Great Bath → Mohenjodaro**
> - **Dockyard → Lothal**
> - **Ploughed field & fire altars → Kalibangan**
> - **Water management → Dholavira**
> - **Largest Indian site → Rakhigarhi**
> - **Horse bones → Surkotada**

## 6. Town Planning & Architecture (The Hallmark)

This is the **most distinctive feature** of IVC and a favourite exam topic.

- **Grid system:** Cities laid out in a **rectangular grid pattern** — roads cut each other at **right angles (90°)**, dividing the city into blocks.
- **Two-part city:** A raised **Citadel** (western, higher mound — for rulers/public buildings) and a **Lower Town** (eastern — for common people). **Dholavira** uniquely had **three** divisions.
- **Burnt bricks:** Houses built of **standardised, kiln-baked (burnt) bricks** in a fixed **ratio of 4:2:1** (length:breadth:height). *(Contemporary Egypt/Mesopotamia mostly used dried/mud bricks.)*
- **Drainage system:** A **world-class covered underground drainage** — every house drain connected to street drains, with **manholes** for cleaning. **The drainage system is considered the most remarkable feature of IVC.**
- **The Great Bath (Mohenjodaro):** A large public tank (~**12 m × 7 m, ~2.4 m deep**), made watertight with **gypsum mortar and bitumen**; likely used for **ritual bathing**.
- **The Great Granary:** Largest building at Mohenjodaro — for **storing grain** (state granary / taxation in kind).
- **Houses:** Built around a **central courtyard**, with **private wells and bathrooms**; windows usually opened to inner courtyards (for privacy).

> **Highlight:** **Grid roads at right angles + covered drainage + burnt bricks (4:2:1)** = the signature of Harappan town planning.

## 7. Economy

- **Agriculture (main occupation):** Grew **wheat, barley** (staples), peas, sesame, mustard, dates, and **cotton**.
  - **The Harappans were the first in the world to produce cotton** — the Greeks called it **"Sindon"** (after Sindh).
  - **Rice** evidence at **Lothal and Rangpur**.
- **Domesticated animals:** Cattle (humped bull), buffalo, sheep, goat, pig, elephant; **the horse was largely unknown / not central** (a major debate point — only doubtful evidence at Surkotada).
- **Trade & commerce:**
  - **Both internal and external (foreign) trade** flourished; trade was through **barter** (**no coins/money** were used).
  - **Trade with Mesopotamia (Sumer):** Mesopotamian texts mention **"Meluhha"** = the Indus region. Trade also with **Dilmun (Bahrain)** and **Magan (Oman)**.
  - **Lothal's dockyard** handled **sea-borne trade**.
  - **Weights and measures** were standardised in **multiples of 16** (16, 32, 64…).
- **Crafts:** Expert at **bead-making, pottery (red & black ware), bronze tools, seal-making, and jewellery.**

> **Highlight:** **First cotton growers in the world; trade with Mesopotamia (Meluhha); weights in multiples of 16; no metallic money — barter only.**

## 8. Religion & Beliefs

No temples have been found, but seals and figurines reveal their beliefs:

- **Mother Goddess** worship (Shakti / fertility cult) — many terracotta female figurines.
- **Pashupati Mahadeva (Proto-Shiva):** The famous **Pashupati seal** shows a three-faced male deity in a yogic posture surrounded by animals (elephant, tiger, rhino, buffalo) — interpreted as a **proto-Shiva**.
- **Nature worship:** **Pipal (sacred fig) tree**, the **humped bull (Nandi)**, and animals.
- **Worship of the Lingam (Phallus) and Yoni** — stone symbols found.
- They believed in **amulets / charms** (fear of ghosts and evil).
- **No definite evidence of a temple or organised priesthood-led mass religion.**

> **Highlight:** **Mother Goddess + Pashupati (proto-Shiva) + Pipal tree + Bull** are the four most-asked religious elements.

## 9. Art, Script & Seals

- **Script:**
  - **Pictographic (logo-syllabic)** — uses pictures/symbols, **NOT alphabetic**.
  - **Undeciphered to this day** (one of the biggest unsolved puzzles).
  - Written in **Boustrophedon style** — i.e., **right to left** in the first line, then left to right in the next, alternating.
- **Seals:**
  - Mostly **square**, made of **steatite (soft stone)**.
  - The **most famous is the "Pashupati Seal"**; the **"Unicorn" (bull) seal** is the most common motif.
  - Seals were likely used in **trade** (to stamp goods) and as **amulets**.
- **Sculpture & art:**
  - **Bronze "Dancing Girl"** (Mohenjodaro) — made by the **lost-wax (cire-perdue) technique**.
  - **Bearded "Priest-King"** statue (steatite, Mohenjodaro).
  - **Red sandstone torso** and grey stone **dancing figure** (Harappa).
  - **Terracotta figurines** (toys, carts, animals) and high-quality **Red-and-Black pottery**.

> **Highlight:** **Script = pictographic, undeciphered, boustrophedon (right-to-left).** **Dancing Girl = bronze, lost-wax technique.**

## 10. Society & Daily Life

- **Largely egalitarian / urban society** — no grand palaces or royal tombs (unlike Egypt's pyramids), suggesting a **merchant/trader-dominated** rather than warrior/king-dominated society.
- People wore **cotton and woollen clothes**, used **ornaments** (beads, bangles, necklaces) of gold, silver, copper, and semi-precious stones — worn by **both men and women**.
- **Games:** Dice, and a board game (early chess-like).
- **Burial practices** (varied by site):
  - **Complete burial** (most common, at Harappa cemetery R-37).
  - **Fractional burial** (bones after exposure).
  - **Post-cremation / urn burials**.

## 11. Decline of the Civilization (Theories)

There is **no single agreed cause**; multiple theories are offered:

- **Climate change / aridity** and **drying up of the Saraswati (Ghaggar–Hakra) river** — a widely accepted modern view.
- **Recurrent floods** (esp. Mohenjodaro) and **changing river courses**.
- **Aryan invasion/migration** (proposed by **Mortimer Wheeler** — "massacre" evidence at Mohenjodaro) — now **largely discredited / less accepted**.
- **Ecological degradation** — deforestation, over-grazing, soil exhaustion, decline in trade with Mesopotamia.
- **Earthquakes / tectonic shifts** and **epidemics**.

> **Highlight:** The most exam-favoured modern explanation is **climate change + drying of the Saraswati/Ghaggar river**, leading to **de-urbanisation** rather than a sudden end.
