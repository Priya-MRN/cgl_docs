# Q1. Number Series

## Question

Find the next term: 5, 11, 23, 47, 95, ?

### Options

- (a) 191
- (b) 190
- (c) 187
- (d) 193

---

## Concept Tested

Multiplicative series of the form ×2 + 1.

---

## Formula(s)

```text
Tₙ₊₁ = Tₙ × 2 + 1
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

```text
95 × 2 + 1 = 191
```

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: 5 × 2 + 1 = 11
Step 2: 11 × 2 + 1 = 23
Step 3: 23 × 2 + 1 = 47
Step 4: 47 × 2 + 1 = 95
Step 5: 95 × 2 + 1 = **191**

---

## Common Trap

Adding the differences (6, 12, 24…) and slipping by one at the last step.

---

## Similar Practice Question

3, 7, 15, 31, ? — Answer: **63** (×2 + 1).

---

## Revision Note

"Each term ≈ double the previous, +1."

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (a) 191**

# Q5. Cube Painting

## Question

A cube is painted red on two opposite faces, blue on two adjacent faces, and green on the remaining two. It is cut into 64 equal small cubes. How many small cubes have exactly one face painted and that face is red?

### Options

- (a) 8
- (b) 16
- (c) 4
- (d) 12

---

## Concept Tested

Single-face painted cubes restricted to specific (red) faces.

---

## Formula(s)

```text
Single-face cubes per face = (n − 2)²,  here n = 4
Red faces = 2 (opposite)
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

64 = 4³ → each face's inner block is 2×2 = 4 cubes; two red faces → 2 × 4 = 8.

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: 64 small cubes ⇒ 4 per edge (n = 4).
Step 2: On any one face, the cubes with exactly one painted face are the inner (n−2)² = 2² = 4 cubes.
Step 3: Red is on two opposite faces, and these inner cubes don't overlap → 4 + 4 = **8**.

---

## Common Trap

Counting only one red face (giving 4) or including edge cubes (which have 2 painted faces).

---

## Similar Practice Question

3×3×3 cube, one face red — single red-face cubes? — Answer: **1** ((3−2)²).

---

## Revision Note

"Single-face cubes = (n−2)² per face."

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (a) 8**

# Q6. Syllogism

## Question

Statements: Some pens are inks. All inks are colours. No colour is white.
Conclusions: I. Some pens are colours. II. No ink is white. III. Some pens are not white.

### Options

- (a) Only I and II
- (b) Only II and III
- (c) All I, II, III
- (d) Only I

---

## Concept Tested

Syllogism with a chain of definite links.

---

## Formula(s)

```text
A conclusion follows only if true in every valid Venn arrangement.
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

Follow the chain: pens → (some) inks → (all) colours → (none) white.

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: Some pens are inks, and all inks are colours → those pens are colours → **I follows**.
Step 2: All inks are colours and no colour is white → no ink is white → **II follows**.
Step 3: The pens that are inks are colours, and no colour is white → those pens are not white → **III follows**.
Step 4: All three follow.

---

## Common Trap

Doubting III; but the "some pens" that are inks are definitely colours, hence definitely not white.

---

## Similar Practice Question

Some A are B; All B are C → "Some A are C"? — Answer: **Follows**.

---

## Revision Note

"Some + All chains pass the 'some' through to the end."

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (c) All I, II, III**

# Q8. Faulty Clock (Gaining)

## Question

A clock gains 10 minutes every hour. If it is set correctly at 12:00 noon, what time will the faulty clock show when the true time is 3:00 PM the same day?

### Options

- (a) 3:30 PM
- (b) 3:20 PM
- (c) 3:25 PM
- (d) 3:15 PM

---

## Concept Tested

Fast clock — clock-time proportional to real-time.

---

## Formula(s)

```text
Faulty clock shows 70 min for every 60 real min.
Clock time = Real time × (70/60)
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

```text
3 hr × 70/60 = 3.5 hr = 3 h 30 min
```

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: In 60 real minutes the clock advances 70 minutes (gains 10).
Step 2: Real time elapsed = 3 hours = 180 min.
Step 3: Clock shows 180 × 70/60 = 210 min = 3 h 30 min.
Step 4: Faulty clock shows **3:30 PM**.

---

## Common Trap

Using 60/70 instead of 70/60 (a fast clock shows *more*, so multiply by 70/60).

---

## Similar Practice Question

Clock gains 5 min/hr; shows what when true time = 4 hr later? — Answer: **4 h 20 min**.

---

## Revision Note

"Fast clock → shows MORE → ×(60+gain)/60."

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (a) 3:30 PM**

# Q11. Direction & Displacement

## Question

A man walks 1 km East, then 1 km North-East, then 1 km North, then 1 km North-West, then 1 km West. His net displacement from start is approximately:

### Options

- (a) 1 km
- (b) √2 km
- (c) (1 + √2) km
- (d) 2 km

---

## Concept Tested

Vector addition of unit displacements.

---

## Formula(s)

```text
Net East  = Σ (cos of each bearing)
Net North = Σ (sin of each bearing)
Distance  = √(East² + North²)
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

By symmetry, the East (+1) and West (−1) cancel and the NE/NW horizontal parts cancel; only the northward parts add up.

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: East-west components: E(+1), NE(+1/√2), NW(−1/√2), W(−1) → sum = 0.
Step 2: North components: NE(1/√2) + N(1) + NW(1/√2) = 1 + 2/√2 = 1 + √2.
Step 3: Net displacement = √(0² + (1+√2)²) = **(1 + √2) km**.

---

## Common Trap

Adding all five kilometres linearly (= 5 km) or assuming everything cancels.

---

## Similar Practice Question

Walk N1, E1, S1, W1 → displacement? — Answer: **0 km**.

---

## Revision Note

"Resolve into E–W and N–S, then Pythagoras."

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (c) (1 + √2) km**

Which statement would the author most likely AGREE with?

### Options

- (a) Exhaustion is proof of accomplishment.
- (b) Motion always equals progress.
- (c) Deliberate idleness can enhance original thinking.
- (d) The busiest people are always the most effective.

---

## Concept Tested

RC — applying the author's viewpoint.

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: The author argues idleness fosters original thought ("guard their idleness most jealously").
Step 2: Options (a), (b), (d) are exactly the views the author *criticises*.
Step 3: Answer = **Deliberate idleness can enhance original thinking**.

---

## Common Trap

Choosing (a), (b) or (d) — these are the misconceptions the passage attacks.

---

## Similar Practice Question

If the author values rest, they'd agree that? — Answer: **Rest aids creativity**.

---

## Revision Note

"Pick the option matching the author's *thesis*, not the views attacked."

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (c) Deliberate idleness can enhance original thinking**
