## The Tier-I Pattern

SSC CGL Tier-I is a **single 60-minute objective paper of 100 questions for 200 marks**, split into four sections of 25 questions each:

| Section | Subject | Questions | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | General Intelligence & Reasoning | 25 | 50 |
| B | General Awareness | 25 | 50 |
| C | Quantitative Aptitude | 25 | 50 |
| D | English Comprehension | 25 | 50 |
| **Total** | | **100** | **200** |

- **Marking:** +2 for every correct answer.
- **Negative marking:** −0.5 for every wrong answer. Un-attempted questions score 0.
- **Time:** 60 minutes for all 100 questions (≈36 seconds per question on average). The clock does not stop between sections.

### Scoring formula

```
Score = (Correct × 2) − (Wrong × 0.5)
```

> Example: 70 correct, 15 wrong, 15 left → (70 × 2) − (15 × 0.5) = 140 − 7.5 = **132.5 / 200**.

## How to take a mock

1. **Sit in one unbroken 60-minute block.** No pauses, no phone, no notes. Use a single timer counting down from 60:00.
2. **Simulate the OMR sheet.** Write your answers as `1-(b) 2-(a) 3-(d) …` on a separate sheet *before* you ever look at the Answer Key. Do not mark on the question paper itself if you want to re-take it later.
3. **Move fast, flag freely.** If a question takes more than ~45 seconds, mark it for review and move on. Reasoning and English are usually faster scorers than Quant — don't let one DI set or one tough puzzle eat your clock.
4. **Decide your guessing rule in advance.** With −0.5 negative marking, a blind 1-in-4 guess is break-even at best (expected value = ¼·(+2) + ¾·(−0.5) = +0.125, but real blind guesses are worse). Only guess when you can eliminate at least one option.
5. **Stop dead at 60:00.** Whatever is un-attempted stays un-attempted. That is the real exam.

## How to analyse (this is where the marks come from)

1. **Score honestly** using the formula above.
2. **Mark every wrong answer AND every question you guessed** — even guesses you got right. A lucky guess is a future wrong answer.
3. **Maintain an error log.** For each mistake record: question, section, topic, *why* you missed it (concept gap / silly error / time pressure / misread), and the correct method.
4. **Compute accuracy** separately from score:

   ```
   Accuracy = Correct ÷ Attempted × 100
   ```

   Aim for **≥ 90% accuracy**. High accuracy on fewer attempts beats low accuracy on many — negative marking punishes recklessness.
5. **Re-work every wrong Quant/Reasoning question by hand** until the method is automatic. Read every Solutions & Explanations note, not just the ones you missed.
6. **Log weak areas in the tracker below** and feed them back into your topic revision.
