# Q1. Number Series

## Question

Find the next term: 3, 7, 16, 35, 74, ?

### Options

- (a) 149
- (b) 153
- (c) 151
- (d) 157

---

## Concept Tested

Multiplicative series with an increasing additive constant (×2 + n).

---

## Formula(s)

```text
Tₙ₊₁ = Tₙ × 2 + k,  where k = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 …
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

Double each term, then add a running counter 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.

```text
74 × 2 + 5 = 153
```

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: 3 × 2 + 1 = 7
Step 2: 7 × 2 + 2 = 16
Step 3: 16 × 2 + 3 = 35
Step 4: 35 × 2 + 4 = 74
Step 5: 74 × 2 + 5 = **153**

---

## Common Trap

Adding a constant (e.g. always +1) instead of an increasing counter, giving 149.

---

## Similar Practice Question

Next term of 2, 5, 12, 27, 58, ? — Answer: **121** (×2 + 1, 2, 3…).

---

## Revision Note

"Double + count up" → ×2 +1, +2, +3…

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (b) 153**

# Q3. Number Analogy

## Question

Number analogy: 6 : 42 :: 8 : ?

### Options

- (a) 64
- (b) 72
- (c) 56
- (d) 80

---

## Concept Tested

Relationship n → n(n+1) (product of a number and its successor).

---

## Formula(s)

```text
n  →  n × (n + 1)
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

6 × 7 = 42, so apply the same to 8: 8 × 9.

```text
8 × 9 = 72
```

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: Test rule on first pair: 6 × (6 + 1) = 6 × 7 = 42 ✓
Step 2: Apply to 8: 8 × (8 + 1) = 8 × 9 = **72**

---

## Common Trap

Reading 42 as 6² + 6 is fine, but applying n² (= 64) instead of n(n+1) gives the wrong option (a).

---

## Similar Practice Question

5 : 30 :: 9 : ? — Answer: **90** (n(n+1)).

---

## Revision Note

n(n+1) = n² + n → "square plus the number itself".

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (b) 72**

# Q5. Direction Sense

## Question

A man starts at point X, walks 10 m North, turns right and walks 8 m, turns right and walks 4 m, then turns left and walks 6 m. In which direction is he now facing and how far (straight line) is he from X?

### Options

- (a) East, 14 m
- (b) East, √(14²+6²) m
- (c) South, 10 m
- (d) East, √(14²+6²) m facing East

---

## Concept Tested

Direction tracking + net displacement using the Pythagoras theorem.

---

## Formula(s)

```text
Net East  = ΣEast − ΣWest
Net North = ΣNorth − ΣSouth
Distance  = √(East² + North²)
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

Track only net E–W and N–S, then apply √(x² + y²).

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: North 10 m → (E 0, N 10).
Step 2: Right from North = East, 8 m → (E 8, N 10).
Step 3: Right from East = South, 4 m → (E 8, N 6).
Step 4: Left from South = East, 6 m → (E 14, N 6); now facing **East**.
Step 5: Straight-line distance = √(14² + 6²) m.

(Option (b) and (d) both state this; (d) adds the facing direction "East", making it the fully correct, most complete choice.)

---

## Common Trap

Adding distances linearly (14 + 6 = 20) instead of using Pythagoras; or stopping at (a) 14 m ignoring the 6 m offset.

---

## Similar Practice Question

Walk N 3, E 4 from start: distance? — Answer: **5 m**.

---

## Revision Note

"Right of North = East; Right of East = South." Rotate clockwise.

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (d) East, √(14²+6²) m facing East**

# Q7. Syllogism

## Question

Statements: All keys are locks. Some locks are doors. No door is a window.
Conclusions: I. Some keys are doors. II. Some locks are windows. III. No key is a window.

### Options

- (a) Only I follows
- (b) Only II follows
- (c) None follows
- (d) Only III follows

---

## Concept Tested

Syllogism — testing what *necessarily* follows, not what *could* be true.

---

## Formula(s)

```text
A conclusion follows only if it is true in EVERY possible Venn arrangement.
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

Draw the least-overlapping (worst-case) Venn and test each conclusion.

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: Conclusion I — "Some keys are doors": the locks that are doors need not be keys, so not necessary. ✗
Step 2: Conclusion II — "Some locks are windows": no link forces locks to meet windows. ✗
Step 3: Conclusion III — "No key is a window": keys are locks, but some locks could still be windows, so a key *might* be a window. Not necessary. ✗
Step 4: None of I, II, III necessarily follows.

---

## Common Trap

Assuming "Some locks are doors" makes "some keys are doors" — the door-locks may sit outside the key region.

---

## Similar Practice Question

All A are B; Some B are C. Does "Some A are C" follow? — Answer: **No**.

---

## Revision Note

"Some" never forces overlap with a *third* set. Test the worst case.

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (c) None follows**

# Q8. Cube Painting

## Question

A cube of side 5 cm is painted on all faces and cut into 1 cm cubes. How many small cubes have exactly two faces painted?

### Options

- (a) 27
- (b) 36
- (c) 54
- (d) 8

---

## Concept Tested

Painted-cube counting — two-face cubes lie on edges (not corners).

---

## Formula(s)

```text
Exactly 2 faces painted = 12 × (n − 2),  where n = cubes per edge
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

n = 5 → 12 × (5 − 2) = 12 × 3.

```text
12 × 3 = 36
```

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: A cube has 12 edges.
Step 2: Each edge has (n − 2) cubes between its two corners = 5 − 2 = 3.
Step 3: Two-face cubes = 12 × 3 = **36**.

---

## Common Trap

Confusing edge count with corner count (8 corners → three faces) and answering 8.

---

## Similar Practice Question

For a 4 cm cube cut into 1 cm cubes, two-face cubes? — Answer: **24** (12 × 2).

---

## Revision Note

1 face = 6(n−2)²; 2 faces = 12(n−2); 3 faces = 8 (always).

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (b) 36**

# Q10. Symbol Substitution (BODMAS)

## Question

If `÷` means `+`, `×` means `−`, `+` means `×`, `−` means `÷`, find the value of: 18 − 3 + 5 × 2 ÷ 4

### Options

- (a) 32
- (b) 33
- (c) 28
- (d) 30

---

## Concept Tested

Operator replacement followed by strict BODMAS.

---

## Formula(s)

```text
− → ÷,  + → ×,  × → −,  ÷ → +
Apply BODMAS after substitution.
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

Rewrite first, then solve: 18 ÷ 3 × 5 − 2 + 4.

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: Substitute: 18 ÷ 3 × 5 − 2 + 4.
Step 2: Division/Multiplication (left→right): 18 ÷ 3 = 6; 6 × 5 = 30.
Step 3: Then 30 − 2 + 4 = **32**.

---

## Common Trap

Solving in the original order without applying BODMAS to the substituted symbols.

---

## Similar Practice Question

Same code: 12 ÷ 4 × 3 ? Substitute and solve 12 + 4 − 3 = **13**.

---

## Revision Note

Substitute → then BODMAS. Never mix the two steps.

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (a) 32**

# Q13. Venn Diagram (Logical Sets)

## Question

Which Venn diagram best represents: Doctors, Women, Mothers?

### Options

- (a) Three mutually intersecting circles
- (b) Women and Mothers concentric, Doctors intersecting both
- (c) Mothers inside Women; Doctors intersecting both
- (d) All three disjoint

## Concept Tested

Class relationships — subset vs overlap.

---

## Formula(s)

```text
Every Mother is a Woman → Mothers ⊂ Women.
Doctors may be male or female → Doctors overlaps both.
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

Ask: "Is every X a Y?" If yes → one inside the other.

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: Every mother is a woman → Mothers is a circle *inside* Women.
Step 2: A doctor can be a man or a woman, and a mother can be a doctor → Doctors intersects both Women and Mothers.
Step 3: This matches option (c).

Choosing (a) three equal overlapping circles — wrong, because Mothers is fully within Women.

---

## Revision Note

"All A are B" → A inside B; "some" → overlap.

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (c) Mothers inside Women; Doctors intersecting both**

# Q14. Faulty Clock

## Question

A clock loses 5 minutes every hour. It was set correctly at 12:00 noon. What is the true time when the faulty clock shows 6:00 PM the same day?

### Options

- (a) 6:32 PM (approx)
- (b) 6:30 PM
- (c) 6:33 PM (approx)
- (d) 6:27 PM

---

## Concept Tested

Faulty (slow) clock — proportion of clock-time to real-time.

---

## Formula(s)

```text
Faulty clock shows 55 min for every 60 real min.
Real time = Clock time × (60 / 55)
```

---

## SSC/TNPSC Shortcut

```text
Real = 360 min × 60/55 ≈ 392.7 min ≈ 6 h 33 min
```

---

## Detailed Solution

Step 1: The clock shows only 55 real-equivalent minutes per hour (loses 5).
Step 2: From 12 to "6 PM" the faulty clock advances 360 clock-minutes.
Step 3: Real minutes = 360 × (60/55) = 392.7 min = 6 h 32.7 min.
Step 4: True time ≈ 12:00 + 6 h 33 m = **6:33 PM (approx)**.

---

## Common Trap

Using 55/60 instead of 60/55 (slow clock needs *more* real time, so multiply by 60/55).

---

## Similar Practice Question

A clock gains 4 min/hr; real time when it shows 8 hr? — Answer: ≈ **7 h 30 m** (× 60/64).

---

## Revision Note

Slow clock → real time is MORE → multiply by 60/55.

---

## Final Answer

**✅ Final Answer: (c) 6:33 PM (approx)**

Direction between two points placed symmetrically from a common point.

```text
SW means equal South and West offsets; SE means equal South and East offsets.
```

Both A and C are the *same distance South* of B; A is West, C is East → C is due East of A.

# Q20. Ranking / Positions

Positions in a row before/after interchange.

After swap, P takes Q's seat → P is 13th from left and 11th from right.

A is 5th from left, after swap with B becomes 9th from left and B was 4th from right. Total? — Answer: **12**.

---

## Revision Note

---
