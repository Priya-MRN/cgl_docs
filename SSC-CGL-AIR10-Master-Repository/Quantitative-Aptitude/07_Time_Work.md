# Time & Work, Pipes & Cisterns — Solved Question Bank

> **Top-priority topic.** Time & Work (with Pipes & Cisterns) appears in almost every SSC CGL Tier-1 and Tier-2 paper. Master the **LCM method** and you can solve most questions in under 30 seconds.
> See the [section index](README.md) and the [Quant Formula Sheet](../Formula-Sheets/Quant_Formula_Sheet.md) for quick revision.

## Key Formulas

- **One day's work:** If A finishes a job in `n` days, A's 1-day work = `1/n`.
- **Two together:** If A takes `a` days and B takes `b` days, together they take `ab/(a+b)` days.
- **Men–Days–Hours (chain rule):** `M₁D₁H₁ / W₁ = M₂D₂H₂ / W₂` (M = men, D = days, H = hours/day, W = work done).
- **Efficiency:** efficiency ∝ `1/time`. More efficient = fewer days. If A is `x%` more efficient than B, then `Efficiency_A : Efficiency_B = (100+x) : 100` and `Time_A : Time_B = 100 : (100+x)`.
- **LCM Method (use this everywhere):** Take **Total Work = LCM of the given days**. Then each person's **efficiency = Total Work ÷ his days** (units of work per day). Add efficiencies for combined work, then `Days = Total Work ÷ combined efficiency`.
- **Wages:** Wages are shared in the ratio of **work done**, i.e. in the ratio of **efficiency × days worked**.
- **Pipes & Cisterns:** An **inlet** pipe does **positive** work (fills), an **outlet/leak** does **negative** work (empties). **Net rate = 1/x − 1/y** (x = filling time, y = emptying time). With LCM, fill = `+`, empty = `−`.

## EASY

**Q1.** A can do a piece of work in 12 days and B in 18 days. Working together, in how many days will they finish it?
(a) 7 1/5 days  (b) 7 days  (c) 6 days  (d) 8 days
**Answer: (a) 7 1/5 days**
*Solution:* LCM(12, 18) = 36 = total work. Efficiency of A = 36/12 = 3, B = 36/18 = 2. Combined = 3 + 2 = 5 units/day. Days = 36/5 = 7 1/5 days.
🔑 *Trick:* Direct formula `ab/(a+b) = (12×18)/(12+18) = 216/30 = 7.2` days.

**Q2.** A is twice as good a workman as B and together they finish a job in 14 days. In how many days can A alone finish it?
(a) 18 days  (b) 21 days  (c) 24 days  (d) 20 days
**Answer: (b) 21 days**
*Solution:* A is twice as efficient as B, so efficiencies A : B = 2 : 1, combined = 3 units/day. Total work = 3 × 14 = 42 units. A alone = 42/2 = 21 days.
🔑 *Trick:* If A is twice as good as B, A alone takes `(combined time × 3)/2`... simpler: A does 2/3 of the work, so A alone = 14 × (3/2) = 21 days.

**Q3.** A can complete a work in 10 days, B in 15 days and C in 30 days. If they work together, how long will they take?
(a) 5 days  (b) 6 days  (c) 7 days  (d) 4 days
**Answer: (a) 5 days**
*Solution:* LCM(10, 15, 30) = 30 = total work. Efficiencies: A = 3, B = 2, C = 1 → combined = 6 units/day. Days = 30/6 = 5 days.

**Q4.** A does 1/4 of a work in 5 days and B does 1/3 of the same work in 8 days. Working together, how long will they take?
(a) 120/11 days  (b) 11 days  (c) 12 days  (d) 10 days
**Answer: (a) 120/11 days**
*Solution:* A does 1/4 in 5 days ⇒ full work in 20 days. B does 1/3 in 8 days ⇒ full work in 24 days. Together = `(20×24)/(20+24) = 480/44 = 120/11` days ≈ 10.9 days.

**Q5.** 12 men can complete a work in 8 days. How many days will 16 men take to do the same work?
(a) 6 days  (b) 5 days  (c) 7 days  (d) 4 days
**Answer: (a) 6 days**
*Solution:* M₁D₁ = M₂D₂ ⇒ 12 × 8 = 16 × D₂ ⇒ D₂ = 96/16 = 6 days.
🔑 *Trick:* Men and days are inversely proportional. Total man-days = 96 is constant.

**Q6.** A pipe can fill a tank in 6 hours and another in 4 hours. If both are opened together, in how long will the tank be full?
(a) 2 2/5 hours  (b) 3 hours  (c) 2 hours  (d) 2 1/2 hours
**Answer: (a) 2 2/5 hours**
*Solution:* LCM(6, 4) = 12 = tank capacity. Rates: +2 and +3 units/hour → net = 5 units/hour. Time = 12/5 = 2.4 = 2 2/5 hours.

## MEDIUM

**Q7.** A is 25% more efficient than B. If A can complete a work in 20 days, in how many days can B complete it?
(a) 25 days  (b) 24 days  (c) 28 days  (d) 16 days
**Answer: (a) 25 days**
*Solution:* Efficiency A : B = 125 : 100 = 5 : 4. Time is inverse, so Time A : B = 4 : 5. A = 20 days (4 parts) ⇒ 1 part = 5 ⇒ B = 25 days.

**Q8.** A and B together can do a work in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, and C and A in 20 days. In how many days can all three together finish it?
(a) 10 days  (b) 8 days  (c) 12 days  (d) 9 days
**Answer: (a) 10 days**
*Solution:* (A+B) = 1/12, (B+C) = 1/15, (C+A) = 1/20. Adding: 2(A+B+C) = 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = (5+4+3)/60 = 12/60 = 1/5. So (A+B+C) = 1/10 ⇒ 10 days.

**Q9.** In Q8, in how many days can A alone do the work?
(a) 30 days  (b) 60 days  (c) 20 days  (d) 24 days
**Answer: (a) 30 days**
*Solution:* A = (A+B+C) − (B+C) = 1/10 − 1/15 = (3−2)/30 = 1/30 ⇒ A alone = 30 days.

**Q10.** A can do a work in 24 days, B in 9 days and C in 12 days. B and C start the work and work for 3 days, then leave. In how many more days will A finish the remaining work?
(a) 10 days  (b) 12 days  (c) 9 days  (d) 8 days
**Answer: (a) 10 days**
*Solution:* LCM(24, 9, 12) = 72 = total work. Efficiencies: A = 3, B = 8, C = 6. B+C in 3 days = (8+6)×3 = 42 units. Remaining = 72 − 42 = 30 units. A's time = 30/3 = 10 days.

**Q11.** A and B can do a work in 30 days. They worked together for 20 days, then B left and A finished the rest in 20 more days. In how many days can A alone do the whole work?
(a) 60 days  (b) 50 days  (c) 48 days  (d) 75 days
**Answer: (a) 60 days**
*Solution:* In 20 days together they did 20/30 = 2/3 of the work. Remaining 1/3 was done by A in 20 days, so A does the whole work in 20 × 3 = 60 days.

**Q12.** A work is to be done by 3 men and 4 boys in 12 days. If 1 man does the work of 2 boys, in how many days will 7 men finish the same work?
(a) 60/7 days  (b) 10 days  (c) 9 days  (d) 8 days
**Answer: (a) 60/7 days**
*Solution:* 1 man = 2 boys, so 3 men + 4 boys = 6 boys + 4 boys = 10 boy-units. Total work = 10 × 12 = 120 boy-days. 7 men = 14 boy-units. Days = 120/14 = 60/7 ≈ 8.57 days.

**Q13.** A contractor employs 30 men to build a wall in 40 days. After 16 days he finds only 1/4 of the work is done. How many extra men must he employ to finish on time?
(a) 30 men  (b) 15 men  (c) 45 men  (d) 20 men
**Answer: (a) 30 men**
*Solution:* In 16 days, 30 men did 1/4 of work. Remaining 3/4 must be done in the remaining 40 − 16 = 24 days. Use M₁D₁/W₁ = M₂D₂/W₂: `(30×16)/(1/4) = (M×24)/(3/4)` ⇒ 1920 = (M×24)/(3/4) = 32M ⇒ M = 60 men needed. Extra = 60 − 30 = 30 men.

## HARD

**Q14.** A, B and C together earn ₹1,540 for a work. A and B together do 7/11 of the work. Find C's share.
(a) ₹560  (b) ₹450  (c) ₹600  (d) ₹400
**Answer: (a) ₹560**
*Solution:* Wages split in the ratio of work done. C does 1 − 7/11 = 4/11 of the work. C's share = (4/11) × 1540 = 4 × 140 = ₹560.
🔑 *Trick:* Wages ∝ work done, never per "number of days agreed."

**Q15.** A can do a work in 20 days and B in 30 days. They worked together and earned ₹600. Find each one's share.
(a) A ₹360, B ₹240  (b) A ₹300, B ₹300  (c) A ₹400, B ₹200  (d) A ₹240, B ₹360
**Answer: (a) A ₹360, B ₹240**
*Solution:* Efficiency ratio = (1/20) : (1/30) = 3 : 2. Total = 5 parts of ₹600 ⇒ 1 part = ₹120. A = 3 × 120 = ₹360, B = 2 × 120 = ₹240.

**Q16.** A and B undertake a work for ₹2,400. A alone can do it in 6 days, B alone in 8 days. With the help of C they finish it in 3 days. Find C's share.
(a) ₹300  (b) ₹400  (c) ₹600  (d) ₹500
**Answer: (a) ₹300**
*Solution:* LCM(6, 8) = 24 = total work. A = 4, B = 3 units/day. In 3 days A+B did (4+3)×3 = 21 units. C did 24 − 21 = 3 units. Ratio A : B : C = 12 : 9 : 3 = 4 : 3 : 1 (over 3 days). C's share = (1/8) × 2400 = ₹300.

**Q17.** 10 men can finish a piece of work in 15 days working 8 hours a day. How many days will 12 men take to finish a work three times as large, working 10 hours a day?
(a) 30 days  (b) 25 days  (c) 36 days  (d) 24 days
**Answer: (a) 30 days**
*Solution:* M₁D₁H₁/W₁ = M₂D₂H₂/W₂ ⇒ (10×15×8)/1 = (12×D×10)/3. So 1200 = (120D)/3 = 40D ⇒ D = 30 days.

**Q18.** A and B can do a work in 12 days, B and C in 8 days, A and C in 6 days. A and B work for 2 days, then B is replaced by C for the rest. In how many total days is the work completed?
(a) 7 days  (b) 6 5/7 days  (c) 6 days  (d) 8 days
**Answer: (a) 7 days**
*Solution:* (A+B)=1/12, (B+C)=1/8, (A+C)=1/6. LCM(12, 8, 6) = 24 = total work. Pair efficiencies: A+B = 2, B+C = 3, A+C = 4 units/day. Sum = 2(A+B+C) = 9 ⇒ A+B+C = 4.5 ⇒ A = 4.5 − 3 = 1.5, B = 4.5 − 4 = 0.5, C = 4.5 − 2 = 2.5 units/day.
A+B for 2 days = 2 × 2 = 4 units. Remaining = 24 − 4 = 20 units done by A+C = 4 units/day ⇒ 20/4 = 5 days. Total = 2 + 5 = 7 days.
🔑 *Trick:* Cross-check each pair against the LCM total before trusting your efficiencies (A+B=2 ✓, B+C=3 ✓, A+C=4 ✓).

## SSC PYQ PATTERN

**Q19.** *(SSC CGL pattern)* Two pipes A and B can fill a tank in 24 minutes and 32 minutes respectively. Both are opened together; after how many minutes should B be closed so the tank is full in 18 minutes total?
(a) 8 min  (b) 6 min  (c) 10 min  (d) 9 min
**Answer: (a) 8 min**
*Solution:* LCM(24, 32) = 96 = tank. A = 4, B = 3 units/min. A runs full 18 min = 4×18 = 72 units. B must supply 96 − 72 = 24 units at 3/min = 24/3 = 8 minutes. So close B after 8 minutes.

**Q20.** *(SSC CGL pattern)* A cistern is filled by a pipe in 10 hours, but a leak at the bottom empties the full cistern in 15 hours. If both work together, how long to fill the cistern?
(a) 30 hours  (b) 25 hours  (c) 20 hours  (d) 12 hours
**Answer: (a) 30 hours**
*Solution:* LCM(10, 15) = 30. Inlet = +3, leak = −2 ⇒ net = +1 unit/hour. Time = 30/1 = 30 hours.
🔑 *Trick:* Net rate = 1/10 − 1/15 = (3−2)/30 = 1/30 ⇒ 30 hours.

**Q21.** *(SSC CGL pattern)* A pipe can fill a tank in 12 hours. Due to a leak, it took 16 hours to fill. In how long will the leak alone empty the full tank?
(a) 48 hours  (b) 40 hours  (c) 36 hours  (d) 24 hours
**Answer: (a) 48 hours**
*Solution:* Effective rate with leak = 1/16. Leak rate = inlet − effective = 1/12 − 1/16 = (4−3)/48 = 1/48 ⇒ leak empties the full tank in 48 hours.

**Q22.** *(SSC CGL pattern)* 15 men can complete a work in 21 days. After 9 days, 6 more men join. In how many more days is the work finished?
(a) 60/7 days  (b) 6 days  (c) 5 days  (d) 7 days
**Answer: (a) 60/7 days**
*Solution:* Total work = 15 × 21 = 315 man-days. Done in 9 days = 15 × 9 = 135 man-days. Remaining = 315 − 135 = 180 man-days. Now 15 + 6 = 21 men. Days = 180/21 = 60/7 ≈ 8 4/7 days.
🔑 *Trick:* Keep everything in man-days; never mix "days remaining" with "men" until the final divide.

**Q23.** *(SSC CGL pattern)* Three pipes A, B, C can fill a tank in 6, 8 and 12 hours respectively. A and B are opened for 2 hours, then closed, and C fills the rest. How long does C take to finish?
(a) 5 hours  (b) 6 hours  (c) 8 hours  (d) 7 hours
**Answer: (a) 5 hours**
*Solution:* LCM(6, 8, 12) = 24 = tank. Rates: A = 4, B = 3, C = 2 units/hour. A+B in 2 hours = (4+3)×2 = 14 units. Remaining = 24 − 14 = 10 units. C's time = 10/2 = 5 hours.
🔑 *Trick:* Always finish by dividing the leftover units by the single remaining pipe's rate.

### 🔑 Exam Tricks Box

- **LCM method first.** Set Total Work = LCM of the given times. Efficiency = total ÷ days. Whole-number efficiencies kill fractions and save 20–30 seconds per question.
- **Efficiency is inverse of time.** "x% more efficient" ⇒ time ratio = `100 : (100+x)`. Convert to a clean ratio, then assign parts.
- **Pipes: sign convention.** Inlet = `+`, outlet/leak = `−`. Net rate = `1/x − 1/y`. If net is negative, the tank can never fill.
- **Wages follow work done,** i.e. efficiency × days worked — not the agreed time. Compute each person's units, take the ratio, split the money.
- **Leave-midway / replacement:** track units done before the change, subtract from total, then divide the leftover by the new combined rate. Keep man-days constant for M-D-H problems.

> **Final tip:** If you can write the efficiencies as small whole numbers, you've set the problem up correctly. Verify every pair (A+B, B+C, etc.) against the LCM total before answering — a 10-second cross-check prevents the most common silly mistakes in this chapter.

## BOOSTER SET — Extra Hard & PYQ Pattern

**B1.** A can do a work in 16 days and B in 24 days. Working together, in how many days will they finish it?
(a) 9 3/5 days  (b) 10 days  (c) 9 days  (d) 8 days
**Answer: (a) 9 3/5 days**
*Solution:* LCM(16, 24) = 48 = total work. Efficiencies A = 3, B = 2 → combined = 5/day. Days = 48/5 = 9.6 = **9 3/5 days**.
🔑 *Trick:* `ab/(a+b) = (16×24)/40 = 384/40 = 9.6` days.

**B2.** A is 50% more efficient than B. If B alone finishes a work in 27 days, in how many days will A finish it?
(a) 18 days  (b) 20 days  (c) 24 days  (d) 21 days
**Answer: (a) 18 days**
*Solution:* Efficiency A : B = 150 : 100 = 3 : 2 → time A : B = 2 : 3. B = 27 (3 parts) → 1 part = 9 → A = 2 × 9 = **18 days**.

**B3.** A, B and C can do a work in 20, 30 and 60 days respectively. Working together, how long will they take?
(a) 10 days  (b) 12 days  (c) 15 days  (d) 9 days
**Answer: (a) 10 days**
*Solution:* LCM(20, 30, 60) = 60. Efficiencies A = 3, B = 2, C = 1 → combined = 6/day. Days = 60/6 = **10 days**.

**B4.** A can do a work in 20 days, B in 30 days. They work together for 4 days, then A leaves. In how many more days will B finish the remaining work?
(a) 20 days  (b) 18 days  (c) 22 days  (d) 16 days
**Answer: (a) 20 days**
*Solution:* LCM(20, 30) = 60. A = 3, B = 2. In 4 days together = (3+2)×4 = 20 units. Remaining = 60 − 20 = 40 units. B's time = 40/2 = **20 days**.

**B5.** A and B can together finish a work in 18 days, B and C in 24 days, and A and C in 36 days. In how many days can all three together finish it?
(a) 16 days  (b) 18 days  (c) 20 days  (d) 12 days
**Answer: (a) 16 days**
*Solution:* (A+B) = 1/18, (B+C) = 1/24, (A+C) = 1/36. Sum = 2(A+B+C) = 1/18 + 1/24 + 1/36 = (4 + 3 + 2)/72 = 9/72 = 1/8. So A+B+C = 1/16 → **16 days**.

**B6.** A does a work in 8 days and B in 12 days. They work on alternate days, A starting first. In how many days is the work completed?
(a) 9 days  (b) 9½ days  (c) 10 days  (d) 8½ days
**Answer: (b) 9½ days**
*Solution:* LCM(8, 12) = 24. A = 3, B = 2. One pair (A then B) = 5 units / 2 days. After 4 pairs (8 days) = 20 units, remaining 4. Day 9 = A does 3 → 23, remaining 1. Day 10 = B at 2/day needs ½ day. Total = 9 + ½ = **9½ days**.
🔑 *Trick:* Per 2-day cycle they do 5 units; fit whole cycles, then finish unit-by-unit.

**B7.** 8 men can complete a work in 12 days. How many days will 6 men take to do the same work?
(a) 16 days  (b) 18 days  (c) 14 days  (d) 20 days
**Answer: (a) 16 days**
*Solution:* M₁D₁ = M₂D₂ → 8 × 12 = 6 × D → D = 96/6 = **16 days**.
🔑 *Trick:* Total man-days = 96 stays constant; divide by new men.

**B8.** A and B can do a work in 12 days. They worked together for 8 days, then B left and A finished the remaining in 8 more days. In how many days can A alone do the whole work?
(a) 20 days  (b) 24 days  (c) 18 days  (d) 16 days
**Answer: (b) 24 days**
*Solution:* In 8 days together = 8/12 = 2/3 done. Remaining 1/3 done by A in 8 days → A does whole in 8 × 3 = **24 days**.

**B9.** (Pipes) A pipe fills a tank in 15 hours and another in 10 hours. Both opened together — how long to fill?
(a) 6 hours  (b) 5 hours  (c) 7 hours  (d) 8 hours
**Answer: (a) 6 hours**
*Solution:* LCM(15, 10) = 30. Rates +2 and +3 → net 5/hour. Time = 30/5 = **6 hours**.
🔑 *Trick:* `ab/(a+b) = (15×10)/25 = 150/25 = 6`.

**B10.** (Pipes) A cistern is filled by a pipe in 8 hours; a leak empties the full cistern in 12 hours. With both open, how long to fill?
(a) 24 hours  (b) 20 hours  (c) 16 hours  (d) 18 hours
**Answer: (a) 24 hours**
*Solution:* LCM(8, 12) = 24. Inlet +3, leak −2 → net +1/hour. Time = 24/1 = **24 hours**.
🔑 *Trick:* Net rate = 1/8 − 1/12 = (3−2)/24 = 1/24 → 24 hours.

**B11.** (SSC CGL pattern) Two pipes A and B fill a tank in 20 and 30 minutes. Both opened together; after how long should B be closed so the tank fills in 15 minutes total?
(a) 7.5 min  (b) 10 min  (c) 12 min  (d) 8 min
**Answer: (a) 7.5 min**
*Solution:* LCM(20, 30) = 60. A = 3, B = 2. A runs full 15 min = 45 units. B must give 60 − 45 = 15 units at 2/min = **7.5 min**.

**B12.** (SSC CGL pattern) A pipe fills a tank in 10 hours, but due to a leak it takes 12 hours. In how long will the leak alone empty the full tank?
(a) 60 hours  (b) 50 hours  (c) 48 hours  (d) 72 hours
**Answer: (a) 60 hours**
*Solution:* Leak rate = inlet − effective = 1/10 − 1/12 = (6 − 5)/60 = 1/60 → leak empties full tank in **60 hours**.
🔑 *Trick:* Leak time = (fill × actual)/(actual − fill) = (10 × 12)/(12 − 10) = 120/2 = 60.
