# Profit & Loss — Solved Question Bank

> **Top-priority topic** for SSC CGL — 2 to 4 questions appear in nearly every Tier-1 and Tier-2 paper. Master the shortcuts below to solve these in under 30 seconds each.
> Back to [Quant Index](README.md) | [Quant Formula Sheet](../Formula-Sheets/Quant_Formula_Sheet.md)

## Key Formulas

- **Profit %** = `(SP − CP) / CP × 100` and **Loss %** = `(CP − SP) / CP × 100`
- **Selling Price** = `SP = CP × (100 + P%) / 100` (use `100 − L%` for loss)
- **Cost Price** = `CP = SP × 100 / (100 + P%)` (use `100 − L%` for loss)
- Two articles at **same SP**, one at +x% and one at −x% → overall **loss** = `x² / 100 %`
- **Marked Price & Discount**: `MP × (100 − d%) / 100 = SP`
- **Two successive discounts** a% and b% → single equivalent discount = `(a + b − ab/100) %`
- Goods marked **x% above CP** and sold at discount **y%** → profit% = `x − y − xy/100`
- **Dishonest dealer** (false weight): `Gain% = Error / (True − Error) × 100`
- **Buy x get y free**: equivalent discount = `y / (x + y) × 100 %`

## EASY

**Q1.** A man buys an article for ₹500 and sells it for ₹600. Find his profit percent.
(a) 15%  (b) 18%  (c) 20%  (d) 25%
**Answer: (c) 20%**
*Solution:* Profit = 600 − 500 = ₹100. Profit% = 100/500 × 100 = **20%**.

**Q2.** By selling a pen for ₹78, a shopkeeper gains 30%. What is the cost price of the pen?
(a) ₹54  (b) ₹60  (c) ₹62  (d) ₹65
**Answer: (b) ₹60**
*Solution:* CP = SP × 100/(100 + P%) = 78 × 100/130 = **₹60**.
🔑 *Trick:* 130% → 100% means multiply by 100/130 = 10/13, so 78 × 10/13 = 60.

**Q3.** An item costing ₹250 is sold at a loss of 12%. Find its selling price.
(a) ₹210  (b) ₹215  (c) ₹220  (d) ₹225
**Answer: (c) ₹220**
*Solution:* SP = CP × (100 − L%)/100 = 250 × 88/100 = **₹220**.

**Q4.** The marked price of a shirt is ₹800 and a discount of 15% is allowed. Find the selling price.
(a) ₹660  (b) ₹680  (c) ₹700  (d) ₹720
**Answer: (b) ₹680**
*Solution:* SP = MP × (100 − d%)/100 = 800 × 85/100 = **₹680**.

**Q5.** A trader sells goods worth ₹1200 at a profit of 25%. Find the profit amount.
(a) ₹250  (b) ₹275  (c) ₹300  (d) ₹320
**Answer: (c) ₹300**
*Solution:* Profit = 25% of 1200 = 1200 × 25/100 = **₹300**.

**Q6.** A cycle bought for ₹3200 is sold for ₹2880. Find the loss percent.
(a) 8%  (b) 10%  (c) 12%  (d) 15%
**Answer: (b) 10%**
*Solution:* Loss = 3200 − 2880 = ₹320. Loss% = 320/3200 × 100 = **10%**.

## MEDIUM

**Q7.** A man sells two watches for ₹990 each. On one he gains 10% and on the other he loses 10%. Find his overall gain or loss percent.
(a) No loss/gain  (b) 1% loss  (c) 1% gain  (d) 2% loss
**Answer: (b) 1% loss**
*Solution:* When SP is equal and rates are +x% and −x%, there is always a loss = x²/100 = 10²/100 = **1% loss**.
🔑 *Trick:* Same SP, equal +/− percent → straight loss of x²/100%. Never a gain.

**Q8.** A shopkeeper marks his goods 40% above cost price and allows a discount of 25%. Find his profit percent.
(a) 5%  (b) 8%  (c) 10%  (d) 12%
**Answer: (a) 5%**
*Solution:* Profit% = x − y − xy/100 = 40 − 25 − (40 × 25)/100 = 15 − 10 = **5%**.
🔑 *Trick:* Let CP = 100 → MP = 140 → SP = 140 × 0.75 = 105 → profit 5%.

**Q9.** Two successive discounts of 20% and 10% are given on an article marked at ₹500. Find the final selling price.
(a) ₹350  (b) ₹360  (c) ₹370  (d) ₹400
**Answer: (b) ₹360**
*Solution:* SP = 500 × 80/100 × 90/100 = 500 × 0.72 = **₹360**.
🔑 *Trick:* Single equivalent discount = 20 + 10 − (20×10)/100 = 28%, so SP = 500 × 0.72 = 360.

**Q10.** A dishonest dealer professes to sell his goods at cost price but uses a weight of 900 g instead of 1000 g. Find his gain percent.
(a) 10%  (b) 11⅑%  (c) 12%  (d) 9%
**Answer: (b) 11⅑%**
*Solution:* Gain% = Error/(True − Error) × 100 = 100/(1000 − 100) × 100 = 100/900 × 100 = **11⅑% (≈11.11%)**.

**Q11.** A man sold an article at a loss of 8%. Had he sold it for ₹100 more, he would have gained 12%. Find the cost price.
(a) ₹450  (b) ₹500  (c) ₹520  (d) ₹540
**Answer: (b) ₹500**
*Solution:* The ₹100 corresponds to (12 − (−8))% = 20% of CP. So CP = 100 × 100/20 = **₹500**.

**Q12.** The cost price of 20 articles equals the selling price of 16 articles. Find the profit percent.
(a) 20%  (b) 22%  (c) 25%  (d) 30%
**Answer: (c) 25%**
*Solution:* Profit% = (20 − 16)/16 × 100 = 4/16 × 100 = **25%**.
🔑 *Trick:* CP of n = SP of m → profit% = (n − m)/m × 100.

**Q13.** A retailer buys 50 kg of rice. He sells 30 kg at 12% profit and the rest at 6% profit. If he sells the whole at the same rate to make overall profit, what is the overall profit percent?
(a) 9%  (b) 9.6%  (c) 10%  (d) 10.2%
**Answer: (b) 9.6%**
*Solution:* Take CP = ₹1/kg. Total CP = ₹50. Profit = 30 × 0.12 + 20 × 0.06 = 3.6 + 1.2 = ₹4.8. Profit% = 4.8/50 × 100 = **9.6%**.

## HARD

**Q14.** A shopkeeper marks his goods 50% above cost price and gives two successive discounts of 10% and 20%. Find his overall profit or loss percent.
(a) 8% profit  (b) 8% loss  (c) 6% profit  (d) 6% loss
**Answer: (a) 8% profit**
*Solution:* Let CP = 100 → MP = 150. After discounts: 150 × 0.90 × 0.80 = 150 × 0.72 = 108. Profit = 108 − 100 = **8% profit**.

**Q15.** A dishonest dealer uses a false weight of 800 g for a kg and also marks up his goods by 20% over cost price. Find his total gain percent.
(a) 40%  (b) 45%  (c) 50%  (d) 55%
**Answer: (c) 50%**
*Solution:* Selling 800 g as 1000 g at 20% markup. CP of 800 g = ₹800 (at ₹1/g). SP = 1000 × 1.20 = ₹1200. Gain = 1200 − 800 = 400. Gain% = 400/800 × 100 = **50%**.
🔑 *Trick:* Combined factor = (markup factor) × (1000/800) = 1.20 × 1.25 = 1.50 → 50% gain.

**Q16.** A man buys oranges at 6 for ₹10 and sells them at 4 for ₹10. Find his profit percent.
(a) 40%  (b) 45%  (c) 50%  (d) 60%
**Answer: (c) 50%**
*Solution:* CP per orange = 10/6 = ₹1.667. SP per orange = 10/4 = ₹2.5. Profit% = (2.5 − 1.667)/1.667 × 100 = 0.833/1.667 × 100 = **50%**.
🔑 *Trick:* Profit% = (6 − 4)/4 × 100 = 50% (since SP rate is "4 for ₹10").

**Q17.** A shopkeeper allows a discount of 10% on the marked price and still gains 17%. By what percent above the cost price is the marked price set?
(a) 25%  (b) 28%  (c) 30%  (d) 32%
**Answer: (c) 30%**
*Solution:* MP × 0.90 = CP × 1.17 → MP/CP = 1.17/0.90 = 1.30. So MP is **30% above CP**.

**Q18.** By selling 33 m of cloth, a trader gains the selling price of 11 m. Find his gain percent.
(a) 30%  (b) 40%  (c) 50%  (d) 60%
**Answer: (c) 50%**
*Solution:* Gain on 33 m = SP of 11 m → CP of 33 m = SP of (33 − 11) = SP of 22 m. Gain% = (33 − 22)/22 × 100 = 11/22 × 100 = **50%**.

## SSC PYQ PATTERN

**Q19.** (SSC CGL pattern) A person sells an article at 5% profit. Had he bought it 10% cheaper and sold it for ₹42 more, he would have gained 40%. Find the original cost price.
(a) ₹160  (b) ₹180  (c) ₹200  (d) ₹250
**Answer: (c) ₹200**
*Solution:* Let CP = x. Original SP = 1.05x. New CP = 0.9x; selling for ₹42 more gives new SP = 1.05x + 42, which is a 40% gain on the new CP: 1.05x + 42 = 1.40 × 0.9x = 1.26x → 42 = 1.26x − 1.05x = 0.21x → x = 42/0.21 = **₹200**.
🔑 *Trick:* Reduce to one linear equation, then back-verify with CP = 200: original SP = 1.05 × 200 = ₹210; new CP = 180, 40% gain SP = 1.4 × 180 = ₹252, and 252 − 210 = ₹42. ✓

**Q20.** (SSC CGL pattern) A trader marks his goods at 25% above cost price and allows a discount of 12%. Find his profit percent.
(a) 9%  (b) 10%  (c) 11%  (d) 12%
**Answer: (b) 10%**
*Solution:* Profit% = x − y − xy/100 = 25 − 12 − (25 × 12)/100 = 13 − 3 = **10%**.
🔑 *Trick:* CP = 100 → MP = 125 → SP = 125 × 0.88 = 110 → 10% profit.

**Q21.** (SSC CGL pattern) A dishonest shopkeeper sells goods at cost price but uses a weight of 950 g instead of 1 kg. Find his profit percent (to two decimals).
(a) 5.00%  (b) 5.26%  (c) 5.50%  (d) 4.75%
**Answer: (b) 5.26%**
*Solution:* Gain% = Error/(True − Error) × 100 = 50/(1000 − 50) × 100 = 50/950 × 100 = **5.26%**.

**Q22.** (SSC CGL pattern) An article is sold at a profit of 20%. If both the cost price and selling price are increased by ₹100, the profit becomes 15%. Find the original cost price.
(a) ₹300  (b) ₹350  (c) ₹400  (d) ₹450
**Answer: (c) ₹400**
*Solution:* Let CP = x, SP = 1.2x. New: (1.2x + 100) − (x + 100) = 0.2x stays the same profit amount = 0.2x. New profit% = 0.2x/(x + 100) = 0.15 → 0.2x = 0.15x + 15 → 0.05x = 15 → x = **₹400**.

**Q23.** (SSC CGL pattern) A shopkeeper offers "Buy 4 Get 1 Free" on items costing ₹50 each (all sold at marked price = CP). What is the effective discount percent he is giving?
(a) 16%  (b) 18%  (c) 20%  (d) 25%
**Answer: (c) 20%**
*Solution:* Buy x get y free → discount = y/(x + y) × 100 = 1/(4 + 1) × 100 = **20%**.
🔑 *Trick:* The customer pays for 4 but gets 5 → 1 free out of 5 → 20% off.

### 🔁 Successive Change & Markup–Markdown Shortcuts

**A. Two successive percentage changes (net effect)**

When a value changes by `a%` and then by `b%` one after another, the single net change is:

$$\text{Net \%} = a + b + \frac{ab}{100}$$

Plug the changes **with their signs** (increase = +, decrease = −) and the formula handles every case:

| Question says | Use a, b as | Net % shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| **Increase then Increase** | `+a , +b` | `a + b + ab/100` |
| **Increase then Decrease** | `+a , −b` | `a − b − ab/100` |
| **Decrease then Decrease** | `−a , −b` | `−a − b + ab/100` |

> A positive result = net **gain/increase**; a negative result = net **loss/decrease**.
> *Example:* +20% then −10% → `20 − 10 − (20×10)/100 = 10 − 2 = +8%` net increase.

**B. Buy x% BELOW cost, Sell y% ABOVE cost → Profit %**

$$\text{Profit \%} = \frac{x + y}{100 - x} \times 100$$

> *Example:* buy 20% below and sell 30% above ⇒ `(20 + 30)/(100 − 20) × 100 = 50/80 × 100 = 62.5%`.
> *Why:* effective CP = `100 − x`, effective SP = `100 + y`, so profit = `(SP − CP)/CP = (x + y)/(100 − x)`.

**C. Buy x% ABOVE cost, Sell y% BELOW cost**

No clean single fraction — **fix actual BP & SP first**, then apply the standard profit/loss formula:

```
Take base cost = 100
Buy price (BP) = 100 + x          (paid x% extra)
Sell price (SP) = 100 − y         (sold y% under base)
Result % = (SP − BP) / BP × 100   → negative ⇒ loss, positive ⇒ profit
```

> *Example:* buy 25% above, sell 10% below ⇒ BP = 125, SP = 90 ⇒ `(90 − 125)/125 × 100 = −28%` → **28% loss**.

### 🔑 Exam Tricks Box

- **Same SP, ±x% on two items** → always a **loss** of `x²/100 %` (never a gain).
- **Markup x%, discount y%** → net profit `= x − y − xy/100` (one-line, no need for assuming CP).
- **Successive discounts a%, b%** → single discount `= a + b − ab/100`; compute SP as `MP × (1−a/100)(1−b/100)`.
- **False weight** → `Gain% = Error/(True − Error) × 100`; combine with markup by multiplying factors `(1+m)×(True/Weight)`.
- **CP of n = SP of m** → `Profit% = (n − m)/m × 100` (instant for "CP of x articles = SP of y" questions).

## BOOSTER SET — Extra Hard & PYQ Pattern

**B1.** An article marked at ₹1200 is sold after two successive discounts of 25% and 20%. Find the selling price.
(a) ₹660  (b) ₹720  (c) ₹700  (d) ₹680
**Answer: (b) ₹720**
*Solution:* SP = 1200 × 0.75 × 0.80 = 1200 × 0.60 = **₹720**.
🔑 *Trick:* Single equivalent discount = 25 + 20 − (25×20)/100 = 40% → SP = 1200 × 0.60 = 720.

**B2.** A shopkeeper marks his goods 60% above cost price and allows a discount of 25%. Find his profit percent.
(a) 18%  (b) 20%  (c) 22%  (d) 25%
**Answer: (b) 20%**
*Solution:* Profit% = x − y − xy/100 = 60 − 25 − (60×25)/100 = 35 − 15 = **20%**.
🔑 *Trick:* CP = 100 → MP = 160 → SP = 160 × 0.75 = 120 → 20% profit.

**B3.** A dishonest dealer professes to sell at cost price but uses a weight of 750 g for 1 kg. Find his gain percent.
(a) 25%  (b) 30%  (c) 33⅓%  (d) 20%
**Answer: (c) 33⅓%**
*Solution:* Gain% = Error/(True − Error) × 100 = 250/(1000 − 250) × 100 = 250/750 × 100 = **33⅓%**.

**B4.** The cost price of 25 articles equals the selling price of 20 articles. Find the profit percent.
(a) 20%  (b) 25%  (c) 30%  (d) 15%
**Answer: (b) 25%**
*Solution:* Profit% = (n − m)/m × 100 = (25 − 20)/20 × 100 = 5/20 × 100 = **25%**.
🔑 *Trick:* CP of n = SP of m → profit% = (n − m)/m × 100.

**B5.** A man sells two cars for ₹3,60,000 each. On one he gains 20% and on the other he loses 20%. Find his overall loss percent.
(a) 0%  (b) 2%  (c) 4%  (d) 5%
**Answer: (c) 4%**
*Solution:* Same SP, ±x% → loss = x²/100 = 20²/100 = **4% loss**.
🔑 *Trick:* Equal SP with equal gain/loss percent is always a loss of x²/100%.

**B6.** A shopkeeper marks goods 50% above cost and gives two successive discounts of 20% and 10%. Find overall profit/loss percent.
(a) 8% profit  (b) 8% loss  (c) 6% profit  (d) 6% loss
**Answer: (a) 8% profit**
*Solution:* CP = 100 → MP = 150 → SP = 150 × 0.80 × 0.90 = 150 × 0.72 = 108 → **8% profit**.

**B7.** By selling 45 m of cloth a trader gains the selling price of 15 m. Find his gain percent.
(a) 40%  (b) 50%  (c) 33⅓%  (d) 60%
**Answer: (b) 50%**
*Solution:* CP of 45 m = SP of (45 − 15) = SP of 30 m. Gain% = (45 − 30)/30 × 100 = 15/30 × 100 = **50%**.

**B8.** A man buys 12 apples for ₹100 and sells 10 apples for ₹100. Find his profit percent.
(a) 15%  (b) 18%  (c) 20%  (d) 25%
**Answer: (c) 20%**
*Solution:* CP per apple = 100/12; SP per apple = 100/10. Profit% = (12 − 10)/10 × 100 = **20%**.
🔑 *Trick:* Profit% = (buy-count − sell-count)/sell-count × 100 = (12 − 10)/10 × 100 = 20%.

**B9.** A shopkeeper allows a discount of 20% on the marked price and still gains 20%. By what percent is the marked price above the cost price?
(a) 40%  (b) 50%  (c) 45%  (d) 60%
**Answer: (b) 50%**
*Solution:* MP × 0.80 = CP × 1.20 → MP/CP = 1.20/0.80 = 1.50 → MP is **50% above CP**.

**B10.** A dishonest trader uses a false weight of 900 g for a kg and also marks up his goods by 35% over cost. Find his total gain percent.
(a) 40%  (b) 45%  (c) 50%  (d) 55%
**Answer: (c) 50%**
*Solution:* Combined factor = (markup) × (1000/weight) = 1.35 × (1000/900) = 1.35 × 10/9 = 1.50 → **50% gain**.
🔑 *Trick:* Multiply the markup factor by True/Weight; subtract 1 for gain%.

**B11.** (SSC CGL pattern) An article sold at 8% loss; had it been sold for ₹120 more it would have given 12% profit. Find the cost price.
(a) ₹500  (b) ₹540  (c) ₹600  (d) ₹620
**Answer: (c) ₹600**
*Solution:* ₹120 = (12 − (−8))% of CP = 20% of CP → CP = 120 × 100/20 = **₹600**.

**B12.** (SSC CGL pattern) A trader marks his goods at 20% above cost and offers a discount of 10%. If his profit is ₹56, find the cost price.
(a) ₹600  (b) ₹700  (c) ₹750  (d) ₹800
**Answer: (b) ₹700**
*Solution:* Profit% = 20 − 10 − (20×10)/100 = 10 − 2 = 8%. So 8% of CP = ₹56 → CP = 56/0.08 = **₹700**.
🔑 *Trick:* Find profit% by `x − y − xy/100` first, then CP = profit ÷ profit-fraction.
