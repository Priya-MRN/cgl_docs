# Simple & Compound Interest — Solved Question Bank

> Navigation: [Back to README](README.md) | [Quant Formula Sheet](../Formula-Sheets/Quant_Formula_Sheet.md)

## Key Formulas

- Simple Interest: `SI = PRT/100`
- Amount (SI): `A = P + SI = P(1 + RT/100)`
- Compound Interest Amount: `A = P(1 + R/100)^T`
- Compound Interest: `CI = A − P = P[(1 + R/100)^T − 1]`
- Difference for 2 years: `CI − SI = P(R/100)²`
- Difference for 3 years: `CI − SI = PR²(300 + R)/100³`
- Rate at which sum doubles in SI: `R = 100/T` (and time to double = `100/R`)
- Half-yearly compounding: use rate `R/2` and time `2T`
- Quarterly compounding: use rate `R/4` and time `4T`

## EASY

**Q1.** Find the simple interest on ₹5000 at 8% per annum for 3 years.
(a) ₹1000  (b) ₹1200  (c) ₹1500  (d) ₹1800
**Answer: (b) ₹1200**
*Solution:* SI = PRT/100 = 5000 × 8 × 3 / 100 = 120000/100 = ₹1200.

🔑 *Trick:* For whole-number rates, compute P×R×T first, then drop two zeros.

**Q2.** At what rate percent per annum will ₹4000 yield ₹720 as simple interest in 2 years?
(a) 8%  (b) 9%  (c) 10%  (d) 12%
**Answer: (b) 9%**
*Solution:* R = 100 × SI / (P × T) = 100 × 720 / (4000 × 2) = 72000/8000 = 9%.

**Q3.** In what time will ₹2500 amount to ₹3000 at 5% simple interest per annum?
(a) 3 years  (b) 4 years  (c) 5 years  (d) 6 years
**Answer: (b) 4 years**
*Solution:* SI = 3000 − 2500 = ₹500. T = 100 × SI / (P × R) = 100 × 500 / (2500 × 5) = 50000/12500 = 4 years.

**Q4.** Find the principal that gives ₹360 simple interest in 3 years at 6% per annum.
(a) ₹1800  (b) ₹2000  (c) ₹2200  (d) ₹2400
**Answer: (b) ₹2000**
*Solution:* P = 100 × SI / (R × T) = 100 × 360 / (6 × 3) = 36000/18 = ₹2000.

**Q5.** Find the compound interest on ₹8000 at 10% per annum for 2 years (compounded annually).
(a) ₹1600  (b) ₹1680  (c) ₹1700  (d) ₹1760
**Answer: (b) ₹1680**
*Solution:* A = 8000(1 + 10/100)² = 8000 × (11/10)² = 8000 × 121/100 = ₹9680. CI = 9680 − 8000 = ₹1680.

**Q6.** The simple interest on a sum for 1 year at 12% per annum is ₹600. Find the sum.
(a) ₹4000  (b) ₹4500  (c) ₹5000  (d) ₹5500
**Answer: (c) ₹5000**
*Solution:* P = 100 × SI / (R × T) = 100 × 600 / (12 × 1) = 60000/12 = ₹5000.

## MEDIUM

**Q7.** Find the compound interest on ₹15625 at 4% per annum for 3 years (compounded annually).
(a) ₹1851  (b) ₹1951  (c) ₹1901  (d) ₹2000
**Answer: (b) ₹1951**
*Solution:* A = 15625 × (26/25)³ = 15625 × 17576/15625 = ₹17576. CI = 17576 − 15625 = ₹1951.

🔑 *Trick:* When P is a perfect power matching the denominator (25³ = 15625), the fraction cancels cleanly.

**Q8.** The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 2 years at 10% per annum is ₹50. Find the sum.
(a) ₹4000  (b) ₹4500  (c) ₹5000  (d) ₹5500
**Answer: (c) ₹5000**
*Solution:* CI − SI = P(R/100)² = P(10/100)² = P/100. So P/100 = 50 ⇒ P = ₹5000.

🔑 *Trick:* For 2 years at 10%, difference = P/100 directly.

**Q9.** The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 3 years at 10% per annum is ₹93. Find the sum.
(a) ₹2500  (b) ₹3000  (c) ₹3500  (d) ₹4000
**Answer: (b) ₹3000**
*Solution:* CI − SI = PR²(300 + R)/100³ = P × 100 × 310 / 1000000 = 31P/1000. So 31P/1000 = 93 ⇒ P = 93 × 1000/31 = ₹3000.

**Q10.** Find the amount on ₹16000 at 20% per annum for 1 year, compounded half-yearly.
(a) ₹17600  (b) ₹19000  (c) ₹19360  (d) ₹19500
**Answer: (c) ₹19360**
*Solution:* Half-yearly: rate = 20/2 = 10%, time = 1 × 2 = 2 periods. A = 16000(1 + 10/100)² = 16000 × 121/100 = ₹19360.

🔑 *Trick:* Half-yearly means halve the rate and double the periods.

**Q11.** A sum becomes ₹6655 in 3 years at 10% per annum compound interest. Find the sum.
(a) ₹4500  (b) ₹5000  (c) ₹5500  (d) ₹6000
**Answer: (b) ₹5000**
*Solution:* P = A / (1 + R/100)³ = 6655 / (11/10)³ = 6655 × 1000/1331 = ₹5000.

**Q12.** A sum of money doubles itself in 8 years at simple interest. In how many years will it triple?
(a) 12 years  (b) 14 years  (c) 16 years  (d) 18 years
**Answer: (c) 16 years**
*Solution:* Doubling means SI = P in 8 years, so rate gives P in 8 years. Tripling needs SI = 2P, which takes 2 × 8 = 16 years.

🔑 *Trick:* In SI, time to become n times = (n−1) × (time to become 2 times).

**Q13.** At what rate of simple interest will a sum double itself in 5 years?
(a) 15%  (b) 18%  (c) 20%  (d) 25%
**Answer: (c) 20%**
*Solution:* For doubling, R = 100/T = 100/5 = 20%.

## HARD

**Q14.** The compound interest on a sum for 2 years is ₹820 and the simple interest is ₹800 at the same rate. Find the rate of interest.
(a) 4%  (b) 5%  (c) 6%  (d) 8%
**Answer: (b) 5%**
*Solution:* SI per year = 800/2 = ₹400. Difference = CI − SI = 820 − 800 = ₹20, which is the interest on the first year's interest (₹400) for 1 year. So R = 100 × 20 / (400 × 1) = 5%.

🔑 *Trick:* The CI−SI gap over 2 years equals interest on one year's SI.

**Q15.** A sum is divided into two parts such that the simple interest on the first at 6% for 3 years equals the interest on the second at 9% for 2 years. If the total sum is ₹3000, find the first part.
(a) ₹1000  (b) ₹1200  (c) ₹1500  (d) ₹1800
**Answer: (c) ₹1500**
*Solution:* Let the parts be x and 3000 − x. Then x × 6 × 3 / 100 = (3000 − x) × 9 × 2 / 100 ⇒ 18x = 18(3000 − x) ⇒ 18x = 54000 − 18x ⇒ 36x = 54000 ⇒ x = ₹1500.

**Q16.** A sum of ₹12600 is to be paid in two equal annual installments at 10% per annum compound interest. Find each installment.
(a) ₹7200  (b) ₹7260  (c) ₹7500  (d) ₹7700
**Answer: (b) ₹7260**
*Solution:* Let each installment = X. The present value of the two future installments equals the loan: P = X/(1+R/100) + X/(1+R/100)². So 12600 = X(10/11) + X(100/121) = X(110 + 100)/121 = 210X/121. Thus X = 12600 × 121/210 = 60 × 121 = ₹7260.

🔑 *Trick:* For installments, discount each future payment back by dividing by (1+R/100)ⁿ, then equate the total to the loan.

**Q17.** The difference between compound interest compounded annually and half-yearly on ₹10000 for 1 year at 20% per annum is:
(a) ₹50  (b) ₹100  (c) ₹150  (d) ₹200
**Answer: (b) ₹100**
*Solution:* Annual: A = 10000 × 120/100 = ₹12000, CI = ₹2000. Half-yearly: rate 10%, 2 periods, A = 10000 × (11/10)² = ₹12100, CI = ₹2100. Difference = 2100 − 2000 = ₹100.

**Q18.** A certain sum amounts to ₹9680 in 2 years and ₹10648 in 3 years at compound interest. Find the rate of interest.
(a) 8%  (b) 10%  (c) 12%  (d) 15%
**Answer: (b) 10%**
*Solution:* The amount in the 3rd year grows from the 2nd year amount: ratio = 10648/9680 = 1.1 = (1 + R/100). So R = 10%.

🔑 *Trick:* Rate = (A₃ − A₂)/A₂ × 100 for consecutive yearly CI amounts.

## SSC PYQ PATTERN

**Q19.** A sum of ₹6000 is lent at 5% per annum simple interest. Find the total amount after 4 years. (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) ₹6800  (b) ₹7000  (c) ₹7200  (d) ₹7500
**Answer: (c) ₹7200**
*Solution:* SI = 6000 × 5 × 4 / 100 = ₹1200. Amount = 6000 + 1200 = ₹7200.

**Q20.** The compound interest on ₹25000 at 8% per annum for 2 years is: (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) ₹4000  (b) ₹4160  (c) ₹4200  (d) ₹4320
**Answer: (b) ₹4160**
*Solution:* A = 25000(1 + 8/100)² = 25000 × (27/25)² = 25000 × 729/625 = ₹29160. CI = 29160 − 25000 = ₹4160.

**Q21.** The difference between CI and SI on ₹12000 for 2 years at 5% per annum is: (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) ₹25  (b) ₹30  (c) ₹35  (d) ₹40
**Answer: (b) ₹30**
*Solution:* CI − SI = P(R/100)² = 12000 × (5/100)² = 12000 × 25/10000 = 12000/400 = ₹30.

**Q22.** At what rate of simple interest will ₹8000 amount to ₹10400 in 3 years? (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) 8%  (b) 9%  (c) 10%  (d) 12%
**Answer: (c) 10%**
*Solution:* SI = 10400 − 8000 = ₹2400. R = 100 × 2400 / (8000 × 3) = 240000/24000 = 10%.

**Q23.** A sum of money trebles itself in 12 years at simple interest. The rate of interest per annum is: (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) 12.5%  (b) 15%  (c) 16⅔%  (d) 20%
**Answer: (c) 16⅔%**
*Solution:* Trebling means SI = 2P in 12 years. R = 100 × 2P / (P × 12) = 200/12 = 16⅔%.

🔑 *Trick:* To become n times in SI: R = 100(n−1)/T.

### 🔑 Exam Tricks Box

- For 2 years, the CI−SI difference is simply `P(R/100)²` — memorise it to skip full CI calculation.
- For 3 years, use `PR²(300+R)/100³`; at 10% this reduces to `31P/1000`.
- In simple interest, time to become n times the sum = `(n−1) × 100/R`; doubling rate = `100/T`.
- For half-yearly compounding, always convert to rate `R/2` and `2T` periods before applying the CI formula.
- For consecutive-year CI amounts, the rate equals the percentage growth from one year's amount to the next.

> Master the CI−SI shortcut formulas first — they convert lengthy SSC problems into single-line calculations and save crucial seconds in the exam.
