# Ratio, Proportion & Partnership — Solved Question Bank

> Navigation: [Back to README](README.md) | [Quant Formula Sheet](../Formula-Sheets/Quant_Formula_Sheet.md)

## Key Formulas

- Proportion: `a:b = c:d ⇒ ad = bc` (product of extremes = product of means)
- Mean proportional between a and b: `√(ab)`
- Third proportional to a and b: `b²/a`
- Fourth proportional to a, b, c: `bc/a`
- Combining ratios `a:b` and `b:c` → `a:b:c` (make b common)
- Componendo & Dividendo: if `a/b = c/d` then `(a+b)/(a−b) = (c+d)/(c−d)`
- Partnership: profit share `∝ capital × time` (equivalent capital)
- Working partner: deduct salary/commission first, then divide remaining profit by capital ratio

## EASY

**Q1.** Simplify the ratio 18 : 24.
(a) 2 : 3  (b) 3 : 4  (c) 4 : 5  (d) 5 : 6
**Answer: (b) 3 : 4**
*Solution:* Divide both terms by their HCF 6: 18/6 : 24/6 = 3 : 4.

**Q2.** Divide ₹600 between A and B in the ratio 2 : 3. Find B's share.
(a) ₹240  (b) ₹300  (c) ₹360  (d) ₹400
**Answer: (c) ₹360**
*Solution:* Total parts = 2 + 3 = 5. B's share = 600 × 3/5 = ₹360.

🔑 *Trick:* Each part = total ÷ sum of ratio terms = 600/5 = ₹120; B gets 3 parts.

**Q3.** Find the mean proportional between 4 and 9.
(a) 5  (b) 6  (c) 7  (d) 8
**Answer: (b) 6**
*Solution:* Mean proportional = √(ab) = √(4 × 9) = √36 = 6.

**Q4.** Find the third proportional to 4 and 8.
(a) 12  (b) 14  (c) 16  (d) 18
**Answer: (c) 16**
*Solution:* Third proportional = b²/a = 8²/4 = 64/4 = 16.

**Q5.** Find the fourth proportional to 3, 6 and 5.
(a) 8  (b) 9  (c) 10  (d) 12
**Answer: (c) 10**
*Solution:* Fourth proportional = bc/a = 6 × 5 / 3 = 30/3 = 10.

**Q6.** Two numbers are in the ratio 5 : 7 and their sum is 96. Find the larger number.
(a) 40  (b) 48  (c) 54  (d) 56
**Answer: (d) 56**
*Solution:* Total parts = 5 + 7 = 12. Each part = 96/12 = 8. Larger number = 7 × 8 = 56.

## MEDIUM

**Q7.** If A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5, find A : B : C.
(a) 8 : 12 : 15  (b) 2 : 3 : 5  (c) 6 : 9 : 10  (d) 8 : 12 : 10
**Answer: (a) 8 : 12 : 15**
*Solution:* Make B common. A:B = 2:3 = 8:12 (×4); B:C = 4:5 = 12:15 (×3). So A:B:C = 8 : 12 : 15.

🔑 *Trick:* Multiply each ratio so the common term (B) matches; here B becomes 12 in both.

**Q8.** The ages of P and Q are in the ratio 4 : 5. After 6 years the ratio becomes 6 : 7. Find P's present age.
(a) 10 years  (b) 12 years  (c) 14 years  (d) 16 years
**Answer: (b) 12 years**
*Solution:* Let ages be 4x and 5x. (4x+6)/(5x+6) = 6/7 ⇒ 7(4x+6) = 6(5x+6) ⇒ 28x + 42 = 30x + 36 ⇒ 2x = 6 ⇒ x = 3. P's age = 4 × 3 = 12 years.

**Q9.** A mixture contains milk and water in the ratio 5 : 2. If 14 litres of water is added, the ratio becomes 5 : 4. Find the initial quantity of milk.
(a) 30 L  (b) 35 L  (c) 40 L  (d) 45 L
**Answer: (b) 35 L**
*Solution:* Let milk = 5x, water = 2x. 5x/(2x+14) = 5/4 ⇒ 4 × 5x = 5(2x+14) ⇒ 20x = 10x + 70 ⇒ 10x = 70 ⇒ x = 7. Milk = 5 × 7 = 35 L.

🔑 *Trick:* Milk is unchanged, so anchor the equation on milk's fixed value while water grows.

**Q10.** If a/b = 5/3, find the value of (a + b)/(a − b) using componendo and dividendo.
(a) 2  (b) 3  (c) 4  (d) 5
**Answer: (c) 4**
*Solution:* By componendo and dividendo, (a+b)/(a−b) = (5+3)/(5−3) = 8/2 = 4.

🔑 *Trick:* When a ratio is given, jump straight to (sum)/(difference) of the numerator and denominator.

**Q11.** A and B start a business with capitals ₹4000 and ₹6000. Find A's share in a total profit of ₹2500.
(a) ₹900  (b) ₹1000  (c) ₹1200  (d) ₹1500
**Answer: (b) ₹1000**
*Solution:* Capital ratio = 4000 : 6000 = 2 : 3. A's share = 2500 × 2/5 = ₹1000.

**Q12.** Divide ₹1080 among A, B, C in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. Find C's share.
(a) ₹360  (b) ₹420  (c) ₹480  (d) ₹540
**Answer: (c) ₹480**
*Solution:* Total parts = 2 + 3 + 4 = 9. Each part = 1080/9 = 120. C's share = 4 × 120 = ₹480.

**Q13.** The incomes of A and B are in the ratio 3 : 4 and their expenditures in the ratio 2 : 3. If each saves ₹2000, find A's income.
(a) ₹5000  (b) ₹6000  (c) ₹7000  (d) ₹8000
**Answer: (b) ₹6000**
*Solution:* Let incomes = 3x, 4x. Savings = income − expenditure. (3x − 2000)/(4x − 2000) = 2/3 ⇒ 3(3x−2000) = 2(4x−2000) ⇒ 9x − 6000 = 8x − 4000 ⇒ x = 2000. A's income = 3 × 2000 = ₹6000.

🔑 *Trick:* Use expenditure = income − savings, then set the expenditure ratio.

## HARD

**Q14.** A starts a business with ₹9000. After 4 months B joins with ₹12000. At the end of the year, find the ratio in which the profit is divided.
(a) 1 : 1  (b) 9 : 8  (c) 3 : 2  (d) 27 : 16
**Answer: (d) 27 : 16**
*Solution:* A's equivalent capital = 9000 × 12 = 108000. B's = 12000 × 8 = 96000. Ratio = 108000 : 96000 = 108 : 96 = 27 : 16.

**Q15.** Three partners A, B, C invest ₹2000, ₹3000 and ₹4000 for 6, 4 and 6 months respectively. Find the ratio of their profits.
(a) 1 : 1 : 1  (b) 1 : 1 : 2  (c) 2 : 2 : 3  (d) 3 : 2 : 4
**Answer: (b) 1 : 1 : 2**
*Solution:* A = 2000 × 6 = 12000; B = 3000 × 4 = 12000; C = 4000 × 6 = 24000. Ratio = 12000 : 12000 : 24000 = 1 : 1 : 2.

🔑 *Trick:* Always multiply capital × time (months) to get the equivalent capital before forming the ratio.

**Q16.** In a partnership, A is a working partner and B is a sleeping partner. A invests ₹5000 and B invests ₹6000. A receives 10% of the profit for managing, and the rest is divided in the capital ratio. If the total profit is ₹2200, find A's total share.
(a) ₹1100  (b) ₹1120  (c) ₹1220  (d) ₹1320
**Answer: (b) ₹1120**
*Solution:* A's management commission = 10% of 2200 = ₹220. Remaining = ₹1980, divided in capital ratio 5000:6000 = 5:6. A's part of the remaining = 1980 × 5/11 = ₹900. A's total = 220 + 900 = ₹1120.

**Q17.** Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 5. If 9 is subtracted from each, the ratio becomes 12 : 23. Find the larger number.
(a) 33  (b) 45  (c) 55  (d) 65
**Answer: (c) 55**
*Solution:* Let numbers be 3x and 5x. (3x−9)/(5x−9) = 12/23 ⇒ 23(3x−9) = 12(5x−9) ⇒ 69x − 207 = 60x − 108 ⇒ 9x = 99 ⇒ x = 11. Larger = 5 × 11 = 55.

**Q18.** ₹2400 is divided among A, B, C so that A : B = 3 : 4 and B : C = 4 : 5. Find C's share.
(a) ₹600  (b) ₹800  (c) ₹1000  (d) ₹1200
**Answer: (c) ₹1000**
*Solution:* B is already 4 in both ratios, so A:B:C = 3:4:5 directly. Total parts = 3 + 4 + 5 = 12. Each part = 2400/12 = 200. C's share = 5 × 200 = ₹1000.

🔑 *Trick:* When chaining ratios, scale on the shared term (B) so it matches in both; here it is already 4, so the combined ratio is immediate.

## SSC PYQ PATTERN

**Q19.** Divide ₹3500 between two persons in the ratio 3 : 4. Find the smaller share. (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) ₹1200  (b) ₹1500  (c) ₹1800  (d) ₹2000
**Answer: (b) ₹1500**
*Solution:* Total parts = 3 + 4 = 7. Each part = 3500/7 = 500. Smaller share = 3 × 500 = ₹1500.

**Q20.** Find the fourth proportional to 8, 12 and 10. (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) 12  (b) 15  (c) 18  (d) 20
**Answer: (b) 15**
*Solution:* Fourth proportional = bc/a = 12 × 10 / 8 = 120/8 = 15.

**Q21.** A, B and C invested ₹6000, ₹8000 and ₹10000 in a business. If the total profit is ₹4800, find B's share. (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) ₹1200  (b) ₹1600  (c) ₹1800  (d) ₹2000
**Answer: (b) ₹1600**
*Solution:* Capital ratio = 6000 : 8000 : 10000 = 3 : 4 : 5, total parts = 12. B's share = 4800 × 4/12 = ₹1600.

**Q22.** The ratio of two numbers is 7 : 9 and their difference is 8. Find the smaller number. (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) 28  (b) 32  (c) 36  (d) 40
**Answer: (a) 28**
*Solution:* Difference of parts = 9 − 7 = 2 parts = 8 ⇒ 1 part = 4. Smaller number = 7 × 4 = 28.

**Q23.** A and B enter a partnership. A invests ₹16000 for 8 months and B invests ₹12000 for the whole year. Find the ratio of their profits. (SSC CGL pattern)
(a) 4 : 3  (b) 8 : 9  (c) 3 : 4  (d) 9 : 8
**Answer: (b) 8 : 9**
*Solution:* A = 16000 × 8 = 128000; B = 12000 × 12 = 144000. Ratio = 128000 : 144000 = 8 : 9.

🔑 *Trick:* Convert every investment to capital × months, then cancel common zeros to simplify.

### 🔑 Exam Tricks Box

- To divide an amount in a ratio, find "one part" = total ÷ sum of ratio terms, then multiply by each share's number.
- Mean proportional = `√(ab)`, third proportional = `b²/a`, fourth proportional = `bc/a` — memorise these three one-liners.
- To combine `a:b` and `b:c`, scale both ratios so the shared term B becomes the same (its LCM) value.
- In partnership, always use equivalent capital = capital × time before forming the profit ratio; equal money-time means equal share.
- For a working partner, deduct salary/commission from total profit first, then split the remainder in the capital ratio.

> Treat every ratio problem as "parts": convert the data into number of equal parts, value one part, and the rest follows instantly — this single habit cracks most SSC ratio and partnership questions.
